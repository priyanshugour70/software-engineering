## 15 – Job Market for Product Strategy & Roadmapping

This file gives a **beginner‑friendly view** of the job market for roles heavily involved in Product Strategy & Roadmap work.

It is not a salary survey but focuses on:

- types of roles,
- typical career paths,
- where this expertise is in demand.

---

### 1. Roles with Strong Strategy & Roadmap Focus

Examples:

- Product Manager (mid‑senior),
- Senior / Lead Product Manager,
- Group / Principal Product Manager,
- Head of Product / Director of Product,
- Portfolio Manager (sometimes in PMO),
- Strategy Lead (product or business),
- some specialized **Technical Product Managers**.

These roles:

- spend a large portion of their time on:
  - defining strategy,
  - shaping roadmaps,
  - aligning stakeholders.

---

### 2. Industries and Domains

High demand for these skills appears in:

- **Technology & SaaS**:
  - B2B platforms, B2C apps, infrastructure products.
- **Financial Services & Banking**:
  - lending, payments, digital banking, wealth, insurance.
- **Telecoms & Utilities**:
  - large customer bases, regulatory and infrastructure constraints.
- **Enterprise Software**:
  - CRM, ERP, HR tech, analytics platforms.

In enterprises/banks:

- strategy and roadmap skills are increasingly required for:
  - modernizing legacy platforms,
  - building digital journeys,
  - partnering with fintechs.

---

### 3. Career Stages

#### 3.1 Early Career (Associate / Junior PM)

- main focus:
  - execution and feature delivery,
  - learning:
    - discovery,
    - metrics,
    - stakeholder communication.
- exposure to strategy:
  - mostly via:
    - supporting roadmap work,
    - participating in planning sessions.

#### 3.2 Mid Career (PM / Senior PM)

- increased:
  - ownership of product areas,
  - input into strategy and roadmaps.
- expected to:
  - drive problem/solution/outcome framing,
  - propose roadmaps aligned with themes and goals.

#### 3.3 Senior / Leadership (Lead PM, Principal, Head of Product)

- responsible for:
  - cross‑team or domain‑level strategies,
  - multi‑year roadmaps,
  - portfolio trade‑offs.
- work closely with:
  - business, finance, risk, and technology leadership.

---

### 4. Signals Employers Look For

Typical signals on CV and in interviews:

- evidence of:
  - having shaped **strategy**, not just executed tasks.
- stories of:
  - making trade‑offs between initiatives,
  - saying “no” or “not now” with rationale.
- comfort with:
  - metrics,
  - basic financial arguments,
  - capacity/roadmap constraints.
- ability to:
  - communicate strategy to different audiences.

Portfolio examples:

- slides or write‑ups of:
  - a product vision,
  - themes and roadmap you created,
  - problem/solution/outcome framing,
  - retrospective on what worked and what changed.

---

### 5. Demand in Enterprises/Banks

Drivers:

- digital transformation (legacy → digital platforms),
- regulatory and risk complexity,
- competition from fintechs and big tech.

Banks increasingly need:

- product leaders who can:
  - bridge:
    - business,
    - risk,
    - technology,
  - craft:
    - realistic strategies,
    - integrated roadmaps,
    - measurable outcomes.

Roles might be called:

- “Digital Product Owner”,
- “Product Lead – Digital Lending”,
- “Head of Digital Channels”,
- “Portfolio Manager – Retail Banking”.

---

### 6. Building Experience if You Are New

If you are early in your career:

- ask to:
  - support your team’s roadmap processes,
  - help maintain initiative/epic catalogues,
  - document decision logs.
- volunteer to:
  - run small planning workshops,
  - create clear slides or docs summarizing:
    - themes,
    - outcomes,
    - roadmaps.

Even without the “strategy” title:

- these experiences show:
  - initiative,
  - systems thinking,
  - ability to handle ambiguity.

---

### 7. Beginner Checklist

- [ ] Do you know:
  - [ ] which roles in your organization are seen as “strategy heavy”?
  - [ ] what expectations they have around roadmapping and outcomes?
- [ ] Can you:
  - [ ] point to at least one example where you shaped a roadmap or prioritization decision?
  - [ ] describe how you handled trade‑offs and constraints?

If not:

- use this module as a guide to:
  - find opportunities to participate in strategy and roadmap work,
  - even if your title is currently junior or more execution‑focused.
