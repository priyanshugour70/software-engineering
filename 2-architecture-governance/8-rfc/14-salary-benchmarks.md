## 14 – Salary Benchmarks & Compensation (Conceptual)

This file provides a **conceptual view** of how RFC‑related responsibilities influence compensation, without listing specific numbers.

---

### 1. Why RFC Responsibility Affects Compensation

Driving RFCs usually means:

- handling:
  - higher‑impact decisions,
  - cross‑team dependencies,
  - significant risk and cost implications.
- influencing:
  - architectural direction,
  - technology strategy,
  - platform evolution.

These are:

- core expectations at:
  - senior,
  - staff/principal,
  - and architect levels,
- which are generally associated with higher pay bands.

---

### 2. Compensation Components

Common components:

- **Base salary** – fixed yearly compensation.
- **Bonus / Variable pay** – often larger at higher levels; tied to:
  - company,
  - business unit,
  - and individual performance.
- **Stock / Long‑term incentives** – more prevalent in:
  - tech companies,
  - startups/scaleups,
  - some large public enterprises.
- **Benefits** – health, pension, training, allowances, etc.

---

### 3. Enterprise/Bank vs Startup/Scaleup

In enterprises/banks:

- salary bands are:
  - more structured,
  - tied to job grades,
  - aligned with:
    - scope of systems,
    - regulatory exposure,
    - leadership responsibilities.

In startups/scaleups:

- titles and compensation:
  - vary widely,
  - equity may be more important,
  - people may play multiple roles (product + architecture + implementation).

RFC skills:

- are valued in both contexts, but:
  - show up differently in job descriptions and pay structures.

---

### 4. Factors That Typically Increase Pay for RFC‑Heavy Roles

- **Scope & Complexity**
  - number of systems, teams, and domains an RFC owner is responsible for.
- **Risk & Regulatory Impact**
  - decisions that materially affect:
    - security posture,
    - regulatory compliance,
    - operational risk.
- **Strategic Importance**
  - platform or architecture decisions that:
    - define future capabilities,
    - impact many products.
- **Leadership & Influence**
  - ability to:
    - drive consensus,
    - coach others,
    - set patterns and standards.

---

### 5. Using Benchmarks Responsibly

For numeric benchmarks, people typically use:

- public salary surveys and databases,
- job postings with ranges,
- internal HR band information.

Always consider:

- geography and cost of living,
- industry and company size,
- specific role mix (architecture vs hands‑on coding vs management).
