## 20 – Best Practices for Market Opportunity Discovery

This file summarizes **practical best practices** that make discovery effective and sustainable.  
It is written as a **checklist plus explanations**, suitable for beginners and teams formalizing their process.

We group best practices into:

- A. Mindset
- B. Process and cadence
- C. Collaboration and communication
- D. Documentation and traceability

---

### A. Mindset Best Practices

#### 1. Treat Opportunities as Hypotheses, Not Facts

- Write them as:
  - “We believe that…”
- Explicitly state:
  - assumptions,
  - unknowns.

Why:

- Keeps teams open to learning and changes.
- Reduces ego attachment to a single idea.

---

#### 2. Stay Longer in the Problem (Opportunity) Space

- Ask:
  - “Who is this for?”
  - “What is their situation?”
  - “What is broken today?”
- Delay jumping to:
  - “We should build feature X.”

Why:

- Leads to **more robust and flexible** solutions later.

---

#### 3. Combine Data and Stories

- Use both:
  - **quantitative metrics** (how big, how often),
  - **qualitative stories** (why it matters, what it feels like).

Why:

- Data without stories can be **cold and hard to act on**.
- Stories without data can be **misleading or anecdotal**.

---

### B. Process and Cadence Best Practices

#### 4. Timebox Discovery Cycles

- Run discovery in **clear time windows**:
  - e.g., 4–8 weeks per cycle.
- Avoid:
  - endless research with no decisions.

Why:

- Forces **prioritization**.
- Builds a **habit of regular reflection** and decision‑making.

---

#### 5. Maintain a Living Opportunity Backlog

- Keep a **central list** of:
  - all identified opportunities,
  - their stage,
  - their owner,
  - their priority.

Why:

- Prevents forgetting ideas.
- Provides a **portfolio view** for leaders and teams.

---

#### 6. Tie Discovery to Strategy and OKRs

- For each top opportunity, answer:
  - “Which strategic objective does this support?”
  - “Which metrics might it move?”

Why:

- Aligns discovery with **executive priorities**.
- Makes it easier to **secure resources**.

---

#### 7. Re‑Evaluate Periodically

- Revisit:
  - existing opportunities,
  - their scores and assumptions,
  - whether the environment has changed (competition, regulation, internal priorities).

Why:

- Keeps the portfolio **current and relevant**.
- Avoids pursuing outdated opportunities.

---

### C. Collaboration and Communication Best Practices

#### 8. Involve Multiple Functions Early

- Include:
  - product,
  - business/strategy,
  - UX/research,
  - data,
  - engineering,
  - risk/compliance (where relevant),
  - operations/support.

Why:

- Surfaces:
  - constraints,
  - knowledge,
  - alternative perspectives,
  early in the process.

---

#### 9. Run Structured Workshops

- Use:
  - clear agendas,
  - frameworks (e.g., clustering, 2×2s, OST),
  - timeboxes for discussion.

Why:

- Avoids unfocused brainstorming.
- Produces **concrete outputs** in a short time.

---

#### 10. Communicate in Plain Language

- Write briefs and readouts for:
  - non‑experts,
  - busy executives.
- Avoid jargon where possible.

Why:

- Increases **alignment and understanding**.
- Helps stakeholders **engage with the content**, not the terminology.

---

### D. Documentation and Traceability Best Practices

#### 11. Standardize Opportunity Briefs

- Use a **consistent template** across teams (see `10-documents.md` and `23-templates.md`).
- Require:
  - target segment,
  - problem description,
  - evidence,
  - link to strategy,
  - risks,
  - next steps.

Why:

- Makes it easier to **compare** and **evaluate** opportunities.
- Reduces time wasted on re‑explaining context.

---

#### 12. Capture Evaluation Rationale

- For each opportunity, record:
  - scores per criterion,
  - key arguments for and against,
  - final decision and who made it.

Why:

- Creates a **transparent history**.
- Helps future teams understand **why decisions were made**.

---

#### 13. Link to Sources

- In briefs and readouts, link to:
  - analytics dashboards,
  - interview summaries,
  - reports,
  - regulatory documents.

Why:

- Allows others to **verify and deepen** understanding.
- Encourages **evidence‑based conversation**.

---

### E. Personal Habits for Practitioners

For individuals (especially PMs and strategists), useful habits include:

- **Curiosity**
  - Regularly talk to:
    - customers,
    - frontline staff,
    - other teams.
- **Note‑taking**
  - Maintain a personal “opportunity notebook” of:
    - patterns you see,
    - questions you have,
    - quick ideas (later fed into the backlog).
- **Reflection**
  - After each discovery cycle, ask:
    - “What did we learn?”
    - “What worked well in our process?”
    - “What should we change next time?”
