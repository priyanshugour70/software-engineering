## 05 – Strategic Themes, Goals & OKRs

This file explains how to translate **vision and product strategy** into:

- a small set of **strategic themes**, and
- clear **goals and OKRs** (or similar outcome metrics).

It is a bridge from `04-vision-and-product-strategy.md` to **roadmap and execution**.

---

### 1. What Are Strategic Themes?

**Strategic themes** (also called pillars) are:

- 3–7 **big areas of focus** that:
  - connect strategy to actual work,
  - help allocate capacity,
  - guide trade‑offs when conflicts arise.

Examples for digital SME lending:

- “Frictionless Digital Experience”
- “Configurable Risk & Compliance Engine”
- “Operational Excellence & Automation”
- “Data & Insights for SMEs”

Themes should:

- be directly traceable to the **diagnosis and vision**,
- be stable for at least **1–2 years** (though they can evolve),
- have **owners** and **metrics**.

---

### 2. From Themes to Goals

For each theme, define:

- 1–3 **objectives** (qualitative),
- with **key results** or equivalent metrics (quantitative).

#### 2.1 Objectives

An **objective** is a:

- short, qualitative statement that:
  - describes a **directional improvement**,
  - is ambitious but realistic within a horizon (e.g., 12–18 months).

Example (Theme: Frictionless Digital Experience):

- Objective:
  - “Make it effortless for SMEs to apply for and manage loans digitally.”

#### 2.2 Key Results (OKRs) or Outcome Metrics

**Key Results (KRs)**:

- describe **how you know** you are achieving the objective,
- should be **measurable** and **time‑bounded**.

Example KRs:

- “Digital share of SME loan applications from 30% → 70% within 12 months.”
- “End‑to‑end digital application completion time median from 10 days → 2 days.”
- “NPS for digital journey from +10 → +35.”

You do not need formal OKRs everywhere, but you **do** need:

- some way to measure success at the **theme** level.

---

### 3. Top‑Down and Bottom‑Up Goal Setting

In enterprises, goals often come from:

- **Top‑down**:
  - company and segment OKRs,
  - revenue, risk, and cost targets,
  - regulatory and strategic commitments.
- **Bottom‑up**:
  - discovery work,
  - feasibility and capacity constraints,
  - realistic delivery estimates.

Good product strategy & roadmap work:

- **connects** these:
  - top‑down ambitions,
  - bottom‑up reality,
- and negotiates a **feasible middle**.

---

### 4. Example – Themed Goals for Digital SME Lending

#### Theme 1: Frictionless Digital Experience

- Objective:
  - “Enable SMEs to apply for and manage loans quickly and transparently, mostly self‑service.”
- Example Key Results:
  - ≥ 70% of SME loan applications initiated digitally.
  - Median time from application start to decision ≤ 2 business days.
  - ≥ 80% of customers say the process is “clear and transparent” in surveys.

#### Theme 2: Configurable Risk & Compliance Engine

- Objective:
  - “Make risk and compliance rules configurable, auditable, and fast to update.”
- Example Key Results:
  - Time to implement regulatory rule changes reduced from 3 months → 4 weeks.
  - 0 material regulatory breaches caused by configuration errors.
  - 90% of standard risk rules managed via configuration (not code changes).

#### Theme 3: Operational Excellence & Automation

- Objective:
  - “Reduce manual effort and errors in SME lending operations.”
- Example Key Results:
  - Manual touchpoints per loan reduced by 40%.
  - Operational cost per loan reduced by 25%.
  - Error rates in key processes reduced by 50%.

These themes and goals:

- guide **which initiatives** we fund,
- and **how we judge success**.

---

### 5. How Themes Connect to Initiatives and Roadmaps

Once themes and goals are defined:

1. **Map initiatives** to themes:
   - each initiative should support at least one theme,
   - avoid initiatives that support nothing important.
2. **Allocate capacity** by theme:
   - e.g., 40% experience, 30% platform/risk, 20% operations, 10% experiments.
3. **Build roadmaps** within themes:
   - sequence initiatives and epics that move the metrics.

This avoids:

- roadmaps that are just **feature lists**,
- losing sight of **why** a piece of work exists.

---

### 6. Common Pitfalls with Themes & OKRs

#### 6.1 Too Many Themes

Symptoms:

- 10–15 themes,
- everything seems important.

Result:

- fragmentation,
- no focus,
- confusion about priorities.

Fix:

- force **hard trade‑offs**:
  - combine or drop themes,
  - ask “If we could only do 3–5 things really well, what would they be?”

#### 6.2 Themes That Are Actually Departments

Examples:

- “Sales,” “Marketing,” “Operations,” “Technology.”

Problem:

- these are **org charts**, not strategic pillars,
- do not help decide **what we do for customers**.

Fix:

- define themes around:
  - customer value,
  - capabilities,
  - platforms,
  - or risk/efficiency outcomes.

#### 6.3 KRs That Are Actually Tasks

Examples:

- “Launch new SME lending app.”
- “Implement new credit scoring engine.”

Problem:

- these are outputs, not outcomes.

Fix:

- ask:
  - “So what?”
  - “If we do that, what changes in behavior or results?”
- restate as:
  - conversion rates,
  - error rates,
  - cost per transaction,
  - satisfaction or usage.

---

### 7. Practical Tips for Enterprises/Banks

- **Start small**:
  - even 2–3 good themes with 1–2 measures each are better than none.
- **Align with risk and compliance**:
  - include measures around:
    - regulatory compliance,
    - risk incident reduction,
    - auditability.
- **Make them visible**:
  - put themes and goals in:
    - roadmap views,
    - Jira labels,
    - dashboards.
- **Review quarterly**:
  - check:
    - are themes still right?
    - are goals still realistic?
    - do we need to pivot or re‑balance capacity?

---

### 8. Simple Exercise (For Your Product)

1. Write your **vision** (using the template in `04-vision-and-product-strategy.md`).
2. List:
   - the top **problems and opportunities** from earlier phases.
3. Group them into **3–7 themes**.
4. For each theme, write:
   - 1 objective,
   - 2–4 outcome metrics.
5. Share with:
   - engineering,
   - business stakeholders,
   - risk/compliance (if needed),
   - refine together.

This becomes the **foundation** of your roadmap.
