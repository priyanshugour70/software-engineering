## 09 – Metrics & Research Quality

This file explains how to think about **quality in research**, and which metrics matter.

We cover:

- A. What “good research” means
- B. Quality dimensions
- C. Practical quality checks and metrics

---

### A. What “Good Research” Means

Good research is:

- **Relevant** – tied to concrete decisions and problems.
- **Credible** – based on sound methods and transparent evidence.
- **Actionable** – leads to clear implications and changes.
- **Efficient** – delivers enough confidence for the decisions at hand, without over‑spending time and money.

---

### B. Quality Dimensions

#### 1. Validity

Question:

- Are we **really measuring or observing what we think we are**?

Examples:

- Survey questions that:
  - measure satisfaction with *support* vs *product*.
- Interview prompts that:
  - elicit past behavior vs hypothetical intentions.

Improve by:

- testing instruments,
- avoiding leading questions,
- being explicit about limitations.

---

#### 2. Reliability

Question:

- Would we get **similar results** if we repeated the study with similar participants?

Examples:

- Consistent patterns across:
  - multiple interviews,
  - different researchers,
  - different cohorts.

Improve by:

- using standard guides and coding schemes,
- training moderators,
- documenting methods clearly.

---

#### 3. Representativeness (for Quantitative & Sampling)

Question:

- Do our participants and data **adequately represent** the population we care about?

Concerns:

- Over‑reliance on:
  - “friendly users,”
  - internal staff as proxies,
  - opt‑in panels that may not match real customers.

Improve by:

- sampling across:
  - segments,
  - usage levels,
  - regions,
  - device types,
  - etc.

---

#### 4. Triangulation

Question:

- Do multiple **independent sources** point in the same direction?

Practice:

- Combine:
  - interviews + analytics + tickets + market data.

Benefit:

- Increases confidence that:
  - patterns are real, not artefacts of a single method.

---

### C. Practical Quality Checks & “Metrics”

Rather than obsessing over formal stats, focus on **simple, trackable signs of quality**.

#### 1. Before Research

- [ ] **Decision linked**:
  - Each study has clear decisions and questions.
- [ ] **Method fit**:
  - Selected methods fit the questions (e.g., don’t use surveys to deeply explore behaviors).
- [ ] **Sample defined**:
  - Participant criteria explicit,
  - segmentation considered.

---

#### 2. During Research

- [ ] **Coverage**:
  - Have we:
    - interviewed enough participants per key segment?
    - observed key journeys end‑to‑end?
- [ ] **Consistency**:
  - Are multiple participants telling similar stories (with understandable variations)?
- [ ] **Anomalies flagged**:
  - Outliers or conflicting data noted, not ignored.

---

#### 3. After Research (Synthesis & Readout)

- [ ] **Evidence links**:
  - Each key insight is backed by:
    - quotes,
    - observations,
    - or metrics (not just opinion).
- [ ] **Limitations noted**:
  - Sample sizes,
  - sampling biases,
  - method constraints,
  are clearly described.
- [ ] **Decisions and changes**:
  - Are there visible changes in:
    - problem statements,
    - requirements,
    - roadmap priorities,
    based on the research?

These act as **meta‑metrics** of research impact and quality.
