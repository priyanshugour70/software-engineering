## 07 – Problem Evaluation: Is This a Good Problem Statement?

This file helps you **critically assess** problem statements before moving on.

We cover:

- A. Evaluation principles
- B. Core criteria
- C. Simple scoring model
- D. Example evaluation

---

### A. Evaluation Principles

1. **Focus on the statement, not the idea**  
   - Evaluate **how well the problem is defined**, not whether you “like” the topic.

2. **Use criteria to guide discussion**  
   - Make disagreements explicit (e.g., “I think the scope is too broad”).

3. **Iterate, don’t chase perfection**  
   - The goal is a **strong enough** problem statement to move into research and design, not something frozen forever.

---

### B. Core Evaluation Criteria

You can rate each problem statement along these dimensions:

#### 1. Clarity

Questions:

- Is it **easy to understand** who has which problem and where?
- Is it free of jargon and ambiguous phrases?

Strong:

- “SME owners in Country X using our online onboarding abandon at the document upload step because requirements are unclear.”

Weak:

- “Improve digital onboarding experience.”

---

#### 2. Solution-Agnostic

Questions:

- Does the statement **avoid prescribing a solution**?

Strong:

- “Customers cannot see their loan application status and must call support for updates.”

Weak:

- “We need a mobile tracking app for loan applications.”

---

#### 3. Scope Appropriateness

Questions:

- Is the scope **narrow enough** to tackle in a realistic initiative?
- Is it **broad enough** to matter?

Examples:

- Too broad:
  - “Fix SME banking.”
- Too narrow:
  - “Change the label on one button in the portal.”
- Reasonable:
  - “Reduce abandonment in the SME online onboarding funnel for Country X.”

---

#### 4. Evidence & Impact

Questions:

- Is there **data** or **qualitative evidence** supporting the problem?
- Is the **impact** (on users and business) explained and, where possible, quantified?

Strong:

- “60% of applications are abandoned at step 3; 30% of SME NPS detractors mention onboarding; call center receives 500 status calls/month.”

Weak:

- “We think this is a big issue.”

---

#### 5. Outcome & Testability

Questions:

- Are there **measurable outcomes** or target metrics?
- Will we be able to say later **whether we’ve solved the problem**?

Strong:

- “Reduce average onboarding time from 3 days to under 30 minutes; increase completion from 60% to 80%.”

Weak:

- “Make it better.”

---

#### 6. Alignment & Constraints Awareness

Questions:

- Is the problem linked to **strategy/OKRs**?
- Are **major constraints** (regulatory, technical, operational) acknowledged?

Strong:

- “Supports OKR ‘Increase SME digital adoption by 20%’; must comply with KYC regulation Y and use existing core banking system Z.”

Weak:

- No mention of strategy or constraints.

---

### C. Simple Scoring Model

Use a 1–5 scale:

- 1 – Very weak
- 2 – Weak
- 3 – Acceptable but needs improvement
- 4 – Strong
- 5 – Very strong

Suggested criteria:

- C1 – Clarity
- C2 – Solution‑agnostic
- C3 – Scope appropriateness
- C4 – Evidence & impact
- C5 – Outcome & testability
- C6 – Alignment & constraints awareness

You can sum or average scores, but the real value is in **discussing differences**.

---

### D. Example Evaluation

Draft problem statement:

> “SME owners applying for loans through our branches and partially online must complete multiple paper forms, have little visibility into their application status, and often wait 5+ business days for a decision, causing frustration, drop‑offs, and higher support call volume.”

Evaluation:

- **C1 – Clarity**: 4  
  - Straightforward; you know who, what, and where.
- **C2 – Solution‑agnostic**: 5  
  - No solution implied.
- **C3 – Scope**: 3  
  - Covers both branch and online; discuss whether to narrow initial scope to one channel.
- **C4 – Evidence & impact**: 3  
  - Mentions “often wait 5+ days” and “higher call volume”, but should include precise numbers.
- **C5 – Outcome & testability**: 2  
  - Outcomes not yet specified; need target metrics.
- **C6 – Alignment & constraints**: 3  
  - Implied link to SME growth; constraints not yet written down.

Result:

- Overall: **good starting point** but:
  - add metrics,
  - define desired outcomes,
  - decide on scope (both channels or one first),
  - explicitly list constraints.
