## 21 – Common Mistakes in Problem Definition

This file lists **frequent pitfalls** teams fall into during Problem Definition, with tips to avoid them.

We group mistakes into:

- A. Mindset errors
- B. Process errors
- C. Collaboration errors

---

### A. Mindset Errors

#### 1. Treating Solutions as Problems

Example:

- “We need a new mobile app.”

Why it’s a mistake:

- Jumps straight to how, skips:
  - who,
  - what,
  - why now.

Fix:

- Ask:
  - “What problem would this app solve?”
  - “For whom?”

---

#### 2. Assuming Everyone Shares the Same Understanding

Pattern:

- People use the same word (“onboarding problem”) but mean:
  - different segments,
  - different journeys,
  - different pain points.

Fix:

- Write down:
  - a **concrete problem statement**,
  - share it,
  - ask others to restate it in their own words.

---

#### 3. Overconfidence in Early Framing

Pattern:

- First version of a problem statement is accepted as final.

Fix:

- Treat early statements as:
  - hypotheses,
  - to be refined with:
    - data,
    - research,
    - stakeholder input.

---

### B. Process Errors

#### 4. No Timebox

Pattern:

- Problem Definition goes on indefinitely:
  - “We’re still refining the problem…”

Consequences:

- Delayed decisions,
- analysis paralysis.

Fix:

- Set:
  - a clear end date,
  - intermediate milestones.

---

#### 5. Skipping Current State Analysis

Pattern:

- Writing problem statements based only on:
  - anecdotes,
  - opinions.

Fix:

- Always:
  - look at relevant data,
  - map current journeys/processes,
  before freezing the problem.

---

#### 6. No Explicit Scope

Pattern:

- Problem statement describes:
  - a general area (e.g., “loan process”),
  but has:
  - no clear in/out of scope.

Consequences:

- Scope creep,
- overloaded initiatives.

Fix:

- Add a simple “Scope” section:
  - In scope:
    - <items>.
  - Out of scope:
    - <items>.

---

#### 7. Missing Metrics

Pattern:

- Problem statement only in words:
  - “It’s slow”,
  - “Many customers are unhappy.”

Consequences:

- No clear way to:
  - assess severity,
  - track improvement.

Fix:

- Include:
  - at least 2–3 key metrics (baseline and desired direction).

---

### C. Collaboration Errors

#### 8. Defining Problems in a Product-Only Bubble

Pattern:

- PM writes a problem doc alone,
  - shows it late to:
    - ops,
    - risk,
    - engineering.

Consequences:

- Discover late that:
  - constraints were missed,
  - framing doesn’t match operational reality.

Fix:

- Involve key roles early:
  - short interviews,
  - small workshops.

---

#### 9. Not Involving Users at All

Pattern:

- Problem is defined purely from:
  - internal perspectives.

Consequences:

- Over‑focus on internal efficiency,
  - missing real customer pain,
  - or misreading its importance.

Fix:

- Include:
  - at least some user input (interviews, feedback),
  in refining the problem.

---

#### 10. Hiding Disagreements

Pattern:

- When stakeholders disagree, final write‑up:
  - waters down language to something vague everyone accepts.

Consequences:

- Looks aligned on paper,
  - but disagreements resurface later.

Fix:

- Capture disagreements explicitly:
  - “Ops believes X; Risk believes Y; open question: Z.”
- Decide:
  - whether to resolve now or document as an assumption to test.
