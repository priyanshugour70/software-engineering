## 18 – Startup vs Enterprise Approaches

This file compares **how Product Strategy & Roadmapping differ** between:

- startups/scaleups, and
- large enterprises/banks.

Use it to avoid copying practices blindly from one world to the other.

---

### 1. Key Differences at a Glance

Dimensions:

- **Speed vs constraints**
- **Governance and risk**
- **Number of stakeholders**
- **Legacy systems**
- **Funding model**

Startups:

- fewer constraints,
- fewer stakeholders,
- smaller but more flexible teams.

Enterprises/banks:

- heavy governance,
- many stakeholders,
- large legacy estates,
- formal funding and portfolio processes.

---

### 2. Strategy & Roadmaps in Startups

Characteristics:

- strategy and roadmap often:
  - live in the founders’ heads initially,
  - become formalized as company grows.
- higher:
  - uncertainty,
  - need to pivot,
  - experimentation pace.

Approach:

- **lightweight strategy documents**:
  - clear vision,
  - few strategic bets,
  - concise roadmap.
- **short planning horizons**:
  - strong focus on 0–6 months,
  - rough ideas for 6–18 months.
- roadmaps:
  - more likely to use now/next/later formats.

Risks:

- lack of documentation and communication as team grows,
- over‑focusing on feature output vs learning and outcomes.

Mitigations:

- introduce:
  - simple strategy and roadmap docs early,
  - basic themes and metrics,
  - regular review cadences.

---

### 3. Strategy & Roadmaps in Enterprises/Banks

Characteristics:

- many products and systems,
- strong regulatory obligations,
- more formal governance.

Approach:

- more layers of roadmaps (company, portfolio, product, team),
- more emphasis on:
  - alignment,
  - risk/compliance,
  - capacity planning,
  - dependencies (see `10-dependencies-and-sequencing.md`).

Roadmaps:

- must satisfy:
  - finance,
  - risk/compliance,
  - operations,
  - technology,
  - plus customer and market needs.

Risks:

- strategy and roadmaps becoming:
  - rigid,
  - over‑detailed far into the future,
  - disconnected from reality on the ground.

Mitigations:

- use:
  - multi‑horizon approach (H1/H2/H3),
  - outcome‑driven plans instead of giant feature lists,
  - explicit change and review cadences (see `24-governance-alignment-and-cadence.md`).

---

### 4. What Startups Can Learn from Enterprises

Positive practices to borrow:

- **risk thinking**:
  - understanding operational, security, and compliance risks early,
  - even if governance is lighter.
- **portfolio thinking**:
  - being explicit about where to invest limited resources,
  - avoiding over‑commitment.
- **documentation and decision logs**:
  - even one or two pages help new joiners and partners.

Avoid:

- copying:
  - heavy processes,
  - multi‑layer committees.

---

### 5. What Enterprises Can Learn from Startups

Positive practices to borrow:

- **experimentation mindset**:
  - frequent tests and pilots,
  - willingness to retire failing initiatives.
- **lightweight roadmaps** where possible:
  - focus on:
    - themes,
    - outcomes,
    - and near‑term detail,
  - less on long‑term date‑driven feature lists.
- **fast feedback loops**:
  - getting real user feedback quickly,
  - including internal users.

Avoid:

- superficial “we are agile now” claims without:
  - adapting governance,
  - empowering teams.

---

### 6. Hybrid Situations

Many organizations:

- are **large enterprises** with:
  - pockets of startup‑like units (innovation labs, digital ventures),
  - joint ventures with fintechs.

Implications:

- need:
  - clear contracts and expectations between:
    - fast‑moving units,
    - slower legacy/core areas.
- roadmaps:
  - must show:
    - integration and migration paths,
    - risk mitigation across both worlds.

---

- [ ] Are you mainly operating in:
  - [ ] startup/scaleup context?
  - [ ] large enterprise/bank context?
  - [ ] hybrid (partnership, innovation lab)?

  - [ ] tuned your strategy & roadmap practices to that context?
