## 12 – Documents & Artifacts for Strategy & Roadmapping

This file describes the **key documents and artifacts** you should create and maintain during the Product Strategy & Roadmap phase.

It tells you:

- what each artifact is,
- why it matters,
- who owns it,
- and where it usually lives (e.g., `Confluence`, `Notion`, `Aha!`, `Jira`).

---

### 1. Strategy & Roadmap Document Set (Overview)

At minimum, you want:

1. **Product Vision & Strategy Document**
2. **Strategic Themes & Goals (OKR) Document**
3. **Initiative & Epic Catalogue**
4. **Roadmap Views (multi‑level)**
5. **Decision & Assumption Log**
6. **Review & Governance Notes**

These can be:

- separate pages,
- or sections in a larger playbook, as long as:
  - they are easy to find,
  - and clearly owned.

---

### 2. Product Vision & Strategy Document

Purpose:

- capture:
  - vision,
  - strategic pillars/themes,
  - diagnosis,
  - where we will/will not play,
  - how we will win,
  - key bets and risks.

Owner:

- product lead / head of product (with inputs from others).

Where:

- `Confluence`, `Notion`, or similar.

Connection:

- based on guidance from `04-vision-and-product-strategy.md`.

Key sections:

- 1–2 pages of core content,
- supporting appendices (market analysis, research links, etc.).

---

### 3. Strategic Themes & Goals (OKR) Document

Purpose:

- define:
  - 3–7 themes,
  - objectives and outcome metrics for each.

Owner:

- product leadership with input from:
  - engineering,
  - business,
  - risk/compliance.

Where:

- standalone doc,
- or part of a broader “strategy” space,
- mirrored in OKR tooling if present.

Connection:

- directly implemented from `05-strategic-themes-goals-and-okrs.md`.

Contents:

- theme descriptions,
- objectives per theme,
- key results/metrics,
- mapping to company/segment-level OKRs.

---

### 4. Initiative & Epic Catalogue

Purpose:

- maintain a **single list** of:
  - initiatives,
  - epics,
  - their themes, value, size, and status.

Owner:

- product managers and portfolio managers.

Where:

- could be:
  - structured table in `Confluence`/`Notion`/`Sheets`,
  - or within `Aha!` / `Productboard` / `Jira Align`.

Fields (example):

- Name,
- Description and problem statement,
- Theme(s),
- Linked objectives/OKRs,
- Initiative vs epic,
- Owner squad,
- Status,
- Value/impact (e.g., RICE or WSJF inputs),
- Size/effort,
- Dependencies.

Benefits:

- you avoid:
  - multiple conflicting lists of initiatives,
  - confusion about naming,
  - loss of context over time.

---

### 5. Roadmap Views

Purpose:

- represent:
  - how initiatives & epics unfold over time,
  - at different levels (see `07-roadmap-levels-and-views.md`).

Artifacts:

- company/segment roadmap (multi‑year),
- portfolio roadmap (12–24 months),
- product roadmap (6–18 months),
- team/squad roadmap (near‑term).

Where:

- `Aha!`, `Productboard`, `Jira Align`, or:
  - visual diagrams,
  - or well‑structured tables and timelines.

Key:

- each roadmap view should link back to:
  - initiatives/epics in the catalogue,
  - strategy and theme documents.

---

### 6. Decision & Assumption Log

Purpose:

- track:
  - **major decisions** related to strategy and roadmaps,
  - key assumptions and what would invalidate them.

Reasons:

- in enterprises/banks, people rotate roles,
- decisions and history get lost,
- new stakeholders question “why this roadmap?”.

Simple structure:

- Date,
- Decision,
- Alternatives considered,
- Rationale (including risks),
- Owners and approvers,
- Linked docs/meetings.

Where:

- lightweight table in `Confluence`/`Notion`/`Sheets`,
- or dedicated “decision log” page for the product/portfolio.

---

### 7. Review & Governance Notes

Purpose:

- store notes from:
  - quarterly business reviews (QBRs),
  - portfolio reviews,
  - risk/governance committees,
  - strategy refresh workshops.

Benefits:

- continuity of:
  - why priorities changed,
  - how metrics evolved,
  - what actions were agreed.

Where:

- structured meeting series in `Confluence`/`Notion`:
  - one page per session,
  - supporting attachments (slides, screenshots).

Tip:

- include:
  - “what changed in the roadmap and why?” section in each review.

---

### 8. Supporting Artifacts

Some additional useful artifacts:

- **Opportunity & Problem Backlog**:
  - source for initiatives,
  - linked to phases 1–3.
- **Business Case & ROI Models**:
  - spreadsheets or tools (see `4-business-case-roi` module),
  - linked to initiatives.
- **Feasibility Assessments**:
  - key findings from technical, risk, legal checks (phase 5).
- **Journey Maps & Service Blueprints**:
  - visual context for where initiatives fit.

These should be:

- discoverable from:
  - the strategy and roadmap documents,
  - and initiative catalogue.

---

### 9. Beginner Checklist

- [ ] Is there:
  - [ ] a single, up‑to‑date **Product Vision & Strategy** doc?
  - [ ] a **Themes & Goals** doc?
  - [ ] a single **Initiative & Epic Catalogue**?
- [ ] Are:
  - [ ] roadmap views linked to initiatives and themes?
  - [ ] decisions and assumptions logged somewhere?
  - [ ] review and governance notes findable?

If you struggle to find these quickly:

- your documentation is likely **fragmented**,
- and strategy/roadmap work will rely too much on memory and PowerPoint slides.
