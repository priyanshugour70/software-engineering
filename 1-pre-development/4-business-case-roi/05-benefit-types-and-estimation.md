## 05 – Benefit Types & Estimation

This file explains **how initiatives create value** and how to estimate that value.

We cover:

- A. Benefit categories
- B. Step‑by‑step estimation patterns
- C. Banking‑focused examples

---

### A. Benefit Categories

#### 1. Revenue Benefits

- **New revenue**
  - acquiring new customers,
  - entering new segments or markets,
  - launching new products.
- **Expanded revenue**
  - cross‑sell and upsell,
  - increased usage/volume,
  - improved pricing or margins.
- **Retained revenue**
  - reduced churn,
  - higher renewal rates.

---

#### 2. Cost Benefits

- **Operational efficiency**
  - fewer manual steps,
  - better straight‑through processing,
  - lower error/rework rates.
- **Support & service**
  - fewer support contacts per customer,
  - shorter handling times.
- **Technology & infrastructure**
  - consolidation of systems,
  - reduction in license or hosting costs.

---

#### 3. Risk Benefits

- **Credit risk**
  - reduced default rates,
  - improved recovery.
- **Fraud risk**
  - fewer fraud incidents,
  - reduced fraud loss amounts.
- **Compliance risk**
  - lower probability of regulatory fines,
  - reduced remediation costs.

Risk benefits can be expressed as:

- reduced **expected loss**:
  - probability × impact.

---

#### 4. Intangible / Strategic Benefits

- brand and reputation,
- customer satisfaction and NPS,
- platform positioning (enabling future products),
- employee productivity and satisfaction.

These may be harder to quantify but should still be:

- described,
- linked to strategy,
- and, where possible, proxied by metrics (e.g., NPS, CSAT).

---

### B. Estimation Patterns

Across categories, the pattern is usually:

> **Baseline × Change × Value per Unit**

Where:

- **Baseline**
  - current volume or rate,
  - e.g., current number of SME loans, current abandonment rate.
- **Change**
  - expected improvement (absolute or relative),
  - e.g., +20% volume, −10 percentage points in abandonment.
- **Value per Unit**
  - revenue or cost per unit,
  - e.g., profit per loan, cost per support call.

---

### C. Example Estimations (Banking Focus)

#### 1. Revenue Benefit – Increased SME Loan Volume

Baseline:

- 10,000 SME loan applications per year,
- 40% approval and draw‑down,
- average profit per loan = 2,000.

Expected change (base case):

- improved UX and speed → applications +15%,
- better conversion → approval/draw‑down rate +5 percentage points (from 40% → 45%).

Estimation:

- new applications = 11,500,
- drawn loans:
  - before: 10,000 × 40% = 4,000,
  - after: 11,500 × 45% = 5,175,
  - delta = 1,175 additional loans.
- revenue uplift (profit):
  - 1,175 × 2,000 = **2.35M per year** (before risk and discounting).

---

#### 2. Cost Benefit – Reduced Manual Processing

Baseline:

- each SME loan currently requires:
  - 2 hours of manual back‑office work,
  - cost per hour = 40,
  - 4,000 loans per year.

Expected change:

- new system reduces manual work by 50%.

Estimation:

- current annual cost:
  - 2 hours × 40 × 4,000 = 320,000.
- future annual cost:
  - 1 hour × 40 × 5,175 (higher volume from previous example) = 207,000.
- cost saving:
  - 320,000 − 207,000 ≈ **113,000 per year**.

---

#### 3. Risk Benefit – Reduced Fraud Loss

Baseline:

- current fraud losses in segment: 5M per year.

Expected change:

- improved detection reduces losses by 10–20%.

Estimation:

- base case:
  - 10% reduction → 0.5M per year,
- conservative:
  - 5% reduction → 0.25M per year,
- aggressive:
  - 20% reduction → 1.0M per year.

These become:

- separate scenario lines in the financial model.

---

#### 4. Intangible Benefits – Example Handling

Example:

- NPS improvement and brand strengthening may:
  - indirectly drive:
    - higher acquisition,
    - reduced churn,
    - easier cross‑sell.

Approach:

- mention in business case qualitatively,
- in some orgs, approximate via:
  - linking NPS bands to churn differences,
  - but only if supported by internal data.
