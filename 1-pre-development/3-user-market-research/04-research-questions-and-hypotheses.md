## 04 – Research Questions & Hypotheses

This file explains how to turn vague interests into **clear research questions and hypotheses** that drive focused studies.

We cover:

- A. Principles
- B. From problems to research questions
- C. Writing good hypotheses
- D. Examples for SME lending

---

### A. Principles

1. **Decision‑driven**  
   Every research question should be tied to a **decision** you need to make.

2. **Specific, not generic**  
   Avoid “learn more about our users.” Instead ask:
   - “How do SMEs decide which bank to use for lending?”

3. **Connected to problems and metrics**  
   Questions should:
   - relate to problem statements,
   - help explain or change key metrics.

4. **Hypotheses guide, not bias**  
   Hypotheses give you a **starting point**, not a conclusion to prove.

---

### B. From Problems to Research Questions

Given a problem statement from `2-problem-definition/`, ask:

> “What do we need to know about users, behaviors, or markets to address this problem well?”

Example problem:

> “SME owners in Country X applying for loans face a slow, opaque process with little status visibility, causing abandonment and support calls.”

Potential research questions:

- **Behaviors & workflows**
  - How do SMEs currently apply for loans (inside and outside our bank)?
  - What steps do they take before, during, and after application?
- **Pain points**
  - At which steps do they feel most confused or anxious?
  - What information are they missing at each point?
- **Mental models**
  - How do SMEs think the approval process works?
  - What do they believe “under review” means?
- **Decision factors**
  - When choosing a provider, how do they trade off:
    - speed,
    - transparency,
    - relationship,
    - cost?
- **Stakeholder roles**
  - What role do accountants or advisors play in the decision?

These questions then drive:

- interview guides,
- analytics deep dives,
- survey items.

### C. Writing Good Hypotheses

Hypotheses help you:

- clarify your current beliefs,
- design research to **challenge** them.

Structure:

> “We believe that **[segment]** does **[behavior]** because **[reason]**, and that this leads to **[impact].**”

or:

> “We believe that **[factor]** is a key driver of **[outcome]** for **[segment].**”

Characteristics of good hypotheses:

- **Testable** – you can gather evidence to support or refute them.
- **Specific** – names segment, behavior, and impact.
- **Open to being wrong** – not just restating your desired solution.

Example hypotheses (SME lending):

- “We believe that SMEs abandon online applications mainly because they are unsure which documents are required, not because the process is too long.”
- “We believe that relationship managers’ recommendations are a major factor in SMEs choosing a lending product, more than website information alone.”
- “We believe that SMEs care more about clear status updates and next steps than about absolute processing time, provided decisions are made within a few days.”

Use:

- to focus:
  - interview and survey questions,
  - analytics exploration.

### D. Examples (Problem → Questions & Hypotheses)

#### 1. Problem: High Abandonment in SME Online Onboarding

Problem statement (simplified):

> “Existing SME customers abandon online onboarding at the document upload step, leading to low completion and hand‑offs to branches.”

Research questions:

- How do SMEs prepare documents today?
- What do they understand from our current document requirements?
- At what point do they decide to give up or switch to branch?
- What reassurance or guidance would prevent abandonment?

Hypotheses:

- “We believe many SMEs don’t read the full requirements list before starting, leading to surprises mid‑flow.”
- “We believe the language and categories we use for documents (e.g., internal names) do not match SME mental models, causing confusion.”

---

#### 2. Problem: Frequent Status Inquiry Calls for Loan Applications

Problem statement (simplified):

> “SMEs often call to ask about loan status because portal status is generic and no proactive updates are sent.”

Research questions:

- How do SMEs interpret different status labels (e.g., ‘processing’, ‘under review’)?
- What information do they expect to receive after they submit?
- How often are they willing to wait before seeking an update?

Hypotheses:

- “We believe that showing an estimated decision time and clearer status explanations will reduce status calls.”
- “We believe that SMEs want to know whether **any action is needed from them** more than they want exact back‑office details.”

---

### E. How to Use This File

When starting a new research effort:

1. Take your latest **problem statements**.
2. For each, write:
   - 3–7 **research questions**,
   - 2–5 **hypotheses**.
3. Use them to:
   - design your research plan,
   - keep interviews and analytics focused,
   - structure your synthesis later (“Which hypotheses were supported? Which were challenged?”).
