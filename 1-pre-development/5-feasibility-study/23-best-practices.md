## 23 – Best Practices for Feasibility Studies

This file summarizes **practical best practices** for running Feasibility well.

We group them into:

- A. Mindset
- B. Process & scope
- C. Collaboration
- D. Documentation & follow‑through

---

### A. Mindset Best Practices

#### 1. Reality-First, Not Wishful Thinking

- Treat Feasibility as:
  - a search for **truth and constraints**, not just validation of a desired design.

---

#### 2. “Options, Not Just Answers”

- Aim to present:
  - 1–3 **viable solution options** with trade‑offs,
not a single predetermined path.

---

### B. Process & Scope Best Practices

#### 3. Timebox Work and Focus on the Big Rocks

- Don’t attempt:
  - to design all details in Feasibility.
- Focus on:
  - identifying big risks,
  - constraints,
  - and show‑stopping issues.

---

#### 4. Start from Current State Reality

- Review:
  - existing systems,
  - processes,
  - policies,
  before sketching solutions.

Prevents:

- infeasible or naive proposals.

---

#### 5. Make Non-Functional Requirements Explicit

- Document:
  - performance,
  - scalability,
  - availability,
  - security,
  - and compliance requirements clearly.

---

### C. Collaboration Best Practices

#### 6. Involve Key Functions Early

- Architecture, ops, security, legal, risk:
  - should be involved from the start,
  - not just review a finished doc.

---

#### 7. Use Workshops for Shared Understanding

- Run:
  - multi‑disciplinary workshops to:
    - map systems,
    - map processes,
    - surface risks.

This builds:

- alignment and reduces later re‑hashing.

---

### D. Documentation & Follow-Through Best Practices

#### 8. Keep Summaries Short and Clear

- Have:
  - a 2–3 page feasibility summary that:
    - non‑experts can understand,
    - with:
      - main options,
      - main risks,
      - recommended direction.

---

#### 9. Link Feasibility Outputs to Design, Delivery, and Risk

- Ensure:
  - risk register and constraints feed:
    - architecture RFCs/HLDs,
    - delivery plans,
    - risk/governance artifacts.

---

#### 10. Revisit Feasibility When Context Changes

- If:
  - major scope,
  - platform,
  - or regulatory changes occur,
  revisit:
  - key assumptions and feasibility conclusions.
