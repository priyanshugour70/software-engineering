## 25 – Glossary of Terms (Problem Definition)

This file defines key terms used throughout the **Problem Definition** handbook.

---

### A

**Abandonment Rate**  
The percentage of users who start a process (e.g., onboarding) but do not finish it.

**Assumption**  
Something believed to be true but not yet proven; should be written down and later validated or disproven.

---

### B

**Baseline (Metric)**  
The current measured value of a metric before any changes (e.g., average time to decision is 5 days).

**Business Outcome**  
A measurable effect on the organization, such as increased revenue, reduced cost, or lower risk.

---

### C

**Constraint**  
A limitation that must be respected when solving a problem (e.g., regulatory rules, system capabilities, staffing levels).

**Current State**  
How things work today, including processes, metrics, and user experience.

---

### D

**Desired State**  
How we want things to be in the future once a problem is solved (often expressed with target metrics and experience descriptions).

**Discovery (Problem Definition Context)**  
Activities aimed at understanding and clarifying a specific problem, distinct from broader market discovery.

---

### E

**Evidence**  
Data, observations, and stories that support or challenge a problem statement (e.g., analytics, interviews, tickets).

---

### F

**Funnel**  
A step‑by‑step breakdown of a process, showing how many users move through each step and where they drop off.

---

### H

**Hypothesis**  
An educated guess stated in a testable way, often used for initial problem framing before full validation.

---

### I

**Impact**  
The effect a problem has on users and the business (e.g., time wasted, lost revenue, increased risk).

---

### J

**Job-To-Be-Done (JTBD)**  
The underlying task or goal a user is trying to accomplish, independent of any particular solution.

---

### M

**Metric**  
A number used to measure something, such as time to complete, error rate, or satisfaction score.

---

### O

**Objective**  
What you aim to achieve in Problem Definition (e.g., produce 2–3 clear, testable problem statements for SME onboarding).

**Outcome**  
The change you expect after solving a problem; often linked to specific metrics (e.g., faster onboarding, fewer errors).

**Opportunity (from phase 1)**  
A potential area to create value, which Problem Definition narrows into specific, well‑formed problems.

---

### P

**Problem Statement**  
The structured description of a problem, explaining who is affected, what is wrong, why it matters, and in what context.

**Problem Space**  
The set of issues, pains, and gaps you are trying to understand and define before designing solutions.

---

### R

**Risk**  
The chance that something negative happens, such as project failure, regulatory breach, or wasted effort.

---

### S

**Scope**  
The boundaries of what a problem covers (in scope) and does not cover (out of scope), such as which segments, journeys, regions, or systems.

**Solution Space**  
The set of possible designs, features, and implementations you consider after defining the problem.

**Stakeholder**  
Any person or group with an interest in or influence over the problem and its solution (e.g., business owners, engineers, risk, ops).

---

### T

**Target (Metric)**  
The desired future value of a metric once a problem is addressed (e.g., reduce abandonment from 40% to 15%).

**Timebox**  
A fixed period allocated to complete Problem Definition activities for a given problem or domain.

---

### V

**Value (User)**  
The benefit users get when a problem is solved, such as time saved, reduced frustration, or increased confidence.

**Value (Business)**  
The benefit the organization gets, such as higher revenue, better efficiency, or lower losses.

---

### W

**Workflow (Problem Definition)**  
The step‑by‑step process used to move from an opportunity to a finalized problem statement package (see `03-workflow.md`).
