## 16 – B2B vs B2C Research

User & Market Research looks different when your users are:

- **Consumers (B2C)** – individuals using products for personal needs.
- **Businesses (B2B)** – organizations with multiple roles and decision‑makers.

This file highlights key differences and how to adapt.

---

### A. B2C Research

Characteristics:

- Users:
  - individuals,
  - often many in number.
- Decisions:
  - more emotional, preference‑driven,
  - often shorter cycles.

Research focus:

- motivations and attitudes,
- ease of use and delight,
- trust and risk perception,
- brand and messaging.

Recruitment:

- can often use:
  - panels,
  - intercepts (in‑product prompts),
  - public calls.

Methods emphasis:

- usability tests,
- interviews,
- surveys,
- analytics and experimentation.

---

### B. B2B Research

Characteristics:

- Users and customers:
  - multiple stakeholders (buyers, users, approvers, influencers),
  - complex organizations.
- Decisions:
  - more rational/structured on surface,
  - slower sales cycles,
  - contract and risk focused.

Research focus:

- workflows and processes within organizations,
- decision chains and approval flows,
- organizational goals and constraints,
- integration with existing tools and systems.

Recruitment:

- often via:
  - account managers,
  - customer success,
  - professional networks.

Methods emphasis:

- stakeholder mapping,
- contextual inquiry (in offices/branches/back‑office),
- interviews with multiple roles (user, manager, admin),
- analysis of usage by role and account.

---

### C. Specific Considerations for B2B in Banking

- Stakeholders:
  - SME owners,
  - CFOs,
  - accountants,
  - relationship managers,
  - operations teams.
- Research must:
  - respect confidentiality,
  - accommodate their time constraints,
  - often be scheduled around business hours and reporting cycles.

Problem examples:

- understanding how SMEs manage cash‑flow across multiple banks,
- how corporates decide on cash management services,
- how accountants influence product adoption.

---

### D. Blended Contexts

Sometimes:

- products serve both:
  - individual users,
  - businesses,
  or:
  - business tools are used by individuals (e.g., freelancers).

In such cases:

- treat segments separately,
- design research for each:
  - even if some methods overlap.
