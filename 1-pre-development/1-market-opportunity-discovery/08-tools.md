## 08 – Tools for Market Opportunity Discovery

This file lists **practical tools** you can use at each step of discovery.  
Tools are grouped by purpose, with concrete examples for startups and enterprises/banks.

We cover:

- A. Collaboration & Documentation
- B. Research & Interviews
- C. Analytics & Data
- D. Market & Competitor Intelligence
- E. Portfolio Management & Roadmapping

> You do **not** need all of these. Pick the ones that fit your context and are already available in your organization.

---

### A. Collaboration & Documentation

These tools keep everyone aligned, store findings, and make discovery reusable.

#### 1. Documentation Spaces

- **Confluence / SharePoint / Notion / Google Docs**

Use for:

- Writing opportunity briefs and decision docs.
- Storing interview notes and research summaries.
- Capturing checklists, templates, and frameworks.

Beginner tips:

- Create a **single “Discovery” space** with:
  - One page per opportunity.
  - Section for “Inputs & Data”.
  - Section for “Decisions & Next Steps”.

---

#### 2. Whiteboarding & Workshops

- **Miro, FigJam, Mural, Lucidchart**

Use for:

- Clustering signals into themes.
- Mapping customer journeys.
- Drawing Opportunity Solution Trees.
- Facilitating workshops with remote stakeholders.

Beginner tips:

- Start from simple templates: sticky notes, 2×2 matrices, journey maps.

---

### B. Research & Interviews

#### 1. Interview & Meeting Tools

- **Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet**

Use for:

- Remote customer and stakeholder interviews.
- Recording sessions (if allowed) for later review.

---

#### 2. Note‑Taking & Transcription

- **Notion, Google Docs, OneNote**
- **Transcription tools** (e.g., Otter, in‑built meeting transcription if approved by compliance)

Use for:

- Structured interview notes (with consistent question sections).
- Quickly revisiting exact phrases customers used.

Compliance note:

- In banks and regulated orgs, ensure **recording and data storage** comply with policies.

---

### C. Analytics & Data

#### 1. Product Analytics

- **Amplitude, Mixpanel, Heap, Pendo, in‑house event pipelines**

Use for:

- Funnel and behavior analysis.
- Segmentation and cohort studies.
- Identifying key “aha” moments and drop‑offs.

For beginners:

- Start with **pre‑built dashboards**:
  - Onboarding completion.
  - Daily/Monthly Active Users.
  - Feature usage.

---

#### 2. Business Intelligence (BI)

- **Tableau, Power BI, Looker, Qlik, in‑house BI**

Use for:

- Revenue and margin by segment / product.
- Portfolio performance and risk metrics.
- Operational KPIs (processing times, SLA breaches).

Tips in enterprises/banks:

- Work with BI teams – don’t try to become a SQL expert overnight.
- Ask for **exploratory slices** (e.g., “show SME revenue by industry over 3 years”).

---

#### 3. Data Warehouses / Lakes

- **Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift, Hadoop, on‑prem data warehouses**

Used primarily by:

- Data analysts and engineers.

Role in discovery:

- Provide **ad‑hoc deeper analysis** when dashboards are not enough.

---

### D. Market & Competitor Intelligence

#### 1. Competitive Intelligence Platforms

- Examples: **CB Insights, Crunchbase, Similarweb, Owler**, local equivalents.

Use for:

- Tracking competitor funding, launches, and partnerships.
- Discovering new entrants in your space.

---

#### 2. News & Industry Feeds

- **Google Alerts**, RSS feeds, LinkedIn, X (Twitter), industry newsletters.

Use for:

- Staying on top of:
  - new product launches,
  - regulatory news,
  - macro trends affecting your segment.

Beginner tip:

- Set up a **few focused alerts**:
  - “SME lending digital banking [your country]”
  - “Open banking regulation [region]”

---

#### 3. App Stores & Review Sites

- **Apple App Store, Google Play, G2, Capterra, Trustpilot**

Use for:

- Reading **customer reviews** of competitors.
- Seeing what customers praise or complain about most.

This is an excellent way to build **empathy and insights quickly**.

---

### E. Portfolio Management & Roadmapping

These tools help turn your prioritized opportunities into planned work.

#### 1. Product Discovery & Portfolio Tools

- **Jira Product Discovery, Productboard, Aha!, airfocus**

Features:

- Capture opportunity ideas with metadata.
- Link opportunities to customer insights and data.
- Score and prioritize opportunities.
- Connect to roadmaps and delivery backlogs.

If you don’t have these, you can approximate in:

- **Trello, Asana, Jira** with custom fields and boards.

---

#### 2. Roadmapping & Alignment

- **Aha!, Productboard, Notion, Confluence roadmaps**

Use for:

- Showing how **prioritized opportunities** map to:
  - epics and features,
  - timelines,
  - teams.

Link each roadmap item back to:

- its **originating opportunity**, and
- **evidence** gathered during discovery.

---

### F. Tooling in Banks and Large Enterprises (Special Notes)

In banks, you must work within:

- Approved collaboration and storage tools.
- Data access and privacy controls.
- Recording and monitoring rules for meetings.

Practical advice:

- Ask early: **“Which tools are approved for storing customer data and recordings?”**
- If modern tools (Miro, Notion) are restricted:
  - Use **Excel/PowerPoint** for matrices and maps.
  - Use **SharePoint/Confluence** as your central wiki.

The **method** matters more than the specific brand of tool.

---

### G. How to Set Up a Minimal, Effective Tool Stack

For a **small team or startup**, a simple stack could be:

- Docs: **Notion or Google Docs**
- Whiteboarding: **Miro or FigJam**
- Analytics: **Mixpanel or Amplitude**
- BI: basic dashboards from your data team
- Task tracking: **Jira** or **Trello**

For a **large enterprise / bank**, a realistic stack may be:

- Docs: **Confluence / SharePoint / internal wiki**
- Whiteboarding: **Miro** (if approved) or **PowerPoint** + wall sessions
- Analytics: existing **BI + product analytics** tools
- Portfolio: **Aha!, Productboard, Jira Product Discovery**, or internal equivalents
