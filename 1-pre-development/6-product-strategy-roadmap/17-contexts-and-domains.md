## 17 – Contexts and Domains for Product Strategy & Roadmapping

This file explains how **Product Strategy & Roadmap work changes by context**:

- B2B vs B2C,
- SME lending vs retail banking vs payments,
- internal platforms vs customer‑facing products.

Use it to adjust the ideas in this module to **your specific domain**.

---

### 1. Dimensions of Context

Important dimensions:

- **Customer type**:
  - consumers (B2C),
  - businesses (B2B/B2B2C),
  - internal users (back‑office, contact center).
- **Regulation level**:
  - heavily regulated (banking, insurance, healthcare),
  - moderately regulated (telecoms, utilities),
  - lightly regulated (many consumer apps).
- **Product type**:
  - digital journeys (e.g., SME lending),
  - platforms (risk, data, identity),
  - tools (internal operations, analytics).

Each dimension:

- changes:
  - who is involved,
  - which constraints dominate,
  - how detailed and formal strategy & roadmaps need to be.

---

### 2. Consumer (B2C) vs Business (B2B/SME) Products

#### 2.1 B2C (Retail Banking App Example)

Characteristics:

- large user base,
- more:
  - behavioral/experience focus,
  - marketing and brand alignment.

Strategy & roadmap implications:

- heavy emphasis on:
  - customer experience themes (onboarding, engagement, self‑service),
  - experimentation (A/B testing, growth loops).
- metrics:
  - acquisition, activation, engagement,
  - churn/retention,
  - NPS or satisfaction.

Roadmap often includes:

- many small iterations and experiments,
- cross‑channel consistency (web, app, branch, call center).

#### 2.2 B2B/SME (Digital SME Lending Example)

Characteristics:

- fewer accounts than B2C, but:
  - higher value per relationship,
  - more complex products and processes.

Strategy & roadmap implications:

- focus on:
  - relationship value,
  - lifecycle (acquisition → servicing → expansion),
  - integration with accounting/ERP tools.
- more:
  - complex decisioning and risk considerations,
  - contractual/legal dimensions.

Roadmap must:

- consider:
  - onboarding complexity,
  - custom deals,
  - integration projects.

---

### 3. Internal Platforms vs Customer‑Facing Products

#### 3.1 Customer‑Facing Products

Examples:

- digital banking channels,
- lending journeys,
- merchant portals.

Strategy themes:

- growth and engagement,
- experience quality,
- differentiation vs competitors.

Roadmaps:

- link clearly to customer outcomes and market positioning.

#### 3.2 Internal Platforms (Risk, Data, Identity, Integration)

Examples:

- risk engines,
- KYC platforms,
- data lakes and analytics platforms,
- integration layers / API gateways.

Strategy themes:

- reliability and scalability,
- configurability and speed of change,
- re‑use across multiple products.

Roadmaps:

- focus on:
  - capabilities offered to consuming products,
  - reduction in time‑to‑market,
  - reduction in operational and risk issues.

Important:

- platform strategies and roadmaps:
  - must be **co‑created** with product teams consuming them,
  - to avoid building unused capabilities.

---

### 4. Domain Examples in Financial Services

#### 4.1 SME Lending

Key aspects:

- risk and compliance intensive,
- complex onboarding and documentation,
- high value per customer.

Strategy & roadmap emphasis:

- balancing:
  - speed and transparency for SMEs,
  - strong risk and compliance controls.
- sequencing:
  - platform risk capabilities,
  - digital experiences,
  - operations tools.

#### 4.2 Retail Payments (Cards, Wallets)

Key aspects:

- high volume transactions,
- user experience and reliability critical,
- many ecosystem partners (schemes, PSPs, wallets).

Strategy & roadmap emphasis:

- resilience and uptime,
- fraud detection and prevention,
- value‑added services (rewards, budgeting tools).

Roadmaps often:

- contain:
  - strong regulatory and scheme‑driven requirements,
  - partnership‑driven initiatives.

#### 4.3 Wealth / Investment Products

Key aspects:

- trust and compliance paramount,
- longer decision cycles,
- advisory and suitability requirements.

Strategy & roadmap emphasis:

- suitability and advice tooling,
- risk profiling and portfolio construction,
- digital engagement for ongoing relationship.

---

### 5. Outside Financial Services (Brief)

These principles also apply to:

- SaaS platforms (project management, CRM, HR),
- logistics and supply chain products,
- health tech (with additional privacy constraints).

Differences often lie in:

- which constraints dominate (e.g., HIPAA in healthcare),
- how quickly experiments can run,
- what “value” and risk mean in that domain.

---

### 6. Adapting This Module to Your Context

Steps:

1. Identify your:
   - customer types,
   - regulation level,
   - product type (journey, platform, tool).
2. Revisit:
   - themes,
   - metrics,
   - roles and stakeholders in this module.
3. Adjust:
   - which themes you use (e.g., add “Ecosystem & Partnerships”),
   - which metrics matter,
   - who you must involve early (e.g., additional regulators or partners).

You do **not** need a totally different playbook:

- you adjust the playbook to your domain realities.
