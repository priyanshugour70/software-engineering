## 16 – Startup vs Enterprise in Business Case & ROI

This file explains how Business Case & ROI work differs between **startups** and **large enterprises/banks**.

We cover:

- A. Startups
- B. Enterprises / banks
- C. Moving between the two

---

### A. Business Case & ROI in Startups

Characteristics:

- constrained runway,
- high uncertainty about product‑market fit,
- small leadership group making fast decisions.

Business case style:

- lighter, more iterative,
- often:
  - simple spreadsheets,
  - lean canvases,
  - board decks.

Focus:

- **growth and learning**:
  - does this unlock product‑market fit?
  - does it improve acquisition, activation, retention, or monetization?
- cash runway and:
  - time to next funding event or breakeven.

Good practice:

- still:
  - write down assumptions,
  - do simple scenario thinking,
  - consider opportunity cost vs alternative bets.
- keep models:
  - simple enough to adjust monthly or quarterly.

---

### B. Business Case & ROI in Enterprises / Banks

Characteristics:

- complex portfolios,
- multiple business lines,
- regulated capital and risk frameworks.

Business case style:

- more formal,
- often:
  - required templates,
  - committees and stage‑gates,
  - deeper involvement from finance and risk.

Focus:

- **capital allocation**:
  - optimizing across many initiatives,
  - balancing:
    - growth,
    - efficiency,
    - risk reduction,
    - regulatory compliance.

Good practice:

- go beyond “tick‑box” business cases:
  - ensure numbers are actually understood,
  - use scenarios and sensitivities,
  - link to post‑launch benefit tracking.

Risks:

- over‑engineering models that:
  - look precise but rest on weak assumptions,
  - slow decisions unnecessarily.

---

### C. Moving Between Contexts

#### From Startup → Enterprise

Bring:

- comfort with:
  - uncertainty,
  - iteration,
  - simple, driver‑based models.

Adapt:

- learn:
  - approval processes,
  - capital/risk frameworks,
  - documentation expectations.

Focus:

- using your startup experience to:
  - simplify complex cases,
  - keep focus on drivers and decisions,
  - avoid “model theatre” (models that look impressive but don’t help decisions).

---

#### From Enterprise → Startup

Bring:

- strength in:
  - structuring business cases,
  - thinking about risk and portfolio,
  - collaborating with finance and risk.

Adapt:

- drop:
  - unnecessary formality,
  - excessive precision.
- prioritize:
  - speed and learning loops.

Focus:

- creating:
  - right‑sized business cases that:
    - help founders and boards,
    - don’t stall experimentation.
