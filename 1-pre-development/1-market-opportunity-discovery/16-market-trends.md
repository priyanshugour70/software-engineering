## 16 – Market Trends that Influence Opportunities

This file explains **common types of trends** that create or reshape market opportunities, with a focus on technology and financial services.  
It is meant as a **conceptual guide**, not a real‑time trends report.

We cover:

- A. Types of trends to watch
- B. Examples relevant to banking and software
- C. How to connect trends to opportunities

---

### A. Types of Trends

#### 1. Technological Trends

Changes in:

- computing power and infrastructure (cloud, edge),
- data and AI capabilities,
- connectivity (5G, IoT, APIs),
- user devices (smartphones, wearables).

Impact:

- Enable **new products and experiences** that were not feasible before.
- Lower the cost of serving certain segments.

---

#### 2. Regulatory and Policy Trends

Especially important in:

- banking,
- healthcare,
- telecoms,
- energy.

Regulation can:

- **force changes** (compliance opportunities),
- **open new spaces** (e.g., open banking, digital ID frameworks).

---

#### 3. Economic and Macro Trends

Examples:

- Interest rate shifts,
- inflation and cost of living,
- recessions or booms,
- globalization vs localization.

Impact:

- Change **customer priorities** (e.g., cost savings vs growth).
- Affect **risk appetite** of banks and investors.

---

#### 4. Social and Demographic Trends

Examples:

- Aging populations in developed countries.
- Growing middle class in emerging markets.
- Urbanization.
- Shifts in trust (e.g., preference for digital over branch).

Impact:

- Create new **segment needs** and expectations.

---

#### 5. Competitive and Industry Structure Trends

Examples:

- Entry of big tech into financial services.
- Consolidation through mergers and acquisitions.
- Rise of niche specialists (e.g., vertical SaaS, fintechs).

Impact:

- Change **where value is captured** in the value chain.
- Force incumbents to **react or reposition**.

---

### B. Examples (Conceptual, Not Time-Specific)

#### 1. Open Banking and API‑Driven Finance

Trend type:

- Regulatory + technological.

Effects:

- Banks must **expose account and payment data** via APIs (under customer consent).
- Third‑party providers can build apps on top of this data.

Opportunities:

- For banks:
  - better **aggregated financial views** for customers,
  - tailored lending based on richer transaction data.
- For fintechs:
  - **account aggregation**,
  - **spending insights**,
  - **cash‑flow tools** for SMEs.

---

#### 2. Shift to Real‑Time Payments

Trend type:

- Technological + regulatory.

Effects:

- Payments clear **in seconds** instead of days.
- Customers expect **instant settlement and confirmation**.

Opportunities:

- New **cash‑flow services** for SMEs.
- **Alerting and monitoring** tools for fraud and treasury.
- **New UX patterns** around instant confirmation and reconciliation.

---

#### 3. Cloud and SaaS Adoption in Enterprises

Trend type:

- Technological + competitive.

Effects:

- More data available in **standardized, accessible systems**.
- Easier integration across tools using APIs.

Opportunities:

- Cross‑tool analytics and automation.
- Category‑specific SaaS for under‑served industries.

In banks:

- Careful adoption of cloud with **strong risk controls** opens:
  - scalable analytics,
  - modern infrastructure for digital products.

---

#### 4. Rise of Embedded Finance

Trend type:

- Technological + competitive.

Effects:

- Financial services (payments, lending, accounts) show up **inside non‑financial apps**:
  - retail platforms,
  - SaaS products,
  - gig economy platforms.

Opportunities:

- For banks:
  - become **“as‑a‑service” providers** (Banking as a Service).
- For platforms:
  - deepen engagement with financial products tied to workflows.

---

### C. Connecting Trends to Opportunities (Beginner Model)

You can think of a simple 3‑step pattern:

1. **Observe a trend**
   - e.g., “more SMEs are selling online and globally.”

2. **Ask what new problems or jobs emerge**
   - “SMEs now deal with multi‑currency payments, FX risk, and cross‑border compliance.”

3. **Form opportunity hypotheses**
   - “We could help SMEs manage cross‑border cash‑flow and FX exposure automatically using our transaction and FX data.”

In practice:

- Combine **trends (top‑down)** with **data and research (bottom‑up)**:
  - If both point to similar spaces, you likely have a strong opportunity.

---

### D. How to Track Trends Systematically

For a discovery practice:

- Assign someone (often Strategy/PM) to:
  - maintain a **“trends log”**:
    - short notes on relevant technological, regulatory, and competitive changes,
    - links to reports, articles, and internal discussions.
- Review this log during:
  - **discovery planning** (see `03-workflow.md`, Stage 1),
  - **opportunity ideation** workshops.

This ensures you don’t rely on **random news** but treat trends as a **structured input** to discovery.
