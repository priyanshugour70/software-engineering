## 14 – Salary Benchmarks (High-Level, Role-Based View)

This file gives a **conceptual overview** of how market opportunity discovery skills influence compensation across roles.  
It is **not** a source of exact numbers (these vary heavily by country, company, and time) but a **guide to relative positioning**.

We cover:

- A. How discovery skills impact compensation
- B. Relative salary levels by role (conceptual)
- C. Factors that influence pay ranges

---

### A. How Discovery Skills Impact Compensation

1. **Closer to strategy, higher leverage**  
   Roles that **shape what gets built and why** (vs. just delivering tasks) usually:
   - have **higher impact** on company outcomes,
   - and therefore **command higher compensation**.

2. **Evidence-based decisions are valued**  
   People who can:
   - identify high‑value opportunities,
   - kill weak ones early,
   - and support decisions with data and research,
   reduce wasted investment and unlock growth → this is highly valued.

3. **Cross-functional influence**  
   Discovery requires influencing:
   - product,
   - engineering,
   - business,
   - risk/compliance,
   - operations.
   Strong influence and communication skills are often associated with **senior, better‑paid roles**.

---

### B. Relative Salary Levels by Role (Conceptual)

Below is a **rough hierarchy** (from lower to higher typical compensation) in most markets.  
Exact numbers depend on region, company size, industry, and seniority.

1. **Junior Product Analyst / Associate PM / UX Researcher (Junior)**
   - Entry‑level roles supporting discovery:
     - running analyses,
     - assisting with research,
     - helping write documents.
   - Compensation often **similar to other junior roles** in tech or business.

2. **Product Manager / Mid‑Level UX Researcher / Business Analyst**
   - Owns discovery for a **narrower scope** (one product or feature area).
   - Can run **end‑to‑end discovery cycles** with support.
   - Typically **mid‑level compensation**, higher than junior IC roles.

3. **Senior Product Manager / Lead UX Researcher / Senior Strategy Analyst**
   - Significant responsibility for:
     - defining and prioritizing opportunities,
     - influencing roadmap and investment.
   - Often **higher compensation band** due to impact and autonomy.

4. **Product Lead / Group PM / Product Director / Strategy Manager**
   - Oversees discovery across **multiple teams or product lines**.
   - Shapes **investment themes and portfolio decisions**.
   - Compensation typically includes:
     - higher base salary,
     - performance bonuses,
     - sometimes equity (especially in tech/startups).

5. **Head of Product / VP Product / Head of Strategy / Innovation Lead**
   - Responsible for:
     - **overall opportunity pipeline** for a business unit or company,
     - aligning with executive and board‑level strategy.
   - Among the **best compensated non‑executive roles** in product/strategy.

6. **CPO (Chief Product Officer) / CSO (Chief Strategy Officer) / Business Unit Head**
   - Own P&L and long‑term strategy.
   - Market opportunity discovery is core to their mandate.
   - Compensation typically includes:
     - executive‑level pay,
     - significant bonus components,
     - and often equity or long‑term incentive plans.

---

### C. Factors That Influence Pay Ranges

1. **Region and City**
   - Major tech hubs and financial centers tend to offer **higher salaries** and **more equity**.
   - Emerging markets may have lower nominal salaries but could offer:
     - faster promotion paths,
     - broader responsibilities.

2. **Company Size & Stage**
   - **Startups and scale‑ups**:
     - may offer lower base pay but higher **equity** and broader scope.
   - **Large enterprises / banks**:
     - often offer more **stable, structured compensation**,
     - strong benefits,
     - but sometimes narrower role definitions.

3. **Industry**
   - Industries where wrong bets are **very expensive** (e.g., banking, telecoms, infrastructure) often:
     - value strong discovery and strategy more,
     - but may be more conservative with equity.

4. **Scope and P&L Responsibility**
   - Roles that own **bigger revenue or profit numbers** tend to be in **higher bands**.
   - A senior PM for a small feature vs. a Group PM for a multi‑million revenue line will differ significantly.

5. **Depth of Expertise**
   - Specialists in **regulated domains** (e.g., payments, capital markets, open banking) who also have strong discovery skills can be especially valuable.

---

### D. Using This Information as a Beginner

If you are early in your career:

- Focus first on **building strong discovery skills**:
  - customer research,
  - analytics,
  - structured thinking,
  - opportunity framing.
- Then:
  - move into roles with **increasing ownership of opportunity pipelines**:
    - from assisting,
    - to leading a domain,
    - to shaping portfolios.

When discussing compensation:

- Highlight:
  - specific opportunities you identified,
  - decisions you influenced,
  - and measurable impacts (if available).

This shows that you are not just “doing tasks” but **creating and shaping value**, which is what higher‑level compensation is usually tied to.
