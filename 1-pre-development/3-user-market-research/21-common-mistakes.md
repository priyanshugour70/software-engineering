## 21 – Common Mistakes in User & Market Research

This file highlights **frequent pitfalls** in research and how to avoid them.

We group mistakes into:

- A. Mindset errors
- B. Process errors
- C. Collaboration and communication errors

---

### A. Mindset Errors

#### 1. Research as Validation Only

Pattern:

- doing research only to **prove a chosen solution is right**.

Risk:

- ignoring evidence that contradicts assumptions,
- missing better alternatives.

Fix:

- explicitly look for:
  - disconfirming evidence,
  - surprises,
  - edge cases.

---

#### 2. Confusing Opinions with Insights

Pattern:

- calling:
  - a stakeholder comment,
  - or a single user quote,
an “insight”.

Fix:

- treat:
  - opinions as hypotheses,
  - only call something an insight when:
    - you see a pattern,
    - it is grounded in multiple sources or strong evidence.

---

#### 3. Overgeneralizing from Tiny Samples

Pattern:

- “Three users said X; therefore, **all** users are like this.”

Fix:

- be explicit:
  - “In this small sample of N users, we observed…”
- use:
  - surveys or analytics to understand how widespread patterns are.

---

### B. Process Errors

#### 4. Vague Goals & Questions

Pattern:

- starting research with:
  - “let’s learn about our users,”
  without concrete questions or decisions.

Fix:

- use:
  - `04-research-questions-and-hypotheses.md`,
  - clarify decisions research should inform.

---

#### 5. Poor Participant Fit

Pattern:

- interviewing:
  - people who do not match the target segment,
  - or who haven’t experienced the relevant journey.

Fix:

- define recruitment criteria clearly (`06-recruitment-and-sampling.md`),
- coordinate with:
  - CRM,
  - sales,
  - support.

---

#### 6. Skipping Synthesis

Pattern:

- running:
  - many interviews or surveys,
  - but never organizing the data into:
    - themes,
    - journeys,
    - decisions.

Fix:

- allocate **dedicated time** for synthesis,
- use:
  - affinity mapping,
  - journey mapping,
  - insight statements.

---

#### 7. No Link to Problem or Strategy

Pattern:

- research outputs do not reference:
  - problem statements,
  - strategic goals.

Risk:

- hard to justify next steps,
- findings get ignored.

Fix:

- always link:
  - each study to a specific problem,
  - each insight to implications for that problem or strategy.

---

### C. Collaboration & Communication Errors

#### 8. Keeping Research “In a Corner”

Pattern:

- research done by a small group,
  - with little involvement from:
    - PMs,
    - engineers,
    - business stakeholders.

Risk:

- low buy‑in,
- misinterpretation,
- underuse of insights.

Fix:

- invite stakeholders to:
  - planning,
  - observation,
  - synthesis,
  - readouts.

---

#### 9. Overloaded or Confusing Reports

Pattern:

- giant decks:
  - full of screenshots and quotes,
  - but no clear messages or implications.

Fix:

- start with:
  - concise summary of:
    - key findings,
    - evidence,
    - “so what”.
- keep detailed data in appendices or repositories.

---

#### 10. Not Updating Downstream Artifacts

Pattern:

- research is done,
  - but:
    - Problem Definitions,
    - strategies,
    - requirements,
  remain unchanged.

Risk:

- research becomes:
  - a ritual,
  - not a driver of change.

Fix:

- after each major study, explicitly:
  - review and update:
    - problem statements,
    - requirements,
    - roadmap items.
