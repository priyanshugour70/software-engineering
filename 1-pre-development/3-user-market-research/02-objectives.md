## 02 – Objectives of User & Market Research

This file explains **what success looks like** for the User & Market Research phase.

---

### 1. Primary Objective (One Sentence)

The main objective is to produce **clear, trustworthy, and decision‑ready insights** about users and the market that:

- validate or refine **problem definitions**,
- uncover **needs, behaviors, and constraints**,
- and directly inform **product strategy, requirements, and priorities**.

---

### 2. Key Outcomes You Should Achieve

#### 2.1 Validated or Refined Problem Statements

Research should:

- confirm whether current problem statements:
  - match user reality,
  - capture the most painful aspects,
  - have the right scope.
- or lead you to:
  - sharpen,
  - split,
  - or reframe problems.

Outcome:

- Problems are no longer just **internal guesses**; they are **backed by user and market evidence**.

---

#### 2.2 Deep Understanding of Users and Stakeholders

You should gain:

- clear understanding of:
  - user goals and jobs‑to‑be‑done,
  - behaviors and decision flows,
  - mental models and language,
  - constraints and workarounds.

Outcome artifacts:

- personas or segment profiles,
- journey maps,
- key behavioral patterns.

---

#### 2.3 Evidence of Pain Points, Needs, and Opportunities

Research should:

- identify **where and why** users struggle:
  - friction points,
  - confusion,
  - unmet needs,
  - risks and fears.
- highlight:
  - which pains are severe and frequent,
  - which are minor.

Outcome:

- a **prioritized view of pain points and opportunities**, not just a long list.

---

#### 2.4 Understanding of Competitive & Market Context

You should understand:

- what competitors offer:
  - features and UX,
  - messaging and pricing,
  - strengths and gaps.
- what is **table‑stakes vs differentiating** in your market.

Outcome:

- insight into:
  - where you must **match the market**,
  - where you can **differentiate**,
  - where the market is **shaping user expectations**.

---

#### 2.5 Actionable Insights, Not Just Data

Beyond raw data, you should have:

- **insights**:
  - concise statements of what you learned and why it matters,
  - each traceable to supporting evidence.
- **implications**:
  - how this should change:
    - problem definitions,
    - product strategy,
    - requirements,
    - risk assessments.

Outcome:

- stakeholders can answer:
  - “So what?”  
  - “What should we do differently because of this research?”

---

### 3. Secondary Objectives

#### 3.1 Build Shared Empathy Across the Team

Research should help:

- engineers,
- designers,
- PMs,
- business stakeholders,

see and feel:

- how users experience products and processes.

Outcome:

- Build **shared mental models** of users,
- reduce reliance on stereotypes or assumptions.

---

#### 3.2 Create Reusable Knowledge Assets

Instead of single‑use decks, research should:

- create assets that can be reused:
  - personas,
  - journey maps,
  - insight repositories,
  - competitive libraries.

Outcome:

- future projects can **build on** past research instead of re‑starting from scratch.

---

#### 3.3 Improve the Organization’s Research Maturity

Each research effort is an opportunity to:

- improve:
  - methods,
  - documentation,
  - ethical practices,
  - collaboration patterns.

Outcome:

- better research questions,
- more efficient execution,
- higher trust in research outputs over time.

---

### 4. What “Good” vs “Bad” Outcomes Look Like

#### 4.1 “Good” Outcomes

- Problem statements have been:
  - refined with real examples and metrics.
- Team members:
  - can describe users and key journeys confidently and consistently.
- Decisions:
  - can be traced back to specific findings and evidence.
- Insights:
  - are summarized clearly,
  - and directly linked to implications for product and business.

#### 4.2 “Bad” Outcomes

- A long report nobody reads.
- Vague personas with no clear link to decisions.
- Research that:
  - confirms what everyone already believed,
  - or is too generic to change anything.
- No explicit changes to:
  - problem statements,
  - priorities,
  - or requirements.

---

### 5. Checklist: Did We Meet the Objectives?

Before considering your research “done” for a phase, ask:

- **Problem validation**
  - [ ] Did the research confirm or refine our problem statements?
  - [ ] Are changes documented and agreed with stakeholders?
- **User understanding**
  - [ ] Do we have clear descriptions of key user segments?
  - [ ] Do we understand their goals, workflows, and pain points?
- **Market & competition**
  - [ ] Do we know how competitors address similar problems?
  - [ ] Can we explain where we must match vs differentiate?
- **Insights & decisions**
  - [ ] Have we summarized key insights and linked them to decisions?
  - [ ] Have these insights led to concrete changes (e.g., updated requirements, roadmap adjustments)?
- **Reuse**
  - [ ] Are artifacts stored and tagged so they can be reused later?
  - [ ] Have we shared findings with relevant teams?

If many boxes are unchecked, iterate or tighten the research and synthesis before moving on.
