## 18 – Case Studies: End‑to‑End User & Market Research

These constructed case studies show how User & Market Research plays out from **plan to impact**, with an emphasis on banking/enterprise contexts.

We cover:

- Case Study 1: SME Digital Onboarding Research
- Case Study 2: Cash‑Flow Management for SMEs

---

### Case Study 1 – SME Digital Onboarding Research

#### Context

Problem Definition identified:

> “Existing SME customers in Country X frequently abandon online onboarding at the document upload step.”

Goal:

- understand:
  - why this happens,
  - what SMEs need,
  - what would change behavior.

#### Stage 1 – Intake & Alignment

- Decisions:
  - which aspects of onboarding to redesign first,
  - how to prioritize investments (content vs flows vs automation).
- Key questions:
  - What makes SMEs stop or switch to branch?
  - How do they prepare documents?
  - How do they feel about uploading sensitive information?

#### Stage 2 – Plan & Design

Plan:

- Methods:
  - 12 SME owner interviews,
  - 4 contextual sessions with branch/ops staff,
  - analysis of onboarding funnel,
  - competitor flow review for 3 banks/fintechs.
- Participants:
  - SMEs who:
    - tried online onboarding in the last 6 months,
    - completed in branch after starting online,
    - abandoned entirely.

#### Stage 3 – Recruitment & Data Prep

- Work with:
  - CRM and relationship managers to invite SMEs.
- Respect:
  - privacy and consent requirements.
- Data analyst:
  - prepares funnels and metrics segmented by:
    - business size,
    - industry,
    - device type.

#### Stage 4 – Data Collection

Interviews:

- SME stories reveal:
  - confusion about document types,
  - fear of uploading sensitive files without clear security assurances,
  - desire to “have everything ready” before starting.

Context sessions:

- branch staff and ops explain:
  - many SMEs come in with incomplete sets,
  - staff often tell them:
    - “It’s easier if you come in, we will help you.”  
    reinforcing branch behavior.

Analytics:

- confirm:
  - 60% drop‑off at document step,
  - mobile users have slightly higher drop‑off than desktop.

Competitor review:

- competitors:
  - show checklists **before** starting,
  - group documents into understandable categories,
  - use clear security messaging.

#### Stage 5 – Synthesis & Modeling

Outputs:

- segments/personas:
  - “Organized Owner” vs “Overwhelmed Owner”.
- journey map:
  - shows:
    - research and prep,
    - attempt online,
    - confusion at docs,
    - branch visit or giving up.
- insights:
  - SMEs want a **sense of control** and **preparedness**.
  - security messaging is crucial for upload trust.
  - current portal assumes they’re ready with all docs, which is often false.

#### Stage 6 – Readout & Impact

Readout highlights:

- 4–5 key insights, each with:
  - quotes,
  - funnel data,
  - screenshots.
- implications:
  - need for:
    - pre‑start document checklist,
    - simplified categories and examples,
    - visible security assurances,
    - mid‑flow save & resume.

Impact:

- Problem definition updated to emphasize:
  - trust and preparedness, not just speed.
- Requirements:
  - “must include pre‑onboarding checklist page”,
  - “clear security banner and messaging at upload.”

Later:

- follow‑up metrics show:
  - reduced abandonment,
  - fewer doc‑related tickets.

### Case Study 2 – Cash‑Flow Management for SMEs

#### Context

Opportunity Discovery suggested:

> “Help SMEs manage short‑term cash‑flow using bank data.”

Problem Definition framed:

> “SMEs struggle to see whether they will have enough cash to cover upcoming obligations, leading to stress and sub‑optimal decisions.”

Research goal:

- understand how SMEs:
  - currently manage cash‑flow,
  - make decisions,
  - use tools and advisors.

#### Stage 1 – Intake & Alignment

Decisions to support:

- whether to:
  - build an in‑bank cash‑flow forecasting feature,
  - partner with external tools,
  - or deprioritize.

Key questions:

- How do SMEs currently monitor cash‑flow?
- Which tools do they trust (spreadsheets, accounting software, advisors)?
- What information do they lack from the bank?

#### Stage 2 – Plan & Design

Plan:

- 15 SME interviews across industries,
- 5 accountant interviews,
- 3 internal interviews with relationship managers,
- analytics review:
  - usage of existing reporting features,
  - export behavior.
- desk research:
  - competitive landscape of SME finance tools.

#### Stage 3 – Recruitment & Data Prep

Recruitment:

- CRM identifies SMEs with:
  - frequent large outbound payments,
  - existing lines of credit.
- Relationship managers:
  - help introduce the study.

Data:

- analytics shows:
  - heavy use of CSV exports,
  - low use of in‑portal reporting.

#### Stage 4 – Data Collection

SME interviews:

- reveal:
  - spreadsheets and accounting tools are central,
  - many manually project cash‑flow,
  - they want:
    - clear visibility of upcoming obligations,
    - simulations (“if I pay X now, what happens?”).

Accountant interviews:

- show:
  - accountants:
    - often advise on cash‑flow,
    - log into bank portals on behalf of clients,
  - desire:
    - multi‑client dashboards,
    - exportable and reconcilable data.

Internal stakeholders:

- relationship managers:
  - confirm:
    - cash‑flow is a frequent discussion topic,
    - they would use better tools to advise clients.

Competitor analysis:

- some fintechs:
  - offer basic cash‑flow visuals,
  - but often require manual configuration.

#### Stage 5 – Synthesis & Modeling

Outputs:

- personas:
  - “Hands‑On Owner”
  - “Delegator (relies on accountant)”
  - “Numbers‑Lite Owner” (trusts summaries over details).
- service blueprint:
  - showing how:
    - owner,
    - accountant,
    - bank systems interact.
- insights:
  - SME pain is less about:
    - *not having any data*,
  - and more about:
    - data being fragmented and hard to interpret.
  - opportunities in:
    - connecting bank and accounting data,
    - giving simple, scenario‑based views.

#### Stage 6 – Readout & Impact

Readout:

- shows:
  - qualitative stories,
  - analytics of export rates,
  - gaps in current reporting tools.

Implications:

- confirm:
  - strong user need,
  - multiple stakeholder roles.
- recommended direction:
  - start with:
    - improved reporting and export,
    - basic projections,
  - explore:
    - deeper integration with accounting platforms later.

Impact:

- informs:
  - strategy document for SME “Financial Health” suite,
  - roadmap:
    - initial reporting enhancements,
    - followed by pilot of simple cash‑flow views.
