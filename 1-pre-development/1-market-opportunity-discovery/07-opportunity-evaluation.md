## 07 – Opportunity Evaluation & Prioritization

This file explains **how to assess and compare opportunities** so you can decide where to invest next.

We will cover:

- A. Evaluation principles
- B. Core criteria (with beginner‑friendly definitions)
- C. Simple scoring model (1–5 scale)
- D. Visual tools (matrices and portfolios)
- E. Example evaluation of 3 opportunities

---

### A. Evaluation Principles

1. **Compare, don’t perfect**  
   Evaluation is about **relative strength**, not finding “the truth”. You want to know which opportunities are **stronger or weaker** than others.

2. **Make assumptions explicit**  
   Scores are based on **assumptions and imperfect data**. Write these down so they can be tested later.

3. **Include multiple perspectives**  
   Involve product, business, engineering, risk/compliance, and operations. Each sees **different risks and value**.

4. **Keep it simple**  
   A short set of criteria with 1–5 scores is usually enough. Overly complex models give a **false sense of precision**.

5. **Use evaluation to drive conversation**  
   The goal is **aligned decisions**, not a spreadsheet for its own sake.

---

### B. Core Evaluation Criteria

Here are commonly used criteria. You can adapt or extend them for your context.

#### 1. Strategic Fit

**Question**: How well does this opportunity support our **company and product strategy**, OKRs, and long‑term direction?

Signals of **high strategic fit**:

- Directly contributes to top‑level goals (e.g., “grow SME lending”, “increase digital adoption”).
- Strengthens core positioning (e.g., “the SME relationship bank”, “the most trusted payments provider”).

---

#### 2. Customer / User Value

**Question**: How much **real, meaningful value** will this create for customers or users if we solve it well?

Consider:

- Pain severity (how big is the problem?).
- Frequency (how often does it happen?).
- Breadth (how many customers are affected?).

---

#### 3. Business Impact (Value Potential)

**Question**: What is the potential **financial or strategic impact** for the organization?

Examples:

- Revenue growth (new sales, upsell, cross‑sell).
- Cost savings (opex reduction, automation).
- Risk reduction (e.g., lower default rates, fewer fraud losses).
- Regulatory impact (avoiding fines, enabling new licensed activities).

At discovery time, rough, **order‑of‑magnitude estimates** are enough (e.g., “small / medium / large” or 1–5).

---

#### 4. Feasibility (Technical + Operational)

**Question**: Can we **realistically deliver and operate** this opportunity with our current or reasonably attainable capabilities?

Consider:

- Technical complexity (new platforms, integrations, data needs).
- Dependencies on other teams or vendors.
- Operational changes (new processes, training, staffing).

At this stage you only need a **high‑level view**, not a full feasibility study.

---

#### 5. Time Sensitivity / Urgency

**Question**: How important is **timing** for this opportunity?

Examples:

- Regulatory deadlines.
- Competitors already moving quickly.
- Temporary windows (e.g., pandemic‑driven behaviors, government programs).

---

#### 6. Risk and Uncertainty

**Question**: How many things are **unknown or risky**, and how severe are they?

Types of risk:

- Market risk – maybe customers **don’t actually want** this.
- Execution risk – maybe we **can’t build or operate** it effectively.
- Regulatory risk – approvals unclear, high compliance burden.

Higher risk doesn’t mean “don’t do it”; it means “handle with care and validation”.

### C. Simple Scoring Model (1–5)

You can create a basic scoring sheet with criteria as columns and opportunities as rows.

#### Step 1 – Define a scale

For each criterion, use:

- **1** – Very low / poor
- **2** – Low
- **3** – Medium
- **4** – High
- **5** – Very high / excellent

Give a **short textual anchor** for each score for clarity (example below).

#### Step 2 – Score in a workshop

1. Explain the criteria to all participants.
2. Review each opportunity brief together.
3. Ask everyone to **score individually** first to avoid group bias.
4. Reveal scores and discuss differences.
5. Agree on a **final score per criterion** and note reasons.

#### Step 3 – Calculate total / weighted scores

- Simple approach: **sum all criteria** → total score per opportunity.
- Advanced approach: apply **weights** (e.g., strategic fit and customer value count double).

Keep the math transparent so stakeholders trust it.

### D. Visual Tools: Matrices and Portfolios

Numbers alone can be hard to interpret. Visualizations help.

#### 1. Value vs Feasibility Matrix (2×2)

- X‑axis: **Value** (customer + business impact).
- Y‑axis: **Feasibility** (how easy it is to implement and operate).

Quadrants:

- Top‑right: **High value / high feasibility** – best near‑term bets.
- Top‑left: high value / low feasibility – long‑term bets or require Enabler work.
- Bottom‑right: low value / high feasibility – “quick wins” but watch out for distraction.
- Bottom‑left: low value / low feasibility – usually avoid.

Plot each opportunity as a dot based on your scores.

---

#### 2. Time Sensitivity vs Strategic Fit

Another useful view:

- X‑axis: **Strategic fit**.
- Y‑axis: **Urgency** (time sensitivity).

This helps surface:

- Opportunities that are **urgent and strategic** (don’t delay).
- Items that are strategic but **not urgent** (plan thoughtfully).
- Items that are urgent but **not strategic** (watch for distractions).

### E. Example: Evaluating Three Opportunities

Imagine three candidate opportunities in SME banking:

1. **Digital SME credit line** (fast approvals).
2. **Cash‑flow forecasting dashboard**.
3. **Gamified financial education app** for SME owners.

**Rough scoring (1–5, equal weights)**

| Criterion           | SME Credit Line | Cash‑Flow Dashboard | Education App |
|--------------------|-----------------|---------------------|---------------|
| Strategic fit      | 5               | 4                   | 2             |
| Customer value     | 5               | 4                   | 3             |
| Business impact    | 5               | 4                   | 2             |
| Feasibility        | 3               | 4                   | 4             |
| Time sensitivity   | 4               | 3                   | 2             |
| Risk / uncertainty | 3               | 3                   | 4             |

If you sum (assuming higher risk score means “more attractive” is **not** the case, you might invert it or treat it separately), you might see:

- SME Credit Line and Cash‑Flow Dashboard as **top priorities**.
- Education App as **nice to have / longer term**.

You would then:

- Move **1 and 2** into Problem Definition and deeper research.
- Maybe **park 3** or tie it to a later phase of the roadmap.

---

### F. Documenting Decisions

Whenever you evaluate:

- Capture:
  - Scores per criterion,
  - **Why** you gave that score,
  - Key assumptions,
  - Decision (e.g., move forward, park, discard).
- Store this with opportunity briefs so future teams can see:
  - The **history and rationale**,
  - Whether something should be **re‑evaluated** later as conditions change.
