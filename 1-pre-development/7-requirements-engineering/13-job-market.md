## 13 – Job Market for Requirements Engineering

This file describes the **job market context** for roles that rely heavily on Requirements Engineering skills.

It focuses on:

- which roles and industries value these skills,
- how they fit into product and delivery careers.

---

### 1. Roles with Strong Requirements Focus

Examples:

- Business Analyst / Requirements Engineer,
- Product Manager / Product Owner (especially in enterprise),
- Systems Analyst,
- Functional Consultant (e.g., in ERP, CRM),
- QA Lead / Test Analyst in requirements‑heavy environments.

In many modern organizations:

- responsibilities may be split or blended between:
  - PM/PO,
  - BA,
  - tech leads,
  - and QA.

---

### 2. Industries & Domains

High demand for strong Requirements Engineering exists in:

- **Financial services**:
  - banking, lending, payments, insurance, wealth.
- **Enterprise software**:
  - CRM, ERP, HRIS, analytics.
- **Telecoms & Utilities**:
  - large customer and operations systems.
- **Government & Public Sector**:
  - large programs with strong compliance needs.

In these contexts:

- complexity and regulation:
  - make clear, traceable requirements especially important.

---

### 3. Career Path Patterns

#### 3.1 Starting from Business Analysis / Requirements

- Many professionals:
  - start as Business Analysts or Requirements Engineers.
- Over time, they:
  - deepen domain knowledge,
  - improve facilitation and stakeholder skills,
  - potentially move toward:
    - Product Management,
    - Solution Architecture,
    - Delivery/Program Management.

#### 3.2 Starting from Product or Engineering

- PMs and engineers who:
  - naturally gravitate toward:
    - clarifying requirements,
    - shaping NFRs,
    - documenting decisions,
  - often move into:
    - more strategic product roles,
    - solution or systems architect roles,
    - or technical leadership.

---

### 4. Signals Employers Look For

Examples of strong signals:

- clear stories of:
  - taking a fuzzy problem,
  - leading refinement and requirements work,
  - and delivering a usable, compliant solution.
- ability to:
  - explain complex rules clearly,
  - manage stakeholders with conflicting views,
  - align requirements across teams.
- evidence of:
  - work in regulated or complex domains,
  - handling NFRs and traceability,
  - interacting with risk/compliance/ops.

In interviews:

- expect:
  - scenario questions about:
    - ambiguous requirements,
    - changing scope,
    - regulatory constraints,
    - and collaboration across roles.

---

### 5. Demand in Enterprises/Banks

Drivers:

- digital transformation of:
  - core journeys (e.g., lending, onboarding),
  - legacy platform replacements,
  - regulatory change programs.

Banks and large FS organizations:

- often need people who can:
  - **bridge**:
    - business,
    - risk,
    - and technology,
  - produce:
    - clear PRDs and NFRs,
    - robust traceability,
    - audit‑ready documentation.

Titles might include:

- “Business Analyst – Lending,”
- “Digital Product Owner,”
- “Functional Analyst – Core Banking,”
- “Product Lead – Regulatory Change.”

---

### 6. Building Experience if You Are New

If you are early in your career:

- ask to:
  - help maintain PRDs,
  - write or improve stories and acceptance criteria,
  - document NFRs and traceability tables.
- volunteer to:
  - run or co‑facilitate refinement sessions,
  - capture and share decisions from requirements meetings.

Examples to collect:

- before/after snapshots of:
  - messy requirements vs clarified versions,
  - how that improved delivery and quality.

These make strong portfolio and interview stories.
