## 16 – Startup vs Enterprise RFC Practices

This file compares how RFCs tend to work in **startups/scaleups** vs **large enterprises/banks**, and how to adapt practices.

---

### 1. Startups / Scaleups

Characteristics:

- fewer people and systems,
- faster change,
- less formal governance.

RFC‑like practices:

- may appear as:
  - short design docs,
  - GitHub design PRs,
  - Google Docs shared for feedback.

Good patterns:

- keep documents:
  - short but structured,
  - easy to discuss and iterate on.
- focus on:
  - main trade‑offs,
  - risk awareness,
  - impact on future flexibility.

Pitfalls:

- skipping written proposals entirely:
  - decisions live only in chat and memory,
  - hard to scale.

---

### 2. Enterprises / Banks

Characteristics:

- many teams and systems,
- strong regulatory and risk oversight,
- architecture and security governance processes.

RFC practices:

- more formal:
  - template use,
  - review and approval flows,
  - traceability expectations.

Pitfalls:

- over‑engineered RFC processes:
  - long documents,
  - slow cycles,
  - perceived bureaucracy.

Better approach:

- strong **content and structure**,
- but:
  - keep RFCs focused,
  - use appendices and links for deep detail.

---

### 3. Hybrid Situations

Many large organisations:

- run “digital ventures” or innovation teams,
- collaborating with core/legacy teams.

RFC implications:

- innovation teams:
  - may use lighter‑weight RFCs, but
  - must still satisfy:
    - core security,
    - risk,
    - and compliance expectations.

Best practice:

- clearly define:
  - when full enterprise RFC + governance is required,
  - when lighter processes are acceptable.

---

### 4. Adapting RFC Practices

If you’re in a startup:

- introduce:
  - minimal RFC templates,
  - but don’t over‑burden small teams.

If you’re in an enterprise:

- streamline:
  - templates,
  - approval paths,
  - avoid unnecessary repetition.

In both:

- ensure:
  - key decisions are captured,
  - collaboration across roles happens,
  - risk is acknowledged and managed.
