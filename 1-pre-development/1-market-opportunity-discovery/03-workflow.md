## 03 – Workflow: Step‑by‑Step Process

This file explains a **practical, repeatable workflow** for Market Opportunity Discovery.  
You can run it as a **full cycle (e.g., 4–8 weeks)** or as a **lightweight ongoing process**.

We will describe:

- A. **High‑level stages**
- B. **Detailed step‑by‑step activities**
- C. **Example timelines (startup vs enterprise)**
- D. **Beginner tips at each step**

---

### A. High‑Level Stages

We divide the workflow into 6 stages:

1. **Prepare & Scope**
2. **Collect Signals**
3. **Synthesize Themes**
4. **Shape Opportunities**
5. **Evaluate & Prioritize**
6. **Share & Hand Over**

You do not have to follow this linearly; you can loop back as you learn more. But this structure is a good **default for beginners**.

---

### B. Detailed Step‑by‑Step Activities

#### Stage 1 – Prepare & Scope

**Goal**: Align on *why* you are doing discovery now and *where* you will look.

**Inputs**

- Company / product **strategy documents**
- Current **OKRs** or annual goals
- Existing **product portfolio and performance data**

**Steps**

1. **Clarify the strategic questions**
   - Example: “Where are the biggest digital growth opportunities in SME banking in the next 2 years?”
   - Example: “Which new use cases could significantly increase engagement in our consumer app?”
2. **Define initial scope**
   - Segments (e.g., SME vs retail vs corporate).
   - Geographies (e.g., EU vs APAC).
   - Product areas (e.g., lending, payments, advisory).
3. **Assemble the core discovery team**
   - Product, strategy, UX/research, data, plus SMEs (e.g., risk, compliance).
4. **Decide timebox and cadence**
   - Example: 6‑week sprint, with weekly check‑ins and a final readout.

**Beginner tip**  
Write down the **scope and questions** in a simple 1‑page brief. This keeps everyone aligned and prevents scope creep.

---

#### Stage 2 – Collect Signals

**Goal**: Gather **raw material** about customers, market, competition, and environment.

**Sources**

- Customer support tickets, NPS, interviews.
- Product analytics and business intelligence.
- Industry reports, analyst notes, competitor tracking.
- Regulatory roadmaps and central bank communications.

> See `05-research-methods.md` and `06-data-sources.md` for deep dives.

**Steps**

1. **Customer & internal data review**
   - Pull key metrics: growth, churn, NPS, funnel drop‑offs.
   - Identify areas of **strong or weak performance**.
2. **Voice of customer**
   - Scan support tickets and NPS verbatims for recurring pains.
   - Interview frontline staff (sales, relationship managers, call center).
3. **Market & competitor scan**
   - List main competitors; for each, note recent launches and positioning.
   - Collect external reports about your segment or industry.
4. **Regulatory & environmental review (for banks and regulated orgs)**
   - Identify upcoming or recent regulations and guidelines.
   - Note where regulation may **force change** or **enable new business models**.

**Beginner tip**  
Don’t worry yet about “good ideas”. **Just collect signals** and write them down in a structured way (spreadsheet, board, or doc).

---

#### Stage 3 – Synthesize Themes

**Goal**: Turn **scattered signals** into **coherent themes** and problem areas.

**Steps**

1. **Cluster signals**
   - Group similar signals (e.g., “onboarding friction”, “SME cash‑flow visibility”, “FX fees”).
   - Use a whiteboard or digital board (Miro, FigJam, etc.).
2. **Name themes**
   - Give each cluster a clear, customer‑oriented label:
     - “SMEs can’t see their future cash‑flow.”
     - “Young customers hate branch visits.”
3. **Describe themes in simple language**
   - Write 2–3 sentences describing what is happening and for whom.
4. **Identify gaps and questions**
   - What do we **not know** yet?
   - What might be **causing** these patterns?

**Beginner tip**  
Use color coding for different signal types: **green** (customer), **blue** (data), **orange** (competitor), **purple** (regulation). This helps you see if you’re over‑relying on one type.

---

#### Stage 4 – Shape Opportunities

**Goal**: Turn themes into **explicit opportunity hypotheses**.

**Steps**

1. **Select promising themes**
   - Pick themes that:
     - appear frequently in your signals,
     - are aligned with strategy,
     - feel “big enough” to matter.
2. **Draft opportunity hypotheses**
   - Use a simple sentence form:
     - “We believe that **[segment]** struggles with **[problem]**, and that if we help them achieve **[desired outcome]**, we will achieve **[business impact]**.”
3. **Create lightweight opportunity briefs**
   - For each hypothesis, fill a 1‑page template:
     - Who is affected?
     - What is their current journey?
     - What are the pains or missed gains?
     - How big might this be (roughly)?
     - Which signals support this?
     - What are key unknowns?
4. **Review with stakeholders**
   - Share briefs in short workshops.
   - Ask stakeholders to **challenge assumptions** and add missing context.

> Templates and examples in `10-documents.md` and `23-templates.md`.

**Beginner tip**  
Resist the urge to jump to **solution designs**. If you catch yourself saying “we should build X feature”, pause and reframe: “What opportunity is this trying to address?”

---

#### Stage 5 – Evaluate & Prioritize

**Goal**: Decide **which opportunities are worth deeper investment** now.

> Detailed evaluation models are in `07-opportunity-evaluation.md` and `09-metrics.md`.

**Steps**

1. **Define evaluation criteria**
   - Example criteria:
     - Strategic fit
     - Customer value
     - Revenue or impact potential
     - Feasibility (high‑level)
     - Risk level
     - Time sensitivity (urgency)
2. **Score each opportunity**
   - Use simple scales (e.g., 1–5) for each criterion.
   - Involve cross‑functional stakeholders in a scoring workshop.
3. **Discuss and adjust**
   - The scores are a **starting point**, not the final truth.
   - Talk through disagreements, adjust scores, and document rationale.
4. **Create a prioritized shortlist**
   - Identify:
     - Top 3–5 **high‑priority** opportunities.
     - Medium‑priority opportunities for **monitoring or later cycles**.
     - Low‑priority opportunities to **park or discard**.

**Beginner tip**  
Visualize the result as a **2×2**:

- X‑axis: value potential,
- Y‑axis: feasibility.

Place each opportunity as a dot. This helps stakeholders see trade‑offs.

---

#### Stage 6 – Share & Hand Over

**Goal**: Ensure the outputs are **understood, used, and owned** by downstream teams.

**Steps**

1. **Prepare a concise readout**
   - Problem/opportunity landscape (themes).
   - Top‑ranked opportunities and why they matter.
   - Key assumptions and uncertainties.
   - Recommended next steps for each top opportunity.
2. **Run stakeholder sessions**
   - Present to leadership, product councils, or portfolio boards.
   - Present to teams who might own next phases (research, product teams).
3. **Assign ownership**
   - For each top opportunity, assign:
     - a **lead** (e.g., product manager),
     - a **squad or working group** if available.
4. **Archive and link artifacts**
   - Store:
     - opportunity backlog,
     - briefs,
     - scoring sheets,
     - presentations.
   - Link them from your **documentation hub** so they can be revisited.

**Beginner tip**  
End each readout with **explicit decisions**:

- “We will move X and Y into Problem Definition this quarter.”
- “We will park Z and revisit in 6 months.”

Ambiguous outcomes kill momentum.

### C. Example Timelines

#### Example: 4‑Week Lightweight Cycle (Startup / Small Team)

- **Week 1** – Prepare & Scope, start collecting signals.
- **Week 2** – Continue signal collection, start synthesis.
- **Week 3** – Shape opportunities and draft briefs.
- **Week 4** – Evaluate, prioritize, and share.

You can run this **every quarter** to refresh your opportunity backlog.


#### Example: 6–8 Week Cycle (Enterprise / Bank)

- **Weeks 1–2**
  - Setup, stakeholder alignment, signal collection (market, internal, regulatory).
- **Weeks 3–4**
  - Deep synthesis workshops and opportunity shaping.
- **Weeks 5–6**
  - Evaluation workshops, prioritization sessions with leadership.
- **Weeks 7–8**
  - Final readouts, hand‑off to Problem Definition and research teams.

Because enterprises have more stakeholders and constraints, **allow more time** for alignment and approvals.

---

### D. Putting It into Practice (Beginner Scenario)

Imagine you are a **new PM** in a mid‑size bank asked to “find digital opportunities for SMEs”.

You could:

1. **Write a one‑page scope** (Stage 1).
2. **Collect signals**:
   - 10 interviews with relationship managers,
   - a data pull on SME product usage,
   - a scan of 5 key competitors and 2 industry reports (Stage 2).
3. **Synthesize**:
   - Identify 4–5 major themes (e.g., onboarding, credit access, cash‑flow visibility) (Stage 3).
4. **Shape**:
   - Turn each theme into a 1–page opportunity brief (Stage 4).
5. **Evaluate**:
   - Run a 2‑hour workshop with strategy, risk, and technology to score them (Stage 5).
6. **Share**:
   - Present a final deck with top 3 opportunities, recommended next steps, and owners (Stage 6).

This is enough to **kick‑start a structured discovery practice** in your organization.
