## 12 – Skills for Effective RFC Work

This file outlines the **skills** needed to author, review, and drive RFCs well.

---

### 1. Technical Depth & Systems Thinking

- understand:
  - system architectures,
  - data flows,
  - integration patterns.
- think in:
  - components and boundaries,
  - dependencies and failure modes,
  - long‑term evolution of the system.

Outcome:

- RFCs that are realistic and coherent, not hand‑wavy.

---

### 2. Product & Domain Understanding

- connect:
  - technical proposals to:
    - business goals,
    - user needs,
    - regulatory constraints.
- understand:
  - key domain concepts (e.g., lending, payments, KYC),
  - existing pain points and metrics.

Outcome:

- RFCs that solve the **right problems**, not just “interesting” technical ones.

---

### 3. Communication & Writing

- write:
  - clear, structured documents,
  - understandable by mixed audiences.
- explain:
  - trade‑offs,
  - risks,
  - and decisions plainly.
- summarise:
  - discussions,
  - feedback,
  - and changes.

Outcome:

- faster reviews,
- fewer misunderstandings,
- more reusable decision records.

---

### 4. Option Analysis & Decision‑Making

- identify:
  - realistic alternatives,
  - relevant comparison dimensions.
- evaluate:
  - value,
  - cost/effort,
  - risk and compliance,
  - operational impact.
- make:
  - clear recommendations with rationale.

Outcome:

- better trade‑offs,
- higher trust in technical decisions.

---

### 5. Collaboration & Facilitation

- bring together:
  - product,
  - engineering,
  - architecture,
  - security,
  - risk,
  - ops.
- run:
  - effective review sessions,
  - resolve conflicting views,
  - drive towards a decision.

Outcome:

- RFCs that genuinely reflect **joint understanding**, not just one view.

---

### 6. Risk & Compliance Awareness

- recognise:
  - security implications of design choices,
  - data protection and regulatory issues.
- speak:
  - enough of the language of risk and compliance to:
    - capture requirements,
    - interpret feedback,
    - document controls.

Outcome:

- RFCs that anticipate risk/compliance concerns,
- smoother security and risk assessments later.

---

### 7. Growth Path

For engineers/architects:

- early:
  - co‑author small RFCs,
  - focus on clear sections and diagrams.
- mid:
  - lead RFCs for your team or service,
  - engage more with security and risk.
- senior:
  - drive cross‑domain RFCs,
  - mentor others,
  - shape standards for RFC quality.
