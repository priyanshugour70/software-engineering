## 06 – Recruitment & Sampling

This file explains how to **find the right participants** for research and think about **sample quality**, especially in B2B/banking contexts.

We cover:

- A. Principles
- B. Defining who you need
- C. Recruitment channels
- D. Sample size guidance
- E. Special notes for enterprises and banks

---

### A. Principles

1. **Right people > big numbers** (for qualitative work)  
   - Talking to a small number of **highly relevant users** is better than many random respondents.

2. **Represent key segments**  
   - Ensure you include:
     - different user types,
     - different levels of experience,
     - different behaviors (e.g., adopters vs non‑adopters).

3. **Be explicit about who is *not* included**  
   - Document which segments are out of scope for this research round.

4. **Respect ethics and privacy**  
   - Follow consent, data handling, and recruitment policies (see `22-risks-ethics-and-compliance.md`).

---

### B. Defining Who You Need

Start from:

- problem statements,
- target segments (from `2-problem-definition/`),
- high‑value personas.

Define:

1. **Primary users**
   - e.g., SME owners using online banking for payments and loans.
2. **Secondary users/stakeholders**
   - e.g., accountants, relationship managers, back‑office staff.
3. **Behavioral criteria**
   - e.g., “has applied for a loan in last 12 months”,
   - “has abandoned onboarding recently”,
   - “heavy vs light users”.

Create a **participant profile table** with:

- must‑have traits,
- nice‑to‑have variations (industry, size, region, etc.).

### C. Recruitment Channels

#### 1. Existing Customers (B2B/B2C)

Sources:

- CRM lists,
- signup forms with “opt‑in to research”,
- in‑product prompts.

Pros:

- Highly relevant,
- real users of your product.

Cons:

- Requires:
  - alignment with sales/support,
  - careful privacy compliance.

---

#### 2. Internal Stakeholders & Frontline Staff

For:

- operations,
- call centers,
- relationship managers.

Pros:

- Deep knowledge of:
  - recurring issues,
  - workarounds,
  - edge cases.

Cons:

- May have their own biases (e.g., more exposure to extreme cases).

---

#### 3. External Panels / Agencies

Use when:

- you need:
  - specific demographics or firmographics,
  - quick survey responses.

Pros:

- Speed,
- access to hard‑to‑reach demographics.

Cons:

- For B2B and banking, panel quality varies,
- participants may not match your customers closely.

---

#### 4. Partnerships & Professional Networks

For B2B:

- industry associations,
- accountants and advisors,
- startup incubators, chambers of commerce.

Pros:

- Access to:
  - real SMEs,
  - decision‑makers.

Cons:

- Recruitment can be slower,
- requires relationship management.

### D. Sample Size Guidance (Qualitative)

There is no single “correct” number, but some practical guidelines:

#### 1. Exploratory Interviews

- 6–8 participants per **relatively homogeneous** segment often enough to see key patterns.
- If you have 2–3 distinct segments, aim for:
  - ~5–8 each (15–20 total) as a starting point.

#### 2. Usability Testing

- 5–8 users per **iteration** of a design is usually enough to catch major issues.
- If there are drastically different user types, test **a few from each**.

#### 3. Contextual Inquiry / Shadowing

- 4–8 sessions can already reveal many workflow patterns.

Always:

- be ready to **add participants** if new, conflicting patterns emerge.

---

### E. Sample Size Guidance (Quantitative)

For analytics:

- You often use:
  - full datasets (no sampling), or
  - large segments.

For surveys:

- Aim for:
  - hundreds of responses for broad patterns,
  - dozens for targeted, internal surveys.
- More important:
  - **who** you sample,
  - and whether they represent the **population you care about**.

---

### F. Special Notes for Enterprises and Banks

1. **Approvals and Permissions**
   - You may need:
     - approvals from legal/compliance,
     - coordination with account managers,
     - restrictions on which customers can be contacted.

2. **High‑value / Sensitive Clients**
   - Some segments (e.g., large corporates) require:
     - more formal outreach,
     - careful handling of incentives and confidentiality.

3. **Internal Participants**
   - Don’t neglect:
     - operations,
     - risk,
     - compliance,
     - internal support teams,
   as research subjects — they have valuable perspectives built from exposure to many customers.

4. **International Contexts**
   - Consider:
     - language and cultural differences,
     - regulatory variation across countries.
