## 27 – Glossary: RFC & Architecture Terms

This file defines key terms used across the **RFC / Technical Proposal** module.

---

### A–C

**ADR (Architecture Decision Record)**  
Short document capturing a single architecture decision, its context, and consequences, often linked to an RFC.

**Architecture Board / Design Authority**  
Formal group that reviews and approves significant architectural decisions and RFCs.

**Auditability**  
Ability to demonstrate what was decided, implemented, and executed, often required by regulators or internal audit.

**Baseline (RFC)**  
An agreed‑upon version of an RFC (usually Accepted) that acts as the reference decision until superseded.

**Compliance**  
Adhering to relevant laws, regulations, and internal policies.

---

### D–F

**Deployment Topology**  
How components are distributed across infrastructure (e.g., regions, zones, clusters).

**Design Doc**  
More detailed technical specification that often follows an RFC, focused on implementation details for a specific component or service.

**Disaster Recovery (DR)**  
Strategy and capabilities for restoring services and data after major failures.

**Feasibility Study**  
Earlier analysis (from pre‑development) that assesses whether a solution is technically, operationally, and economically viable.

**Flow / Sequence Diagram**  
Diagram showing step‑by‑step interactions between components or actors.

---

### G–I

**Governance (Architecture)**  
Structures and processes guiding how architectural decisions are proposed, reviewed, approved, and documented.

**Idempotency**  
Property of an operation that can be safely repeated without changing the result beyond the initial application; often relevant in payments and APIs.

**Impact Analysis**  
Assessment of what systems, components, requirements, and controls will be affected by a change.

---

### M–O

**Migration Plan**  
Plan describing how to move from current to target state, including phases, co‑existence, and cutover strategies.

**Monitoring & Observability**  
Practices and tools to understand system behaviour in production via metrics, logs, and traces.

**NFR (Non‑Functional Requirement)**  
Requirement about qualities like performance, availability, security, and operability, rather than specific behaviours.

**Operational Considerations**  
Aspects of running the system in production: deployment, monitoring, incident response, capacity, backups, etc.

---

### P–R

**POC (Proof of Concept)**  
Small, focused experiment to validate key technical assumptions or performance claims.

**PRD (Product Requirements Document)**  
Document that describes product context, goals, functional and non‑functional requirements; often an input to RFCs.

**RFC (Request for Comments)**  
Structured technical proposal document used to make and record significant architecture decisions.

**Risk Appetite**  
Amount and types of risk an organisation is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives.

---

### S–U

**Scope (of an RFC)**  
The systems, teams, and decisions an RFC is intended to cover, and what is explicitly out of scope.

**Security Review**  
Formal assessment of a proposed design or change by security specialists.

**SLA/SLO (Service Level Agreement / Objective)**  
Agreed or targeted measurements of service quality (e.g., uptime, latency).

**Superseded (RFC)**  
Status indicating that an RFC’s decisions have been replaced by a newer RFC.

**Traceability**  
Ability to connect RFCs to requirements, design docs, implementation items, and tests in both directions.

---

### V–Z

**Vendor Risk**  
Risk arising from using third‑party products or services, including availability, security, and compliance concerns.

**Version Control**  
System (e.g., Git) used to manage and track changes to code and documents, including RFCs.
