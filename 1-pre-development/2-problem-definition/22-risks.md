## 22 – Risks in Problem Definition

This file describes **risks related to Problem Definition itself** and to problems you select to focus on.

We group risks into:

- A. Product and market risks
- B. Execution and delivery risks
- C. Regulatory and compliance risks
- D. Organizational risks

---

### A. Product and Market Risks

#### 1. Solving the Wrong Problem

Risk:

- Misframing leads teams to:
  - optimize a low‑value area,
  - or address a symptom rather than a root cause.

Mitigation:

- Validate problem framing with:
  - data,
  - users,
  - cross‑functional stakeholders.

---

#### 2. Underestimating Problem Size or Urgency

Risk:

- Problem is more severe or urgent than defined, leading to:
  - under‑investment,
  - missed opportunities,
  - or late reactive scrambling.

Mitigation:

- Use robust metrics from `09-metrics.md`.
- Include:
  - both user impact,
  - and business impact,
in framing.

---

### B. Execution and Delivery Risks

#### 3. Overly Broad Problem Statements

Risk:

- Teams try to “fix everything at once”:
  - multi‑country,
  - multi‑segment,
  - multi‑channel.

Consequences:

- Projects stall,
- budgets explode,
- nothing finishes.

Mitigation:

- Define:
  - **phase 1 scope** explicitly,
  - later phases as separate future problems.

---

#### 4. Over‑Commitment Without Constraints Acknowledged

Risk:

- Problem statements ignore:
  - technical limitations,
  - resource caps,
  - operational capacity.

Consequences:

- Replanning,
- stakeholder frustration,
- loss of trust in planning.

Mitigation:

- Always include a **Constraints** section (see `10-documents.md` and `23-templates.md`).
- Involve:
  - engineering,
  - ops,
  - risk,
early.

---

### C. Regulatory and Compliance Risks

#### 5. Non-Compliant Problem Framing

Risk:

- Problem is defined in a way that implies:
  - ignoring or bending regulations.

Example:

- “Approve loans instantly for all SMEs” in a context where:
  - certain KYC/AML checks must be manual.

Mitigation:

- Include risk/compliance in Problem Definition.
- Document:
  - which parts can be automated,
  - which must remain manual.

---

#### 6. Ignoring Data Privacy and Security

Risk:

- Problem definitions that:
  - assume new data combinations,
  - or broader data use,
  without considering privacy/security rules.

Mitigation:

- Check:
  - data classification,
  - allowed uses,
  with security/privacy teams.
- Capture:
  - data constraints,
in the constraints register.

---

### D. Organizational Risks

#### 7. Misalignment Across Stakeholders

Risk:

- Different stakeholders agree to a problem document **in name only**, but:
  - hold different mental models.

Consequences:

- Conflicting expectations later,
- scope fights,
- friction in delivery.

Mitigation:

- Use:
  - collaborative workshops,
  - explicit readbacks,
to ensure **shared understanding**.

---

#### 8. Problem Definition Fatigue

Risk:

- People see Problem Definition as:
  - bureaucracy,
  - documentation for its own sake,
especially if:
  - problems are defined but not acted on.

Consequences:

- Less engagement,
- weaker inputs,
- superficial documents.

Mitigation:

- Ensure:
  - some defined problems **move forward** to real projects.
- Communicate:
  - where strong Problem Definition led to:
    - better outcomes,
    - less waste.

---

#### 9. Political Capture of Problem Framing

Risk:

- Senior individuals push their preferred framing:
  - ignoring data or other perspectives.

Consequences:

- Biased problem definitions,
- mis‑prioritized work.

Mitigation:

- Use:
  - transparent criteria (`07-problem-evaluation.md`),
  - shared evidence,
to ground discussions.

---

### Using This File

When drafting or reviewing a problem statement:

- Add a **Risks & Mitigations** section covering:
  - product/market risks,
  - execution risks,
  - regulatory risks,
  - organizational risks.

This makes your Problem Definition more:

- credible to stakeholders,
- resilient in the face of real‑world complexity.
