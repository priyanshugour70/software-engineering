## 08 – Tools for Business Case & ROI Analysis

This file describes **practical tools** used to build and communicate business cases.

We group tools into:

- A. Modeling & analysis
- B. Data access
- C. Documentation & collaboration
- D. Portfolio & decision support

---

### A. Modeling & Analysis

**Excel / Google Sheets / Internal Financial Tools**

Use for:

- building cash‑flow models,
- calculating ROI, NPV, IRR, payback,
- running scenario and sensitivity analyses.

Best practices:

- keep formulas simple and transparent,
- separate:
  - inputs (assumptions),
  - calculations,
  - outputs (summary metrics, charts).

---

### B. Data Access

**Analytics & BI Platforms**

Tools:

- Amplitude, Mixpanel (product analytics),
- Tableau, Power BI, Looker, Qlik, internal BI (business/ops analytics).

Use for:

- baselines:
  - volumes,
  - conversion rates,
  - time and cost metrics,
  - risk and loss statistics.

Collaborate with:

- data/BI teams to:
  - validate data sources,
  - ensure definitions match finance and risk.

---

### C. Documentation & Collaboration

**Confluence / Notion / SharePoint / Docs**

Use for:

- business case narratives,
- assumption logs,
- risk and dependency registers,
- decision records.

Best practices:

- link:
  - from the document to the financial model,
  - from portfolio tools back to the case.

**Presentation Tools (PowerPoint, Google Slides)**

Use for:

- summarizing:
  - key numbers,
  - scenarios,
  - recommendations,
for executive and committee presentations.

---

### D. Portfolio & Decision Support

**Portfolio Management Tools**

Tools:

- Planview, Clarity, Jira Align,
- in‑house portfolio dashboards.

Use for:

- comparing:
  - initiatives by:
    - strategic fit,
    - value vs cost vs risk,
    - time horizons.
- tracking:
  - funding,
  - stage‑gates,
  - benefit realization.

---

### Minimal Stack Example

You can do solid Business Case & ROI work with:

- Excel/Sheets for modeling,
- BI/analytics for baseline data,
- Confluence/Notion for documents,
- PowerPoint/Slides for presentations.

Sophisticated tools help, but the **thinking and structure** matter far more.
