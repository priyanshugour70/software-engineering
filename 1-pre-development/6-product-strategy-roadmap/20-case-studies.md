## 20 – Case Studies for Product Strategy & Roadmap

This file provides **longer, narrative case studies** to show how Product Strategy & Roadmap work looks in practice.

These are simplified, anonymized composites for learning.

We cover:

- Case 1: Digital SME Lending Transformation in a Bank
- Case 2: Internal Data Platform Strategy in an Enterprise

---

### Case 1 – Digital SME Lending Transformation

#### 1.1 Context

- Large universal bank,
- fragmented SME lending processes:
  - paper‑heavy,
  - slow decisions,
  - inconsistent experiences across channels.
- strong fintech competition in the SME space.

Goal:

- develop a **multi‑year product strategy and roadmap** to:
  - modernize SME lending,
  - protect and grow market share,
  - manage risk and regulatory expectations.

---

#### 1.2 Strategy Work

The team:

- started with:
  - diagnostics (market trends, internal pain points),
  - user & market research (SME interviews, journey mapping),
  - financial analysis (profitability, cost per loan, risk costs),
  - feasibility and risk assessments.

They crafted a **product vision**:

> “Enable SMEs to access tailored, transparent credit in days, not weeks, with a digital experience that matches their expectations while maintaining robust risk and compliance.”

Strategic pillars:

- “Frictionless Digital Experience”
- “Configurable Risk & Compliance Platform”
- “Operational Excellence & Automation”

For each pillar:

- they defined:
  - objectives and metrics (digital share, decision time, cost per loan, risk indicators).

---

#### 1.3 Roadmap Construction

Key initiatives under each pillar:

- Experience:
  - “Digital Application v2”
  - “Self‑Service Loan Management Portal”
- Risk & Compliance:
  - “Configurable Risk Rules Engine”
  - “Enhanced Monitoring & Reporting”
- Operations:
  - “Operations Workbench”
  - “Document Management & Workflow Automation”

They:

- used WSJF and risk‑adjusted ROI (`06-prioritization-frameworks.md`) to:
  - rank initiatives,
  - combine strategic importance with feasibility.
- considered:
  - regulatory deadlines,
  - capacity constraints (platform and product squads),
  - dependencies (risk engine before some digital features).

Roadmaps:

- portfolio view:
  - lanes for:
    - SME Product,
    - Risk Platform,
    - Data Platform,
    - Operations.
- product view:
  - 18‑month horizon:
    - H1: core digital onboarding and basic risk engine integration,
    - H2: advanced risk configuration and monitoring,
    - H3: regional and product expansion options.

---

#### 1.4 Governance and Iteration

Governance setup (`24-governance-alignment-and-cadence.md`):

- quarterly portfolio reviews with:
  - Head of SME Banking,
  - Head of Product,
  - Chief Risk Officer delegate,
  - Technology leadership,
  - Operations lead.

Cadence:

- quarterly:
  - revise roadmap based on outcomes and constraints,
  - decide on adding/removing initiatives.
- monthly:
  - cross‑team syncs on dependencies and risk items.

Over 2–3 years:

- improvements:
  - digital share of applications,
  - decision time,
  - operational cost per loan,
  - reduced manual errors.
- strategy refreshed:
  - to include:
    - new products for SMEs,
    - ecosystem partnerships.

### Case 2 – Internal Data Platform Strategy

#### 2.1 Context

- Global enterprise (not only financial services),
- data scattered across:
  - multiple legacy warehouses,
  - bespoke mart solutions,
  - shadow IT spreadsheets.
- product and analytics teams struggle to get:
  - reliable, timely data.

Goal:

- create a **unified data platform strategy and roadmap** that:
  - consolidates data,
  - improves quality and governance,
  - accelerates analytics and product development.

---

#### 2.2 Strategy Work

The data platform and product leadership teams:

- assessed:
  - current pain points (slow delivery, duplicated pipelines),
  - risks (data quality, compliance gaps),
  - opportunities (self‑service analytics, ML use cases).

Vision:

> “Provide a governed, self‑service data platform that makes trusted data easily discoverable and usable for analytics and product use cases, across the company.”

Strategic themes:

- “Trusted & Governed Data”
- “Self‑Service Access & Tools”
- “Scalable Data Infrastructure”
- “High‑Value Use Case Enablement”

Objectives and metrics:

- reduction in time to onboard new datasets,
- decrease in data quality incidents,
- time‑to‑insight for key analytics scenarios,
- adoption of curated datasets by product teams.

---

#### 2.3 Roadmap Construction

Initiatives:

- Governance:
  - “Data Catalog and Metadata Management”
  - “Domain‑Oriented Data Ownership Model”
- Platform:
  - “Cloud Data Warehouse Migration”
  - “Standardized Ingestion and Transformation Pipelines”
- Enablement:
  - “Self‑Service Analytics Toolkit”
  - “Data Literacy Program”
- Use Cases:
  - “Priority Analytics and Product Use Cases” (e.g., churn models, SME risk scoring).

Dependencies:

- data catalog and governance:
  - needed early for trust,
  - but adoption must be incremental.
- cloud migration:
  - staged by domain,
  - with clear cut‑over plans.

Roadmap views:

- platform roadmap:
  - multi‑year, focusing on infrastructure and core capabilities.
- product/analytics roadmap:
  - linking specific use cases to platform milestones.

---

#### 2.4 Outcomes and Lessons

Positive outcomes:

- reduction in time to create new dashboards and models,
- reduction in conflicting “versions of truth,”
- better auditability and compliance posture.

Lessons:

- strategy & roadmap needed:
  - strong **stakeholder engagement** (business, IT, risk),
  - constant communication about benefits.
- early wins:
  - small but meaningful use cases built on the new platform,
  - increased trust and adoption.

---

### How to Use These Case Studies

As a beginner:

- read cases to:
  - see how abstract concepts (themes, roadmaps, governance) show up in real programs.
- ask:
  - “What is similar to my context?”
  - “What is different?”

Then:

- adapt patterns:
  - vision and themes structure,
  - roadmap layering,
  - governance cadence,
  - stakeholder mix.
