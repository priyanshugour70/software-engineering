## 18 – Startup vs Enterprise in Feasibility Studies

This file compares how Feasibility work looks in **startups** vs **large enterprises/banks**.

We cover:

- A. Startups
- B. Enterprises / banks
- C. Moving between the two

---

### A. Feasibility in Startups

Characteristics:

- fewer systems and processes,
- more flexibility in tech choices,
- high speed and experimentation.

Feasibility style:

- lightweight,
- often combined with:
  - architecture and design,
  - early spikes and prototypes.

Focus:

- prove:
  - concept viability,
  - basic scalability path,
  - security/privacy at an appropriate level for stage.

Good practices:

- use:
  - small technical spikes to test critical assumptions,
  - simple risk lists,
  - quick conversations with advisors on legal/security where needed.

---

### B. Feasibility in Enterprises / Banks

Characteristics:

- many interdependent systems,
- strict regulatory and security regimes,
- multiple governance bodies.

Feasibility style:

- more structured and documented,
- cross‑functional working groups,
- formal engagement with:
  - architecture boards,
  - security & risk committees,
  - legal/compliance.

Focus:

- ensure:
  - solutions are compatible with existing landscape,
  - risk and compliance are satisfied,
  - operations can support the change.

Risks:

- analysis paralysis if:
  - scope is too broad,
  - stakeholders try to design everything upfront.

Good practices:

- timebox Feasibility,
- focus on:
  - major constraints and risks,
  - feasible option set,
  - leaving details for subsequent design.

---

### C. Moving Between Contexts

#### From Startup → Enterprise

Bring:

- comfort with:
  - uncertainty,
  - rapid prototyping,
  - pragmatic trade‑offs.

Adapt:

- learn:
  - governance processes,
  - risk/security/compliance expectations,
  - documentation standards.

---

#### From Enterprise → Startup

Bring:

- strength in:
  - structured risk thinking,
  - cross‑system understanding,
  - long‑term impact awareness.

Adapt:

- avoid:
  - over‑formalizing early,
  - demanding full certainty before experimenting.

Focus:

- “just enough” Feasibility to:
  - avoid obvious failures,
  - while still moving quickly.
