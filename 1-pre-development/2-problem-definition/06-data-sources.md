## 06 – Data Sources for Problem Definition

This file lists **where to get the data** you need to:

- describe current state,
- quantify impact,
- and set realistic targets for problem statements.

We group sources into:

- A. Product & customer data
- B. Operational and process data
- C. Risk, compliance, and audit data
- D. External context

---

### A. Product & Customer Data

#### 1. Product Analytics

Tools:

- Amplitude, Mixpanel, Pendo, in‑house tracking.

Useful for:

- Understanding **behavior in journeys**:
  - onboarding flows,
  - application processes,
  - feature usage patterns.

Common metrics:

- Step‑by‑step **funnels** with drop‑off points.
- Time spent on specific steps or pages.
- Errors or retries (e.g., failed submissions).

Role in Problem Definition:

- Provides **baselines** for:
  - completion rates,
  - cycle times,
  - error frequencies.

---

#### 2. CRM & Customer Master Data

Tools:

- Salesforce, Dynamics, in‑house CRM.

Useful for:

- Segment definitions (size, industry, geography).
- Customer tenure and relationship value.
- Win/loss reasons for deals (when captured).

Role:

- Helps decide:
  - which **segments are in scope**,
  - where the problem is **most severe or valuable** to fix.

---

#### 3. Support & Contact Center Data

Tools:

- Zendesk, ServiceNow, in‑house ticketing, call center logs.

Useful for:

- Number of tickets **related to the problem area**.
- Common issue categories and tags.
- Average handling time and escalation rates.

Role:

- Quantifies **operational pain** and **user frustration**.
- Helps justify “why this problem matters now.”

---

### B. Operational and Process Data

#### 1. Process Metrics & SLAs

Sources:

- Line‑of‑business dashboards,
- operations reports,
- SLA documents.

Useful for:

- Current **cycle times**:
  - time from application to decision,
  - time from ticket open to close.
- Compliance with **agreed SLAs**.

Role:

- Forms basis for:
  - “current vs desired state” sections,
  - setting target metrics in problem statements.

---

#### 2. Process Maps & Documentation

Sources:

- BPM tools (e.g., Signavio),
- Visio diagrams,
- documentation repositories.

Useful for:

- Seeing:
  - all the steps,
  - hand‑offs,
  - systems,
  - and roles involved in the current process.

Role:

- Helps scope:
  - which parts of the process are affected by the problem,
  - where you might **limit scope** for the first initiative.

---

#### 3. Incident Logs and Operational Risk Records

Sources:

- Operational risk systems,
- incident management tools,
- internal audit findings.

Useful for:

- Recording:
  - process failures,
  - system outages,
  - control breakdowns,
linked to the problem area.

Role:

- Highlights:
  - **risk and compliance impact** of current problems.
- Supports stronger **problem impact statements**.

---

### C. Risk, Compliance, and Audit Data (Especially in Banking)

#### 1. Regulatory Requirements and Policies

Sources:

- Internal policy documents,
- regulatory guidance summaries,
- legal/compliance memos.

Useful for:

- Understanding:
  - which constraints **cannot be violated**,
  - minimum requirements for:
    - KYC,
    - reporting,
    - data retention, etc.

Role:

- Feeds into the **constraints** section of problem statements.

---

#### 2. Compliance & Audit Reports

Useful for:

- Identifying:
  - recurring findings,
  - weaknesses in current controls,
  - areas where regulators expect improvement.

Role:

- Turns “we should” into **“we must”** for certain problems.
- Helps prioritize problem definition work linked to **regulatory deadlines**.

---

### D. External Context

Although Problem Definition is mainly about **your internal and customer reality**, some external data is still useful.

#### 1. Industry Benchmarks

Sources:

- Industry reports,
- consulting studies,
- public statements from competitors.

Useful for:

- Comparing:
  - your cycle times,
  - error rates,
  - digital adoption,
to peers.

Role:

- Helps set:
  - realistic targets,
  - or identify where you **lag the market** significantly.

---

#### 2. Customer Expectations from Other Sectors

Sources:

- Research and reports on:
  - digital experiences,
  - self‑service expectations.

Useful for:

- Arguing:
  - why “3 days is too long” even if it meets current internal SLAs.

Role:

- Strengthens the **“why now”** in problem statements.

---

### How to Use These Sources in Practice

1. Start with **product and operational data**:
   - funnels,
   - cycle times,
   - abandonment,
   - support volumes.
2. Add **risk and compliance** inputs:
   - constraints,
   - findings,
   - deadlines.
3. Layer in **external benchmarks** only where helpful:
   - to support targets,
   - to highlight competitive gaps.

Always tie data back to:

- specific parts of your **problem statement**:
  - who, what, why it matters, and outcomes.
