## 25 – Glossary of Terms (Market Opportunity Discovery)

This file defines key terms used throughout the **Market Opportunity Discovery** handbook.  
It is written in **simple language** for beginners.

---

### A

**Analytics**  
Data about how users behave in your product (e.g., which pages they visit, what actions they take, where they drop off).

**Assumption**  
Something you believe is true, but do **not yet have strong proof** for. Assumptions should be written down and later validated or disproven.

---

### B

**Backlog (Opportunity Backlog)**  
A prioritized list of opportunity ideas or hypotheses, with basic information (title, owner, stage, priority).

**Business Impact**  
The effect an opportunity or product could have on company outcomes such as revenue, profit, cost, or risk.

---

### C

**Case Study**  
A detailed story that walks through a real or realistic example from start to finish, showing how discovery was done and what happened.

**Compliance**  
Following the rules and regulations set by governments, regulators, or internal policies.

**Customer Segment**  
A group of customers with **similar characteristics or needs** (e.g., SMEs in retail, high‑net‑worth individuals, students).

---

### D

**Data Source**  
Any place where you can get information for discovery (e.g., analytics dashboards, support tickets, industry reports).

**Discovery (Product Discovery / Market Opportunity Discovery)**  
The phase where you **search for and define** valuable opportunities, before detailed solution design and implementation.

---

### E

**Evaluation Criteria**  
The factors you use to **judge and compare** opportunities (e.g., strategic fit, customer value, business impact, feasibility, risk).

**Evidence**  
Information that supports or challenges an opportunity hypothesis. Can be qualitative (quotes, stories) or quantitative (metrics, counts).

---

### F

**Feasibility**  
How realistic it is to **build, launch, and operate** a solution for an opportunity, considering technology, people, processes, and constraints.

**Funnel**  
A step‑by‑step view of a process (e.g., onboarding) showing how many users move from one step to the next, and where they drop out.

---

### G

**Governance**  
Formal structures and processes (e.g., committees, boards, approval steps) used to guide and control decisions in an organization.

---

### H

**Hypothesis (Opportunity Hypothesis)**  
A structured belief about a potential opportunity, usually in the form:  
“We believe that **[segment]** has **[problem]**, and if we help them achieve **[outcome]**, the business will gain **[impact]**.”

---

### I

**Insight**  
An important understanding or pattern you learn from data or research, which changes or sharpens how you see a problem or opportunity.

---

### J

**Job-To-Be-Done (JTBD)**  
The underlying task or goal a customer is trying to achieve in a given situation (e.g., “ensure I can pay suppliers on time without worrying about cash‑flow”).

---

### L

**Lifecycle (Product Lifecycle)**  
The stages a product or initiative goes through: from idea and discovery, to build, launch, growth, and eventually maintenance or retirement.

---

### M

**Market**  
A group of people or organizations who have **similar needs** and may be interested in buying or using a particular type of product or service.

**Metric**  
A number used to measure something (e.g., conversion rate, churn rate, NPS score).

---

### O

**Objective**  
What you want to achieve. In discovery, objectives might include “identify the top 3–5 opportunities for SME growth this year.”

**Opportunity**  
The potential to create value by solving a meaningful problem for a segment, in a way that your organization can realistically deliver and benefit from.

**Opportunity Brief**  
A short, structured document that explains an opportunity: who it is for, what problem it addresses, why it matters, evidence, risks, and next steps.

**Opportunity Space**  
The set of problems, needs, and desired outcomes you focus on **before** choosing specific solutions or features.

---

### P

**Portfolio (Opportunity Portfolio)**  
The collection of opportunities a company or team is considering or pursuing, usually with different time horizons and risk levels.

**Prioritization**  
The process of deciding **which opportunities to pursue first**, based on criteria and constraints.

**Problem Space**  
The set of user or business problems, needs, and contexts you are trying to understand and improve.

---

### R

**Regulation**  
Official rules created by governments or regulators that organizations must follow (e.g., data privacy, KYC/AML in banking).

**Risk**  
The chance that something will **go wrong or not as expected**, leading to negative outcomes (financial loss, legal issues, reputation damage, wasted effort).

---

### S

**Signal**  
Any piece of information suggesting that something may be important (e.g., repeated complaints, a sharp drop in a funnel, a new regulation).

**Solution Space**  
Specific features, products, UX designs, and technical implementations you create **after** understanding the opportunity.

**Stakeholder**  
A person or group that has an interest in, or is affected by, a decision or product (e.g., business leaders, engineers, risk/compliance, operations).

**Strategic Fit**  
How well an opportunity aligns with the organization’s overall strategy, goals, and positioning.

---

### T

**Theme**  
A recurring pattern or topic that appears across many signals or inputs (e.g., “onboarding friction”, “lack of cash‑flow visibility”).

**Trend**  
A longer‑term change in technology, regulation, economy, or society that affects markets and opportunities.

---

### U

**User Research**  
Activities like interviews, surveys, and observations used to understand users’ behaviors, needs, and experiences.

---

### V

**Value Proposition**  
The clear promise of value you make to a segment: what problem you solve and why your approach is better than alternatives.

---

### W

**Workflow (Discovery Workflow)**  
The structured series of steps you use to run discovery (e.g., prepare & scope → collect signals → synthesize → shape opportunities → evaluate → share & hand over).
