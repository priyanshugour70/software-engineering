## 22 – Risks in Market Opportunity Discovery

This file describes **key risks** connected to discovery work itself and to the opportunities you identify.  
It helps you **anticipate issues early** and design safer, more robust discovery practices.

We group risks into:

- A. Product and market risks
- B. Execution and operational risks
- C. Regulatory and compliance risks (especially in banking)
- D. Organizational and political risks

---

### A. Product and Market Risks

#### 1. Misjudging Customer Demand

Risk:

- You overestimate:
  - how many customers care about the problem,
  - how strongly they care,
  - or their willingness to change behavior.

Consequences:

- Underused products,
- Poor adoption despite strong internal support.

Mitigation:

- Use:
  - customer interviews,
  - surveys,
  - small experiments,
  to validate **demand signals** before big investments.

---

#### 2. Misreading Competitive Landscape

Risk:

- You assume:
  - “no competitor offers this,”
  - or “our solution will be clearly better,”
without deep checking.

Consequences:

- Enter a **crowded or red‑ocean space** with little differentiation.

Mitigation:

- Systematically:
  - analyze competitor offerings,
  - read customer reviews of competitors,
  - update your view regularly.

---

#### 3. Wrong Timing

Risk:

- Opportunity is:
  - **too early** (market not ready, enabling tech immature),
  - or **too late** (space already dominated, table‑stakes only).

Mitigation:

- Pair discovery work with:
  - **trend and technology monitoring** (see `16-market-trends.md`),
  - realistic assessments of your organization’s **ability to execute in time**.

---

### B. Execution and Operational Risks

#### 4. Underestimating Implementation Complexity

Risk:

- Discovery identifies a strong opportunity,
  - but technical or operational complexity is much higher than expected.

Consequences:

- Delays,
- cost overruns,
- scope cuts that **weaken the original value**.

Mitigation:

- Involve:
  - engineering,
  - operations,
  early in discovery (see `11-roles-and-positions.md`).
- Use a **rough feasibility check** (Stage 5 in `03-workflow.md`) before final prioritization.

---

#### 5. Ignoring Change Management

Risk:

- Opportunity requires:
  - new processes,
  - retraining staff,
  - new roles,
  but the organization is not prepared.

Consequences:

- Good solutions fail in practice because:
  - adoption is low internally,
  - people revert to old processes.

Mitigation:

- In discovery, ask:
  - “What would need to change in **operations and roles**?”
  - “Who would own this day‑to‑day?”
- Flag:
  - opportunities with high **change management demands**.

---

### C. Regulatory and Compliance Risks (Banking Focus)

#### 6. Non‑Compliance with Regulations

Risk:

- Opportunity requires:
  - processing new types of data,
  - changing onboarding or KYC flows,
  - cross‑border data flows,
  without proper regulatory analysis.

Consequences:

- Regulatory findings,
  - fines,
  - forced rollbacks of offerings.

Mitigation:

- Involve:
  - risk, compliance, and legal **from early stages**.
- Treat them as:
  - **partners in discovery**, not only gatekeepers.

---

#### 7. Data Privacy and Security Risks

Risk:

- Discovery suggests:
  - new ways of combining or exposing data (e.g., dashboards, APIs),
  which could conflict with:
  - privacy rules,
  - security policies.

Consequences:

- Data breaches,
  - reputational damage,
  - legal consequences.

Mitigation:

- Check:
  - data classification and allowed use cases.
- Consult:
  - security and privacy teams about new data flows early.

---

### D. Organizational and Political Risks

#### 8. Misalignment with Strategy and Stakeholders

Risk:

- Teams pursue opportunities:
  - misaligned with current **strategic priorities**,
  - or important stakeholders don’t buy in.

Consequences:

- Projects struggle to get funding or support.
- Work is blocked or cancelled mid‑way.

Mitigation:

- Tie each opportunity explicitly to:
  - strategy themes and OKRs (see `02-objectives.md`).
- Involve:
  - key leaders in evaluation sessions (see `07-opportunity-evaluation.md`).

---

#### 9. Political Bias in Prioritization

Risk:

- Decisions skewed by:
  - seniority,
  - internal politics,
  - pet projects,
instead of evidence.

Consequences:

- Strong opportunities are ignored,
  - weak ones receive resources.

Mitigation:

- Use:
  - explicit criteria and scoring,
  - structured workshops,
to reduce uncontrolled bias.

---

#### 10. “Discovery Fatigue” and Cynicism

Risk:

- Organization has seen many:
  - research efforts,
  - strategy projects,
that **never led to real change**.

Consequences:

- Stakeholders disengage:
  - they don’t show up to interviews or workshops,
  - they treat discovery as a “checklist exercise”.

Mitigation:

- Ensure:
  - **at least some opportunities** from discovery cycles move into actual projects.
- Communicate:
  - successes,
  - learnings,
  and how discovery led to **real decisions**.

---

### E. Using This File in Practice

When drafting opportunity briefs or planning discovery cycles:

- Add a section called **“Risks and Mitigations”**.
- Walk through:
  - product/market risks,
  - execution risks,
  - regulatory risks,
  - organizational risks.
- Document:
  - which ones apply,
  - what early actions you will take.

This makes your discovery work:

- more **credible**,
- and more likely to survive governance and prioritization reviews.
