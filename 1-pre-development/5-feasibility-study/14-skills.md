## 14 – Skills Needed for Feasibility Studies

This file describes the **skills** that make Feasibility work effective.

We cover:

- A. Core skill areas
- B. Emphasis by role
- C. Beginner learning path

---

### A. Core Skill Areas

#### 1. Systems Thinking

Abilities:

- see:
  - how systems, processes, and people interact,
  - how changes in one area affect others.

---

#### 2. Technical & Architectural Literacy

Abilities:

- understand:
  - high‑level architectures,
  - integration patterns,
  - basic NFRs (performance, resilience).

You don’t need to code every part, but:

- must be comfortable reading diagrams and asking good questions.

---

#### 3. Risk & Constraint Awareness

Abilities:

- think in terms of:
  - risks (what may go wrong),
  - constraints (hard limits),
  - dependencies (what must be in place).

---

#### 4. Cross-Functional Collaboration

Abilities:

- work effectively with:
  - architecture,
  - ops,
  - security,
  - legal,
  - risk,
  - product,
  - business.
- facilitate:
  - workshops,
  - structured discussions.

---

#### 5. Documentation & Communication

Abilities:

- create:
  - clear diagrams,
  - concise summaries,
  - risk registers and recommendations
in a way that:
  - non‑experts can understand.

### C. Beginner Learning Path

If you are new to Feasibility (especially as PM or engineer):

1. **Study prior feasibility or architecture docs** in your org:
   - look at:
     - how options and risks are documented,
     - how constraints are expressed.
2. **Shadow a feasibility or architecture workshop**:
   - observe:
     - questions asked,
     - trade‑offs debated.
3. **Practice mapping a small system**:
   - create a simple context/system diagram and process flow,
   - identify:
     - systems,
     - interfaces,
     - actors.
4. **Draft a mini feasibility note**:
   - pick:
     - a minor change,
     - write 1–2 pages on its feasibility across dimensions.
5. **Get feedback**:
   - from:
     - architects,
     - ops,
     - security,
     - risk.

Over time:

- take on larger feasibility scopes,
- help run cross‑functional sessions.
