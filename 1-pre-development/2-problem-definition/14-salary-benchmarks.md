## 14 – Salary Benchmarks (Conceptual View for Problem Definition)

This file gives a **high-level**, non‑numerical view of how owning Problem Definition tends to correlate with compensation and seniority.  
Exact numbers vary by country, company, and time, so always check local data.

We cover:

- A. Why Problem Definition increases role value
- B. Relative positioning by role level
- C. Factors influencing pay

---

### A. Why Problem Definition Increases Role Value

1. **Deciding what to solve is leverage**  
   - Choosing the right problems to attack has a huge effect on:
     - revenue growth,
     - cost savings,
     - risk reduction.

2. **Senior decision-makers rely on clear framing**  
   - Executives and boards make decisions based on:
     - how well problems (and their impacts) are defined and presented.

3. **Reduces waste**  
   - Strong Problem Definition prevents:
     - building the wrong things,
     - investing in low‑impact areas.

Roles that consistently do this well are often **rewarded with higher responsibility and compensation**.

---

### B. Relative Positioning by Role Level

Conceptually, across many organizations:

1. **Junior Analyst / Associate PM / Junior BA**
   - Supports Problem Definition:
     - helps gather data,
     - documents current state,
     - drafts early problem descriptions.
   - Compensation is aligned with other junior technical or business roles.

2. **Product Manager / Business Analyst / UX Researcher (Mid-Level)**
   - Owns Problem Definition for:
     - a feature area,
     - a single journey,
     - or a specific domain.
   - Typically mid‑band compensation.

3. **Senior PM / Lead BA / Senior UX Researcher**
   - Frames and prioritizes problems:
     - with significant business impact,
     - across multiple sub‑journeys.
   - Often higher salary bands plus performance‑linked components.

4. **Product Lead / Group PM / Product Director / Strategy Manager**
   - Oversees **multiple problem spaces** and teams.
   - Sets **standards for Problem Definition** and ensures alignment across domains.
   - Compensation reflects:
     - wider scope,
     - decision authority,
     - portfolio impact.

5. **Head of Product / VP Product / Head of Strategy / BU Head**
   - Owns the **portfolio of problems and opportunities** for an entire business line or company.
   - Responsible for:
     - deciding **which problems the organization will invest in**, and when.
   - Executive‑level compensation structures.

---

### C. Factors Influencing Pay Ranges

1. **Industry & Regulation**
   - In highly regulated industries (banking, healthcare, telecoms):
     - the **cost of bad problem framing** can be high (fines, incidents),
     - strong Problem Definition at senior levels is highly valued.

2. **Scope & P&L Ownership**
   - Roles that own:
     - larger revenue,
     - more critical journeys,
     - or cross‑market problems,
   often sit in higher bands.

3. **Company Stage**
   - In startups/scaleups:
     - compensation may be a mix of salary and equity,
     - broad Problem Definition responsibilities are often combined with delivery.
   - In large enterprises:
     - more defined role boundaries,
     - more structured salary bands and bonuses.

4. **Location & Market**
   - Major tech and financial hubs often pay more,
   - remote roles and emerging markets may pay less but offer:
     - broader scope,
     - faster growth opportunities.

---

### D. How to Use This as a Beginner

If you’re early in your career:

- Focus on **building a track record** of:
  - clear, evidence‑based problem statements,
  - strong stakeholder alignment.
- When discussing progression or offers, emphasize:
  - the **size and complexity of problems** you’ve framed,
  - the **business outcomes** tied to them.

This shows that you operate **beyond simple task execution**, which is key to moving into higher‑impact, better‑compensated roles.
