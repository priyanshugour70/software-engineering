## 17 – Startup vs Enterprise: Problem Definition Differences

Problem Definition follows the **same principles** everywhere, but practice differs a lot between startups and large enterprises/banks.

We cover:

- A. Key differences
- B. Startup style Problem Definition
- C. Enterprise/bank style Problem Definition
- D. How to adapt when moving between the two

---

### A. Key Differences

#### 1. Speed vs Formality

- **Startups**
  - Faster cycles,
  - Lighter documentation,
  - Decisions often made by a few people.
- **Enterprises**
  - More formal documents (for governance and traceability),
  - More people to align,
  - Slower approval cycles.

#### 2. Risk Appetite

- **Startups**
  - Can tolerate more product/market risk,
  - Often test multiple problem framings quickly.
- **Enterprises/Banks**
  - Lower tolerance for:
    - regulatory,
    - operational,
    - reputational risk.
  - Need careful consideration of constraints in problem statements.

#### 3. Constraints Landscape

- **Startups**
  - Fewer legacy systems,
  - More technical freedom, but limited resources.
- **Enterprises**
  - Many constraints:
    - legacy systems,
    - complex org structure,
    - regulators,
    - existing contracts.

---

### B. Problem Definition in Startups

Characteristics:

- Problem statements may live in:
  - Notion pages,
  - Slack messages,
  - product backlogs.
- Emphasis on:
  - **rapid understanding**,
  - **experiments**.

Typical approach:

1. Talk to users early and often.
2. Draft simple, lean problem statements.
3. Test understanding by:
   - building small experiments or prototypes.
4. Iterate quickly.

Risks:

- Under‑documented problems:
  - new team members may not know **why** something is being done.
- Jumping into solutions without:
  - enough thinking about scope and impact.

How to strengthen:

- Adopt **lightweight templates** from `23-templates.md`.
- Keep a simple **problem backlog** with:
  - current state,
  - outcomes,
  - constraints.

---

### C. Problem Definition in Enterprises/Banks

Characteristics:

- Often part of **formal processes**:
  - project initiation,
  - investment cases,
  - change management.
- More documentation required:
  - problem packages,
  - scope agreements,
  - sign‑offs.

Typical approach:

1. Receive an opportunity or theme from strategy/portfolio.
2. Run a **Problem Definition sprint** with:
   - interviews,
   - workshops,
   - data analysis.
3. Produce:
   - formal problem statements,
   - scopes,
   - constraint registers.
4. Present to:
   - committees,
   - leadership,
   - architecture or risk boards.

Benefits:

- Better traceability and auditability.
- Stronger alignment across many teams.

Risks:

- Over‑engineering:
  - long documents nobody reads.
- Slow cycles:
  - excessive analysis before any action.

How to strengthen:

- Keep documents:
  - **short, clear, and visual**.
- Align with:
  - real decisions,
  - not documentation for its own sake.

---

### D. Moving Between Startup and Enterprise Contexts

#### From Startup → Enterprise/Bank

Keep:

- Bias for:
  - clarity,
  - action,
  - user centricity.

Adapt:

- Learn:
  - how governance works,
  - where risk/compliance are involved.
- Accept that:
  - more documentation is needed,
  - more people must be aligned.

Focus:

- Using your startup experience to:
  - **simplify complex discussions**,
  - prevent over‑complication of problem statements.

---

#### From Enterprise/Bank → Startup

Keep:

- Strong habits of:
  - documenting problems,
  - using data,
  - involving key perspectives.

Adapt:

- Reduce:
  - heavy templates,
  - long cycles.
- Embrace:
  - experimentation,
  - faster iteration.

Focus:

- Bringing **just enough structure** to guide decisions,
  - without blocking speed and learning.
