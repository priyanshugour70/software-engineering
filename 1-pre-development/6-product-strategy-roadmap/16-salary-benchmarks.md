## 16 – Salary Benchmarks & Compensation Considerations

This file provides **high‑level guidance** on how Product Strategy & Roadmap responsibilities can influence compensation.

It does **not** give exact numbers (these vary by country, company, and year), but explains:

- compensation components,
- how strategy responsibility usually maps to seniority,
- what tends to increase earning potential over time.

---

### 1. Compensation Components (Conceptual)

Typical components:

- **Base salary**:
  - fixed yearly pay.
- **Annual bonus**:
  - based on:
    - company performance,
    - team performance,
    - individual performance.
- **Equity / Stock / Long‑term incentives**:
  - more common in:
    - tech,
    - startups/scaleups,
    - some large public companies.
- **Other benefits**:
  - pension, healthcare, allowances, training budgets, etc.

Strategy & roadmap roles:

- often have:
  - higher base pay,
  - and more variable/incentive components,
  - as seniority increases.

---

### 2. How Strategy Responsibility Relates to Seniority

In many organizations:

- as you move from:
  - **Associate PM → PM → Senior PM → Lead/Principal → Head of Product/Director**,
- your:
  - **scope** (teams, products, revenue),
  - and **decision impact** increase.

Product Strategy & Roadmap responsibility:

- is a strong indicator that you’re at:
  - mid or senior levels,
  - or on a leadership track.

Signs:

- you own:
  - multi‑quarter or multi‑year roadmaps,
  - complex trade‑offs across domains,
  - cross‑functional alignment.

This tends to be:

- associated with:
  - higher compensation bands,
  - especially when:
    - outcomes are visible and measurable.

---

### 3. Enterprise/Bank vs Startup/Scaleup Patterns

#### 3.1 Enterprises/Banks

Patterns:

- often:
  - more stable salaries,
  - structured bands,
  - smaller equity components.
- bonuses:
  - linked to:
    - business unit performance,
    - risk/compliance outcomes,
    - personal objectives.

Product leaders:

- may sit at:
  - senior manager,
  - director,
  - or VP levels,
  - with corresponding compensation.

#### 3.2 Startups/Scaleups

Patterns:

- more variance:
  - lower base salary possible,
  - higher equity upside.
- product strategy roles:
  - may formally be “Head of Product”,
  - but scope and compensation levels vary widely.

Risk/return profile:

- potentially:
  - lower stability,
  - higher upside if the company grows significantly.

---

### 4. Factors That Typically Increase Compensation Over Time

Non‑exhaustive list:

- **Breadth and depth of ownership**:
  - from single features to entire products or portfolios.
- **Impact on measurable outcomes**:
  - revenue, margin, risk reduction, cost savings, customer satisfaction.
- **Ability to navigate complexity**:
  - regulated environments,
  - multi‑team dependencies,
  - legacy + modern stacks.
- **Leadership and influence**:
  - leading cross‑functional strategy and roadmap work,
  - coaching other PMs and leaders.

Strategy & roadmap capability:

- is central to all of the above.

---

### 5. Using Benchmarks Responsibly

To find **actual numbers** you would typically use:

- public salary data and reports,
- job platforms and postings,
- industry surveys,
- internal HR band information.

Common sources (conceptually):

- public compensation databases,
- professional networks,
- industry organizations’ reports.

Important:

- account for:
  - geography,
  - seniority,
  - cost of living,
  - company size and stage,
  - role scope and title inflation (e.g., “Head of Product” in a 10‑person startup vs a global bank).

### 6. Beginner Checklist

- [ ] Do you understand:
  - [ ] how your current role’s scope compares to market expectations?
  - [ ] whether you are considered:
    - early,
    - mid,
    - or senior in product career terms?
- [ ] Do you know:
  - [ ] what additional responsibilities (especially around strategy & roadmap) are expected at the next level?
  - [ ] how those responsibilities connect to compensation bands in your company?

If not:

- use:
  - internal career frameworks,
  - conversations with your manager or HR,
  - and external job descriptions,
  - to clarify expectations.
