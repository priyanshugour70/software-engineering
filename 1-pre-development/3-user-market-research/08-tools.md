## 08 – Tools for User & Market Research

This file lists **practical tools** that support research work.  
Focus on **capabilities**, then map to tools your org already uses.

We group tools into:

- A. Planning & documentation
- B. Qualitative data collection
- C. Quantitative data & analytics
- D. Synthesis & repositories

---

### A. Planning & Documentation

**Confluence / Notion / SharePoint / Google Docs**

Use for:

- Research plans,
- discussion guides,
- recruitment trackers,
- readouts and reports.

Best practices:

- Create a **central research space** per domain:
  - `/pre-development/user-market-research/<domain>/`
- Link:
  - problems,
  - research plans,
  - findings,
  - artifacts.

---

### B. Qualitative Data Collection

#### 1. Interviews & Remote Sessions

Tools:

- Video: Zoom, Teams, Google Meet (enterprise‑approved tools).
- Recording & transcription (where allowed):
  - built‑in call recording,
  - transcription services approved by your org.

Use for:

- user & stakeholder interviews,
- remote usability tests,
- expert interviews.

Compliance:

- Always:
  - inform participants,
  - obtain consent,
  - follow recording rules.

---

#### 2. Usability Testing & Session Capture

Tools (if allowed):

- UserTesting, Lookback, UserZoom, or internal equivalents.
- Screen recording tools for in‑house tests.

Use for:

- observing participants interacting with:
  - prototypes (Figma, etc.),
  - existing products.

---

#### 3. Survey Tools

Tools:

- Qualtrics, Medallia, internal survey platforms,
- in‑product survey banners/tools.

Use for:

- targeted pulse surveys,
- NPS/CSAT related to journeys,
- validating qualitative findings at scale.

---

### C. Quantitative Data & Analytics

#### 1. Product Analytics Platforms

Tools:

- Amplitude, Mixpanel, Pendo, in‑house event pipelines.

Use for:

- funnels,
- retention,
- feature usage,
- behavioral segmentation.

Researchers should:

- work closely with data teams,
- agree on event definitions,
- annotate charts with context.

---

#### 2. BI & Data Warehouses

Tools:

- Tableau, Power BI, Looker, Qlik, Snowflake, BigQuery,
- internal reporting systems.

Use for:

- performance and risk KPIs,
- multi‑dataset joins (e.g., CRM + product usage),
- breakdowns by:
  - segment,
  - region,
  - product line.

---

### D. Synthesis & Repositories

#### 1. Note‑Taking & Coding

Tools:

- Dovetail, EnjoyHQ, Notion, Confluence.

Use for:

- storing interview notes and transcripts,
- tagging quotes by:
  - theme,
  - persona,
  - journey step.

Benefits:

- builds a **knowledge base** that can be reused.

---

#### 2. Mapping & Visualization

Tools:

- Miro, FigJam, Lucidchart, service design tools.

Use for:

- affinity mapping,
- user journeys,
- service blueprints,
- opportunity maps.

---

### Minimal Stack Example

For a team starting from scratch:

- Docs & plans: Confluence or Notion.
- Calls: Zoom/Teams, with manual note‑taking.
- Analytics: whatever BI/product analytics is already available.
- Mapping: Miro or PowerPoint + shared drive.

You can still run **high‑quality research** with this minimal set if your process is strong.
