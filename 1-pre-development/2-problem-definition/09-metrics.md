## 09 – Metrics for Problem Definition

This file explains **which metrics matter** when defining a problem and how to use them.

We focus on:

- A. Metrics that describe the current problem
- B. Metrics that define desired outcomes
- C. Example metric sets by problem type

---

### A. Metrics That Describe the Current Problem

These metrics help answer:

- “How bad is the problem?”
- “For whom, and how often does it occur?”

Common categories:

#### 1. Volume & Frequency

Examples:

- Number of:
  - onboarding attempts per week,
  - loan applications per month,
  - support tickets about a specific issue.

Use:

- Shows **how common** the problem is.

---

#### 2. Conversion & Completion

Examples:

- Onboarding completion rate.
- Application completion rate.
- % of users who finish a key journey.

Use:

- Highlights **drop‑offs and friction points**.

---

#### 3. Time & Latency

Examples:

- Time from:
  - application start to decision,
  - ticket open to resolution,
  - first use to successful completion of a key task.

Use:

- Captures **speed and efficiency** problems.

---

#### 4. Error & Failure Rates

Examples:

- % of:
  - failed transactions,
  - rejected forms due to validation errors,
  - rework (cases re‑opened).

Use:

- Shows **quality and reliability** issues.

---

#### 5. Support & Operational Load

Examples:

- Number of:
  - support tickets related to the problem,
  - calls about a specific status,
  - manual interventions in a process.

Use:

- Quantifies **internal pain and cost**.

---

### B. Metrics That Define Desired Outcomes

These metrics help answer:

- “What will success look like if we solve this problem?”

Categories usually mirror those above:

#### 1. Target Completion & Conversion

Examples:

- “Increase onboarding completion rate from 60% to 80%.”
- “Increase % of digital‑only applications from 40% to 70%.”

---

#### 2. Target Time Improvements

Examples:

- “Reduce average time to decision from 5 days to under 2 hours.”
- “Reduce average handling time for issue X by 30%.”

---

#### 3. Target Error / Failure Reductions

Examples:

- “Reduce payment failure rate from 3% to <1%.”
- “Reduce rework on KYC reviews by 50%.”

---

#### 4. Target Support Load Reductions

Examples:

- “Reduce call center volume related to onboarding status by 30%.”
- “Reduce number of escalations on issue Y by half.”

---

#### 5. Satisfaction & Perception

Examples:

- “Improve NPS for SMEs in onboarding journey by +10 points.”
- “Increase % of surveyed users who say ‘the process is clear’ from 50% to 80%.”

Use:

- Complements operational metrics with **user‑perceived improvement**.

---

### C. Example Metric Sets by Problem Type

#### 1. Problem: High Abandonment in Online Onboarding

Current metrics:

- 1000 onboarding starts / month.
- 600 abandon before completion (60% completion).
- Average time to completion: 45 minutes (for successful cases).
- 300 onboarding‑related support tickets / month.

Target outcomes:

- Increase completion from 60% → 80%.
- Reduce average time to completion from 45 → 20 minutes.
- Reduce onboarding support tickets by 40%.

These numbers then appear directly in the **problem statement**.

---

#### 2. Problem: Slow SME Loan Decisions

Current metrics:

- Average time from application to decision: 5 business days.
- 20% of applicants call at least once to ask about status.
- 15% of applications abandoned before final decision.

Targets:

- Reduce time to decision to < 2 hours for eligible segments.
- Cut status inquiry calls by 50%.
- Reduce abandonment from 15% to < 5%.

---

#### 3. Problem: High Manual Rework in KYC Process

Current metrics:

- 30% of KYC cases require rework.
- Average rework adds 2 days to onboarding.
- 10% of operational capacity spent on corrections.

Targets:

- Reduce rework rate from 30% → 10%.
- Recover 30–40% of manual capacity for other tasks.

---

### How to Use Metrics in Problem Definition

1. **Describe current state**  
   - Use metrics to **anchor** the problem in reality.
2. **Set initial targets**  
   - Use them to express **ambition level**; refine later with business case work.
3. **Enable testability**  
   - Ensure you can later say:
     - “We changed X, and Y metric improved as expected.”
