## 25 – Glossary of Terms (Business Case & ROI)

This file defines key terms used throughout the Business Case & ROI handbook.

---

### A

**Adoption Rate**  
The proportion of the target population that starts using a new product, feature, or process.

**Assumption**  
An explicit belief about a value or behavior (e.g., adoption, conversion, cost) that is not certain and must be validated or revisited.

---

### B

**Baseline**  
Current, pre‑change level of a metric (e.g., number of loans per year, current abandonment rate).

**Benefit**  
Any positive effect of an initiative: increased revenue, reduced cost, reduced risk, or strategic advantage.

**Business Case**  
Structured argument, supported by data and assumptions, explaining why an initiative should (or should not) be funded.

---

### C

**CapEx / OpEx**  
Capital Expenditures (CapEx) and Operating Expenditures (OpEx) – how investments and ongoing costs are categorized in financial accounting (definitions vary by org).

**Cash Flow**  
Net inflow or outflow of money in a period (e.g., year) due to a project.

**Cost of Capital**  
The required return rate for investments, often used as discount rate in NPV calculations.

---

### D

**Discount Rate**  
Rate used to convert future cash flows into their present value (reflecting time value of money and risk).

**Driver**  
A variable that directly influences outcomes, such as adoption rate, conversion percentage, or cost per unit.

---

### F

**Forecast**  
An estimate of future values based on assumptions, models, and historical data.

---

### I

**IRR (Internal Rate of Return)**  
The discount rate at which a project’s Net Present Value (NPV) is zero; often compared to a hurdle rate.

**Investment Committee**  
A group of senior stakeholders that reviews and approves or rejects funding for initiatives.

---

### K

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)**  
A metric used to track progress toward specific objectives.

---

### N

**NPV (Net Present Value)**  
The sum of all future cash flows (positive and negative) discounted back to today; positive NPV indicates expected value creation at the chosen discount rate.

---

### O

**Opportunity Cost**  
The value of the best alternative use of money or capacity that is not chosen.

**Option (Implementation Option)**  
A specific combination of scope, timing, and delivery approach for an initiative (e.g., MVP vs full rollout).

---

### P

**Payback Period**  
The time it takes for cumulative net cash flows from an initiative to cover its initial investment.

**Portfolio**  
The set of all initiatives and projects competing for funding and capacity.

**P&L (Profit & Loss)**  
Financial statement summarizing revenues, costs, and profits over a period; in this context, often refers to the owned revenue/cost scope of a business owner.

---

### R

**ROI (Return on Investment)**  
A measure of net benefit relative to total investment, usually calculated as (Benefits − Costs) / Costs over a period.

**Risk (Project / Financial)**  
Uncertainty about outcomes that can lead to worse‑than‑expected results, such as delivery issues, adoption failure, or regulatory changes.

---

### S

**Scenario (Financial)**  
A coherent set of assumptions (e.g., conservative/base/aggressive) used to model different possible futures.

**Sensitivity Analysis**  
Analysis showing how much results change when individual drivers or assumptions change.

---

### T

**Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)**  
The full cost of building, running, and maintaining a solution over its life, including direct and indirect costs.

---

### V

**Value Driver**  
A factor that, when changed, increases or decreases value (e.g., number of active customers, average revenue per user, cost per transaction).
