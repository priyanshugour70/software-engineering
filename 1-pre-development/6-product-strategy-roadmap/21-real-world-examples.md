## 21 – Real‑World Examples & Scenarios

This file gives **smaller, focused scenarios** to show how you might apply Product Strategy & Roadmap concepts in day‑to‑day situations.

These are shorter than the case studies in `20-case-studies.md`, and meant to feel closer to your daily work.

---

### Example 1 – Saying “Not Now” to a Senior Stakeholder Feature Request

Scenario:

- senior sales leader requests:
  - “urgent” new feature for SME lending portal,
  - to support a specific partner campaign.
- your roadmap:
  - already full for the next quarter with:
    - digital journey improvements,
    - risk platform work,
    - key regulatory items.

Approach:

1. **Map the request to themes and goals**:
   - does it support:
     - growth goals?
     - risk and compliance?
     - long‑term strategic pillars?
2. **Estimate value and effort**:
   - quick rough cut:
     - potential uplift in new SME applications,
     - integration and ops complexity.
3. **Compare against existing initiatives**:
   - using your prioritization framework (e.g., RICE).
4. **Communicate clearly**:
   - if it ranks lower:
     - explain trade‑offs,
     - offer alternative:
       - “We can re‑scope initiative X or delay Y if we prioritize this, is that acceptable?”

Outcome:

- you avoid:
  - silently over‑committing your teams,
  - damaging trust with missed commitments.
- you use:
  - roadmap and strategy as **shared decision tools**, not just a list.

### Example 2 – Adjusting Roadmap After New Regulatory Guidance

Scenario:

- regulator issues new guidance impacting credit decisioning timelines,
- risk and compliance raise:
  - a set of mandatory changes,
  - with a deadline in 9 months.

Approach:

1. **Clarify requirements**:
   - work with risk/compliance and legal,
   - understand what changes are needed in:
     - product experience,
     - risk engine,
     - reporting and monitoring.
2. **Shape regulatory initiatives**:
   - create explicit epics/initiatives:
     - “Regulation XYZ – SME Lending Changes.”
3. **Reprioritize**:
   - re‑score affected initiatives,
   - adjust roadmap:
     - some discretionary growth items may move to “later.”
4. **Communicate**:
   - update roadmap views,
   - explain:
     - why items are moving,
     - regulatory nature and risks,
     - new expected timelines.

Outcome:

- roadmap:
  - remains a **living artifact**,
  - reflects real risks and constraints.
- you:
  - maintain credibility with stakeholders and regulators.

### Example 3 – Introducing a Platform Initiative into a Product‑Heavy Roadmap

Scenario:

- product teams want:
  - many new customer‑facing features.
- engineering sees:
  - performance and scalability issues,
  - high operational pain.

Approach:

1. **Articulate platform problem and benefits**:
   - show:
     - current incidents and costs,
     - performance impact,
     - risk of outages.
2. **Propose a platform initiative**:
   - e.g., “Lending Platform Resilience Upgrade.”
   - define:
     - objectives (uptime, latency, incident reduction),
     - KRs/metrics.
3. **Run prioritization**:
   - score platform initiative alongside feature initiatives,
   - include:
     - risk reduction and opportunity enablement.
4. **Integrate into roadmap**:
   - allocate:
     - a portion of capacity to platform work,
     - visible lane in roadmap views.

Outcome:

- platform work is:
  - visible,
  - justified,
  - protected,
  - not just “invisible tech chores.”

### Example 4 – Dealing with a Failing Initiative

Scenario:

- initiative launched 6 months ago to:
  - increase cross‑sell of loans to existing SME customers.
- early results:
  - **far below expectations**,
  - even after improvements.

Approach:

1. **Review hypothesis and metrics**:
   - was the opportunity mis‑estimated?
   - are we measuring the right things?
2. **Run targeted discovery**:
   - speak with customers and front‑line staff,
   - examine:
     - friction points,
     - value proposition.
3. **Decide**:
   - pivot (change approach),
   - persevere with clear new hypotheses,
   - or stop and reallocate capacity.
4. **Update strategy/roadmap**:
   - document decision:
     - in decision log (`12-documents-and-artifacts.md`),
   - adjust:
     - roadmap items and capacity allocations.

Outcome:

- you treat roadmap:
  - as a tool to manage bets,
  - not a contract to deliver pre‑agreed outputs regardless of outcomes.

### Example 5 – Introducing Multi‑Horizon Roadmapping in a Team

Scenario:

- team currently:
  - maintains a single backlog in `Jira`,
  - struggles to communicate future direction beyond a few sprints.

Approach:

1. **Introduce horizons**:
   - Horizon 1 (0–3 months),
   - Horizon 2 (3–12 months),
   - Horizon 3 (12+ months).
2. **Group epics/initiatives**:
   - H1:
     - committed work,
     - detailed scope.
   - H2:
     - likely bets,
     - less detailed.
   - H3:
     - themes and options.
3. **Create simple visual**:
   - now/next/later board,
   - or timeline with bands.
4. **Share with stakeholders**:
   - use it:
     - in planning and alignment sessions,
     - to discuss trade‑offs.

Outcome:

- stakeholders:
  - get clearer view of direction,
  - understand uncertainty levels at each horizon.
- team:
  - has better tool to discuss changes and ideas.
