## 19 – Real-World Style Examples (Micro-Scenarios)

These short scenarios illustrate **specific Problem Definition moves**.  
They are simplified but realistic.

We cover:

- A. Tightening a vague problem from leadership
- B. Using tickets to refine a problem
- C. Adjusting scope after discovering constraints

---

### A. Tightening a Vague Problem from Leadership

**Initial request from leadership**

> “Our SME digital experience is bad. Fix it.”

This is:

- broad,
- vague,
- solution‑agnostic but unusable as is.

**Problem Definition actions**

1. Review:
   - NPS by journey,
   - support tickets,
   - funnel data for key flows.
2. Discover:
   - SME onboarding and loan applications have the lowest satisfaction and highest abandonment.
3. Focus on onboarding first.

**Refined problem statement**

> “Existing SME customers in Country X who attempt to open an additional current account via our online portal experience confusing document requirements and unclear progress indicators, leading to only 25% completing digitally and 35% of starts ending with a branch visit or abandonment.”

Now you have:

- a **specific** problem,
- with a clear **segment and journey**,
- supported by **data**.

### B. Using Tickets to Refine a Problem

**Symptom observed**

Support team says:

> “We get a lot of calls about loan status.”

**Problem Definition actions**

1. Pull 3 months of **support tickets** tagged:
   - “loan status”,
   - “application progress”.
2. Group by reasons:
   - “No update after 3 days”,
   - “Don’t understand what ‘under review’ means”,
   - “Did I miss something?”.
3. Check **application data**:
   - time from application to first update,
   - frequency of status changes.

**Refined problem statement**

> “SME applicants in Country X receive no proactive updates for up to 3 days after submitting loan applications, and see only a generic ‘under review’ status in the portal, prompting 500+ status inquiry calls per month and leaving customers uncertain about whether additional action is required.”

What changed:

- Vague “loan status is a problem” → **clear description of timing and messaging issues**.

### C. Adjusting Scope After Discovering Constraints

**Initial problem framing**

> “Automate approvals for all SME loans to deliver instant decisions.”

After talking to risk and compliance:

- You learn:
  - only a subset of low‑risk SMEs can be considered for automation,
  - regulator requires manual oversight for certain segments and thresholds.

**Adjusted problem statement**

> “For low‑risk existing SME customers in Country X, current loan approvals are processed manually with 5‑day average decision time, despite having sufficient transaction and behavioral data to make faster decisions within our risk policies. This causes delays and lost deals in a segment where automation is permitted and feasible.”

Scope:

- Narrowed to:
  - low‑risk existing customers,
  - Country X.

Constraints:

- Manual oversight remains required for:
  - high‑risk segments,
  - certain thresholds, documented in the constraints section.

Result:

- A **realistic problem** that:
  - acknowledges constraints,
  - still unlocks meaningful value.
