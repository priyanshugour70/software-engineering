## 12 – Skills Needed for Business Case & ROI

This file covers the **skills** individuals need to contribute effectively to Business Case & ROI work.

We group skills into:

- A. Core skill areas
- B. Emphasis by role
- C. Beginner learning path

---

### A. Core Skill Areas

#### 1. Driver-Based Thinking

Abilities:

- break outcomes into:
  - volume × rate × value per unit,
  - cost per unit × units, etc.
- avoid single magic numbers; focus on **drivers**.

---

#### 2. Basic Financial Literacy

Abilities:

- understand:
  - revenue vs margin vs profit,
  - CapEx vs OpEx (in your org’s terms),
  - ROI, payback, NPV (at least conceptually).
- read simple financial tables.

---

#### 3. Data & Analytics Literacy

Abilities:

- interpret:
  - product analytics,
  - performance dashboards,
  - risk statistics.
- ask:
  - “Is this data complete?”
  - “What segment does this describe?”

---

#### 4. Estimation & Assumption Management

Abilities:

- estimate:
  - rough ranges,
  - bracket uncertainty,
  - choose base/conservative/aggressive values.
- write down:
  - assumptions,
  - sources,
  - confidence levels.

---

#### 5. Communication & Storytelling

Abilities:

- explain:
  - complex models in simple language,
  - trade‑offs and scenarios clearly.
- tailor:
  - communication to:
    - product and tech teams,
    - business leadership,
    - finance and risk.

---

#### 6. Collaboration & Stakeholder Management

Abilities:

- work with:
  - finance,
  - data,
  - engineering,
  - operations,
  - risk,
  - strategy.
- reconcile:
  - different perspectives and incentives.

### C. Beginner Learning Path

If you’re new to this area (especially as a PM or engineer):

#### Step 1 – Learn Basic Financial Concepts

- Read:
  - `01-concepts.md`,
  - `04-financial-metrics-and-formulas.md`.
- Ask a finance partner to:
  - walk you through:
    - one or two existing business cases or investment memos.

---

#### Step 2 – Practice Driver-Based Thinking

- Take:
  - a metric like “SME lending revenue”,
  - break it down into:
    - number of customers,
    - products per customer,
    - average balance or loan size,
    - margin.
- Identify:
  - which drivers your initiative could influence.

---

#### Step 3 – Work Through a Simple Model

- Build:
  - a small Excel/Sheets model for a hypothetical initiative using:
    - benefit and cost estimation patterns.
- Calculate:
  - payback and simple ROI.

---

#### Step 4 – Co-Author a Business Case Section

- Volunteer to draft:
  - the benefits section,
  - or the cost breakdown,
for a real initiative under guidance from:
  - PM,
  - business owner,
  - or finance partner.

---

#### Step 5 – Present Numbers to Stakeholders

- Present:
  - a subset of the case (e.g., adoption scenarios) to:
    - your squad,
    - or a small steering group.

Focus on:

- explaining:
  - assumptions,
  - trade‑offs,
  - and what would change decisions.
