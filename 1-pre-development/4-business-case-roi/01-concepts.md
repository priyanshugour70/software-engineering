## 01 – Concepts: What Is Business Case & ROI Analysis?

### 1. Simple Definition (For Beginners)

**Business Case & ROI Analysis** is the process of answering:

> “Is this initiative worth doing, at this scale, right now, compared to everything else we could do?”

It:

- turns **problems and research insights** into:
  - expected **benefits** (money in, money saved, risk reduced),
  - expected **costs** (money and capacity out),
  - and **risks/uncertainty** around those numbers.

The end result is a **structured argument** – with numbers and assumptions – that decision‑makers can use to:

- approve,
- adjust,
- delay,
- or reject an initiative.

---

### 2. Relationship to Other Phases

Business Case & ROI sits **after**:

- `1-market-opportunity-discovery/` (where you identify promising spaces),
- `2-problem-definition/` (where you clarify what problem to solve),
- `3-user-market-research/` (where you gather evidence about users and markets),

and **before**:

- detailed product strategy and roadmap commitments,
- requirements engineering and build.

So it is the **bridge between discovery and investment**.

---

### 3. Key Concepts You Must Know

#### 3.1 Benefits

Different types of benefits:

- **Revenue benefits**
  - New customers, more volume, higher prices, better retention.
- **Cost benefits**
  - Lower operating costs (automation, fewer errors, less manual work),
  - lower support costs, simpler processes.
- **Risk benefits**
  - Reduced fraud losses,
  - fewer compliance fines,
  - lower credit losses.
- **Intangible / strategic benefits**
  - Brand strength,
  - customer satisfaction,
  - market positioning.

Some benefits are easier to **quantify** than others; all should at least be **named and reasoned about**.

---

#### 3.2 Costs

Costs usually include:

- **One‑time / upfront (CapEx‑like)**
  - engineering and design,
  - integrations,
  - infrastructure setup,
  - training and change management.

- **Ongoing (OpEx‑like)**
  - hosting and licenses,
  - support and maintenance,
  - vendor fees,
  - monitoring and compliance overhead.

Ignoring ongoing costs is a very common mistake.

---

#### 3.3 Return on Investment (ROI) and Related Metrics

Common metrics (explained in detail in `04-financial-metrics-and-formulas.md`):

- **ROI (Return on Investment)**
  - Typically:
    - (Net benefit over period) / (Total cost over period).
- **NPV (Net Present Value)**
  - Value of future cash flows discounted back to today.
- **IRR (Internal Rate of Return)**
  - Discount rate at which NPV = 0.
- **Payback Period**
  - How long it takes for cumulative benefits to cover initial investment.

You may not calculate all of these for every initiative, but you should understand:

- what they mean,
- what decisions they help support.

---

#### 3.4 Scenarios and Sensitivity

Because future outcomes are uncertain, you should think in **ranges and scenarios**, not single numbers:

- **Scenarios**
  - Base case,
  - conservative case,
  - aggressive case.
- **Sensitivity**
  - How results change when a single assumption changes (e.g., adoption rate, conversion, cost overrun).

This helps decision‑makers understand:

- what the main drivers are,
- where uncertainty is highest.

---

### 4. “Business Case” vs “Pitch Deck” vs “Budget Request”

- **Business case**
  - Detailed, structured, evidence‑based.
  - Includes numbers, assumptions, scenarios, and risks.
- **Pitch deck**
  - Often more narrative and persuasive,
  - may be used early or in startup funding contexts.
- **Budget request**
  - Short form linking to business case or prior approvals.

In enterprises/banks, Business Case & ROI should **stand up to scrutiny** by:

- finance,
- risk,
- strategy,
- auditors.

It is not just a sales pitch; it is an **investment analysis**.

---

### 5. Example: SME Digital Lending Initiative

Given:

- problem definition:
  - slow, opaque SME loan process,
- research:
  - SMEs want transparency, faster decisions,
  - competitors moving quickly in digital lending.

Business Case work might ask:

- How much additional loan volume can we realistically gain?
- How will this affect:
  - interest income,
  - fee income,
  - credit losses?
- How much can we reduce:
  - operations cost per loan,
  - call center volume?
- What will it cost to:
  - build digital flows,
  - integrate with bureaus and KYC providers,
  - run and support the solution?

Then:

- build multi‑year models,
- calculate ROI/NPV/payback,
- analyze sensitivity to adoption and conversion,
- weigh it against other initiatives (e.g., SME cash‑flow tools, onboarding).

---

### 6. Mental Models for Beginners

#### 6.1 “Investment Lens”

Think like an investor:

- “If we invest X now and over the next few years, what **credible** benefits do we expect to receive, and with what risks?”

#### 6.2 “From Story to Spreadsheet”

- Start with a **story**:
  - user pain, opportunity, strategy.
- Translate it into:
  - quantitative drivers,
  - assumptions,
  - cash flows.
- Then check:
  - “Does the story still make sense when we look at the numbers?”
