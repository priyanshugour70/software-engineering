## 03 – Workflow: Step‑by‑Step User & Market Research

This file describes a **practical, end‑to‑end workflow** for the research phase.  
You can scale it up or down depending on scope, risk, and timelines.

We’ll cover:

- A. High‑level stages
- B. Detailed steps
- C. Example timelines (lean vs full)

---

### A. High‑Level Stages

1. **Intake & Alignment**
2. **Plan & Design Research**
3. **Recruit Participants & Prepare Data**
4. **Collect Data (Qualitative & Quantitative)**
5. **Synthesize & Model**
6. **Communicate & Integrate**

You often loop between 4 and 5 as you uncover new questions.

---

### B. Detailed Steps

#### Stage 1 – Intake & Alignment

**Goal:** Ensure research is **decision‑driven** and aligned with problem & strategy.

Inputs:

- Problem statement(s) from `2-problem-definition/`,
- opportunity briefs,
- strategic/OKR documents,
- known risks and constraints.

Activities:

1. Review problems and context:
   - What do we already know?
   - What are the biggest unknowns and assumptions?
2. Identify decisions:
   - “What decisions do we need to make after this research?”
   - “What would we do differently depending on what we learn?”
3. Draft research goals:
   - E.g., “Understand SME owners’ decision process for choosing a lending provider.”

Output:

- A short **research intent note** summarizing:
  - decisions,
  - key questions,
  - constraints.

---

#### Stage 2 – Plan & Design Research

**Goal:** Turn intent into a **research plan**.

Activities:

1. Convert goals into **research questions & hypotheses** (see `04-research-questions-and-hypotheses.md`).
2. Choose methods:
   - interviews, shadowing, usability tests,
   - analytics deep dives, surveys,
   - competitive analysis, etc. (see `05-methods-overview.md`).
3. Define participants:
   - segments, roles, minimum sample sizes (see `06-recruitment-and-sampling.md`).
4. Draft:
   - discussion guides,
   - survey questionnaires,
   - instrumentation needs (if applicable).
5. Plan logistics:
   - timing,
   - responsibilities,
   - tools to use (recording, note‑taking, analysis).

Output:

- A **Research Plan** document (see `23-templates.md`).

---

#### Stage 3 – Recruit Participants & Prepare Data

**Goal:** Get the right people and data ready.

Activities:

1. Recruitment:
   - Define inclusion/exclusion criteria,
   - Work with:
     - panels,
     - internal CRM,
     - sales/support,
     - external agencies (if needed),
   - Ensure incentives, consent, and scheduling.
2. Data preparation:
   - Identify analytics events and dashboards needed,
   - Align with data/BI teams about:
     - queries,
     - segmentation,
     - sample extracts.

Ethics & compliance:

- Ensure:
  - consent flows,
  - data handling,
  - NDAs (if required),
follow legal and privacy policies (see `22-risks-ethics-and-compliance.md`).

Output:

- Recruitment list & invitations,
- confirmed sessions,
- ready access to key quantitative data.

---

#### Stage 4 – Collect Data (Qualitative & Quantitative)

**Goal:** Run the planned research and gather high‑quality data.

Qualitative:

- Conduct:
  - user interviews,
  - contextual inquiries or shadowing,
  - workshops,
  - usability tests.
- Practices:
  - Use guides but stay flexible,
  - Record sessions (if allowed),
  - Take structured notes (who, what, quotes, observations).

Quantitative:

- Run:
  - analytics queries,
  - funnel analyses,
  - segmentation,
  - surveys (if in scope).
- Ensure:
  - clear documentation of queries and definitions,
  - basic data quality sanity checks.

Tip:

- Involve PMs, designers, and engineers as **observers** where possible to build shared empathy.

---

#### Stage 5 – Synthesize & Model

**Goal:** Turn raw data into **insights, models, and artefacts**.

Activities:

1. Organize data:
   - Transcripts and notes into a repository (e.g., tagged by theme, segment),
   - Analytics findings into clear charts/tables.
2. Identify patterns:
   - Repeated pains and behaviors,
   - differences across segments,
   - correlations in metrics.
3. Create models:
   - Personas or segment profiles,
   - user journeys and service blueprints,
   - insight clusters (e.g., affinity maps).
4. Link to existing knowledge:
   - Compare with previous research,
   - Align with or adjust earlier hypotheses.

Output:

- A **set of models and insights** that are:
  - easy to explain,
  - directly connected to evidence.

---

#### Stage 6 – Communicate & Integrate

**Goal:** Ensure research **changes decisions and plans**, not just sits in a folder.

Activities:

1. Prepare **readouts** tailored to audiences:
   - short narrative summaries with:
     - key findings,
     - supporting quotes and data,
     - implications and recommendations.
2. Run sessions:
   - with product, design, engineering, business, risk/ops.
   - Encourage questions and challenges:
     - “What surprised you?”
     - “What will we change because of this?”
3. Integrate into downstream artefacts:
   - update problem statements and scopes as needed,
   - inform:
     - PRDs and requirement docs,
     - roadmap priorities,
     - success metrics.
4. Store and tag artefacts:
   - so they are findable and reusable later.

Output:

- Research readout(s),
- Updated problem definitions or requirements,
- Linked artefact set in your documentation space.

---

### C. Example Timelines

#### 1. Lightweight 1–2 Week Research Sprint

Use when:

- scope is narrow,
- risk is moderate,
- you mainly need **quick validation** or a few missing insights.

Outline:

- Days 1–2:
  - align on questions and decisions,
  - plan methods,
  - recruit a few participants.
- Days 3–5:
  - conduct 5–8 interviews or tests,
  - run targeted analytics queries.
- Days 6–7:
  - synthesize,
  - prepare a concise readout,
  - update problem statements / decisions.

---

#### 2. Full 4–6 Week Research Cycle (Enterprise / Bank)

Use when:

- problem is complex and high‑risk,
- multiple segments and systems involved,
- regulatory or strategic stakes are high.

Outline:

- Week 1:
  - intake and alignment,
  - research planning,
  - recruitment start,
  - analytics scoping.
- Weeks 2–3:
  - qualitative fieldwork (interviews, shadowing),
  - ongoing analytics and surveys.
- Weeks 4–5:
  - synthesis,
  - modelling (personas, journeys),
  - competitive analysis.
- Week 6:
  - readouts and iteration,
  - integration into strategy, problem statements, and requirements.
