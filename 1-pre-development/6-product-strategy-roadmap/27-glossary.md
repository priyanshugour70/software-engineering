## 27 – Glossary: Product Strategy & Roadmap Terms

This file defines key terms used across the **Product Strategy & Roadmap** module, in beginner‑friendly language.

Use it whenever you meet a word you don’t fully understand.

---

### A–C

**Capacity**  
Roughly, how much work teams can complete in a given time (e.g., per quarter). Depends on number of people, skills, and complexity of work.

**Case Study**  
Longer story describing a real or realistic situation, showing how concepts (like strategy and roadmapping) are applied in practice.

**Compliance**  
Following laws, regulations, and internal policies. In banking, includes things like KYC/AML, consumer credit rules, and data protection.

**Cost of Delay (CoD)**  
In WSJF, a way of representing how much value is lost or risk created by delaying a piece of work. Often approximated by combining business value, time criticality, and risk reduction/opportunity enablement.

**Customer (External)**  
People or organizations who use and pay for your products and services. In this module we often talk about SMEs (small and medium‑sized enterprises) as customers.

**Customer (Internal)**  
People inside your company (e.g., operations staff, call center agents) who use internal tools and platforms.

---

### D–F

**Decision Log**  
Simple record of important decisions, who made them, why, and what they impact. Helps future teams understand context and avoid repeating old discussions.

**Dependency**  
When one piece of work cannot be completed or deliver value until another piece is done. For example, a new lending journey depending on changes in a risk engine.

**Discovery**  
Learning activities (research, experiments, analysis) used to understand problems, opportunities, and possible solutions before full delivery.

**Epic**  
A sizable, coherent body of work within an initiative. Usually too big for a single sprint, but small enough to understand and deliver in phases. Often made up of multiple features or stories.

**Feasibility**  
Whether something can realistically be done, considering technology, operations, risk, regulation, and organization. Explored deeply in the `5-feasibility-study` module.

**Feature**  
Specific piece of product functionality that delivers value to a user (e.g., “upload documents for loan application”).

---

### G–I

**Governance**  
Structures and forums where decisions are made, priorities are set, and risks are reviewed. Includes things like portfolio reviews, risk committees, and steering groups.

**Horizon 1 / 2 / 3 (H1 / H2 / H3)**  
Time horizons used to structure roadmaps:
- H1 – near term (0–12 months) with more detail and commitment,  
- H2 – mid term (12–24 months) with directional plans,  
- H3 – long term (24+ months) with themes and options.

**Initiative**  
Large, outcome‑oriented piece of work that usually spans multiple epics and sprints. Tied to strategic themes and objectives (e.g., “Digital SME Onboarding v2”).

**Internal Platform**  
Shared technical system used by multiple products or teams (e.g., risk engine, KYC service, data platform). Often has its own roadmap and strategy.

---

### J–L

**Job Market (for Product Roles)**  
Overall demand for product managers, product leaders, and related roles, influenced by industry, geography, and required skills (see `15-job-market.md`).

**Key Result (KR)**  
Measurable outcome that shows whether you are achieving an objective. Part of the OKR framework.

**Legal (Function)**  
Team responsible for contracts, legal risk, terms & conditions, and interpreting laws that affect product and strategy decisions.

---

### M–O

**Multi‑Horizon Roadmap**  
Roadmap structure that uses time horizons (H1/H2/H3) instead of precise dates far into the future. Helps manage uncertainty honestly.

**Objective (in OKRs)**  
Qualitative goal describing what you want to achieve (e.g., “Make SME lending fast and transparent”).

**OKR (Objectives and Key Results)**  
Framework that connects goals (Objectives) with measurable outcomes (Key Results). Used here to connect strategy and themes to real‑world results.

**Outcome**  
Change in behavior, performance, or risk that indicates success (e.g., increased digital share of applications, reduced time to decision). Different from simple output (e.g., number of features shipped).

**Output**  
What you build or deliver (features, releases). Important, but only meaningful if it leads to desired outcomes.

---

### P–R

**Portfolio (Product Portfolio)**  
Group of related products, platforms, and initiatives managed together, usually serving a segment or journey (e.g., SME services portfolio).

**Prioritization Framework**  
Structured method (e.g., RICE, WSJF, value vs effort) to decide which initiatives or epics to do first, given limited capacity.

**Product Strategy**  
Set of choices about where and how a product will compete and deliver value, given context and constraints. Includes vision, pillars/themes, scope, differentiators, and key bets.

**Product Vision**  
Concise, inspiring description of what the product aims to be for its users and why it matters.

**Regulation / Regulated Product**  
Product or activity that must comply with specific laws and rules, overseen by regulators (e.g., lending, payments). Strongly influences strategy and roadmaps.

**Risk Appetite**  
Amount and type of risk an organization is willing to accept to achieve its goals. Important input into strategy for financial products.

**Roadmap**  
Time‑phased view of initiatives and epics, showing what work is planned in what sequence to move towards strategy and outcomes. Multiple levels and views exist for different audiences.

---

### S–U

**Scenario**  
Short, concrete example situation showing how strategy and roadmapping concepts apply in practice (see `21-real-world-examples.md`).

**Stakeholder**  
Anyone with a legitimate interest in product outcomes and roadmaps (e.g., business owners, risk, operations, technology, regulators).

**Strategic Theme / Pillar**  
High‑level area of focus that connects strategy to portfolios, roadmaps, and day‑to‑day work (e.g., “Frictionless Experience,” “Risk & Compliance,” “Platform Resilience”).

**Trade‑Off**  
Explicit choice to prioritize one thing over another (e.g., spending more capacity on regulatory work this year, delaying some growth features).

**Uncertainty**  
Unknowns about how users, markets, technology, or regulation will behave. Managed via horizons, experiments, and regular roadmap reviews.

---

### V–Z

**Value Stream**  
End‑to‑end flow of value for a customer or internal user (e.g., from loan application to disbursement and servicing). Sometimes used as a basis for organizing teams and funding.

**WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First)**  
Prioritization technique that orders work by the ratio of Cost of Delay to Job Size. Higher WSJF score means higher priority.
