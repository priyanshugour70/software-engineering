## 12 – Skills for Requirements Engineering

This file explains the **skills** needed to do Requirements Engineering well, especially in complex and regulated environments.

It is useful for:

- PMs/POs,
- Business Analysts / Requirements Engineers,
- Tech leads and QA leads who engage deeply in requirements work.

---

### 1. Skill Categories Overview

Key categories:

1. **Product & Domain Understanding**
2. **Analysis & Modelling**
3. **Communication & Facilitation**
4. **Technical & Systems Awareness**
5. **Quality & Testing Mindset**
6. **Risk, Compliance & Governance Awareness**

---

### 2. Product & Domain Understanding

Abilities:

- understand:
  - user needs and journeys,
  - business processes and objectives,
  - domain rules (e.g., lending policies, KYC rules).

Skills:

- reading and interpreting:
  - research findings,
  - market and business documents.
- asking good questions:
  - “What problem is this solving?”
  - “For whom?”
  - “How does it fit in the bigger journey?”

This ensures:

- requirements are not just “feature lists,”
- but grounded in real needs and strategy.

---

### 3. Analysis & Modelling

Abilities:

- break down:
  - complex problems into smaller, understandable requirements.
- model:
  - flows,
  - states,
  - rules,
  - data.

Skills:

- process modelling:
  - using simple flow diagrams or BPMN (where needed).
- data thinking:
  - identifying entities, attributes, relationships relevant to requirements.
- structuring PRDs:
  - clear sections,
  - consistent IDs,
  - readable narratives.

These skills are central for Business Analysts, but valuable for PMs too.

---

### 4. Communication & Facilitation

Abilities:

- communicate:
  - requirements clearly to mixed audiences.
- facilitate:
  - refinement sessions,
  - PRD reviews,
  - cross‑functional workshops.

Skills:

- plain‑language writing:
  - explain complex rules simply,
  - avoid jargon or over‑technical language when not needed.
- listening and summarizing:
  - capturing what stakeholders say,
  - reflecting it back accurately.
- conflict navigation:
  - when stakeholders disagree on requirements, help:
    - surface assumptions,
    - find acceptable trade‑offs.

---

### 5. Technical & Systems Awareness

You don’t have to be an engineer, but:

- you need enough understanding to:
  - discuss architecture options,
  - understand constraints,
  - write realistic NFRs.

Skills:

- understanding:
  - basic system components (UI, services, databases, integrations),
  - synchronous vs asynchronous flows,
  - key platform capabilities and limitations.
- reading:
  - high‑level architecture diagrams,
  - API definitions (at least at a conceptual level).

This helps ensure:

- requirements are implementable,
- NFRs are sensible.

---

### 6. Quality & Testing Mindset

Abilities:

- think like a tester:
  - “How could this break?”
  - “What edge cases are we missing?”

Skills:

- writing:
  - good acceptance criteria (Given/When/Then),
  - scenarios for both positive and negative cases.
- collaborating:
  - with QA and test automation,
  - to ensure coverage.
- understanding:
  - basics of test types:
    - unit, integration, system, regression,
    - performance and security tests.

This mindset:

- leads to fewer bugs escaping to production,
- and smoother acceptance.

---

### 7. Risk, Compliance & Governance Awareness

Especially critical in enterprises/banks.

Abilities:

- be aware of:
  - regulatory and risk requirements that impact behaviour and NFRs.
- anticipate:
  - where governance checks will occur,
  - what documentation and traceability will be needed.

Skills:

- reading:
  - high‑level policy or regulatory summaries,
  - and asking experts for interpretation.
- capturing:
  - controls and logging requirements in PRDs/NFRs.
- participating in:
  - risk/compliance reviews with confidence.

---

### 8. Growing These Skills

If you are newer to Requirements Engineering:

- **0–6 months**:
  - focus on:
    - clear writing,
    - user stories and acceptance criteria,
    - learning the domain and basic flows.
- **6–18 months**:
  - deepen:
    - NFR understanding,
    - systems and data literacy,
    - facilitation of multi‑stakeholder sessions.
- **18+ months**:
  - take on:
    - complex initiatives with many dependencies,
    - regulatory-heavy changes,
    - mentoring others on requirements practices.

Practical actions:

- shadow:
  - experienced PMs/BAs in workshops and reviews.
- practice:
  - writing draft PRDs and stories for smaller features.
- ask:
  - QA, architects, and risk partners for feedback on your requirements.

---

### 9. Beginner Checklist

- [ ] Do you feel comfortable:
  - [ ] explaining the product problem and context?
  - [ ] writing user stories and acceptance criteria?
  - [ ] having basic conversations about NFRs with engineers?
- [ ] Are you:
  - [ ] involving QA, design, risk, and ops when shaping requirements?
  - [ ] able to navigate disagreements constructively?

If not, this module gives you many concrete examples and patterns to practice with.
