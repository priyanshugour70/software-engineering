## 03 – Workflow: Step‑by‑Step Business Case & ROI

This file describes a **practical workflow** to go from inputs (problems, research, rough solution ideas) to a **decision‑ready business case**.

We cover:

- A. High‑level stages
- B. Detailed steps
- C. Example timelines

---

### A. High‑Level Stages

1. **Intake & Framing**
2. **Identify Value Drivers & Cost Drivers**
3. **Estimate Benefits & Costs**
4. **Build Financial Model**
5. **Analyze Risk, Uncertainty & Options**
6. **Draft & Review Business Case**
7. **Decision & Follow‑Up**

You can iterate between 2–5 as you refine assumptions and options.

---

### B. Detailed Steps

#### Stage 1 – Intake & Framing

**Goal:** Ensure the business case is grounded in **clear problems, scope, and strategy**.

Inputs:

- latest problem statement(s),
- research findings,
- preliminary solution concepts and scope options,
- strategic goals / OKRs.

Activities:

1. Clarify the **initiative definition**:
   - What user problems and opportunities does it address?
   - What scope and solution options are in play?
2. Identify **strategic links**:
   - Which objectives/OKRs will this support?
   - Is it mandatory (e.g., regulatory) or discretionary?
3. Align stakeholders:
   - PM, business owner, finance, strategy/portfolio.

Output:

- a short **business case brief** describing:
  - purpose,
  - scope options,
  - strategic context.

---

#### Stage 2 – Identify Value Drivers & Cost Drivers

**Goal:** Understand *how* this initiative could create value and *what* will drive costs.

Activities:

1. **Value drivers** (see `05-benefit-types-and-estimation.md`):
   - Revenue:
     - increased volume?
     - higher conversion?
     - new products?
   - Cost:
     - fewer manual steps?
     - fewer errors and rework?
   - Risk:
     - lower fraud losses?
     - fewer fines?
2. **Cost drivers** (see `06-cost-types-and-estimation.md`):
   - build complexity (systems, integrations),
   - operational impact (support, monitoring),
   - third‑party and licensing needs,
   - change management (training, communications).

Output:

- a **driver map** listing:
  - key value levers,
  - key cost levers,
for use in estimation and modeling.

---

#### Stage 3 – Estimate Benefits & Costs

**Goal:** Turn drivers into **quantitative estimates** (with ranges where needed).

Activities:

1. Estimate **benefits**:
   - use baselines from analytics and BI (`09-data-sources.md`),
   - apply reasonable, justified changes:
     - e.g., “conversion improves from 10% → 14% in base case”.
   - document:
     - assumptions,
     - sources,
     - benchmarks.
2. Estimate **costs**:
   - collaborate with:
     - engineering,
     - operations,
     - vendors,
   - distinguish:
     - one‑time vs ongoing,
     - internal vs external.
3. For each estimate, capture:
   - low/base/high values (for scenarios),
   - confidence level (e.g., high/medium/low).

Output:

- a **benefit & cost estimation sheet** with:
  - line items,
  - amounts,
  - timing,
  - assumptions.

---

#### Stage 4 – Build Financial Model

**Goal:** Translate estimates into **multi‑year cash‑flow and ROI metrics**.

Activities:

1. Choose **time horizon** (e.g., 3–5 years).
2. Build a **cash‑flow model** (see `04-financial-metrics-and-formulas.md`):
   - year‑by‑year:
     - revenue impact,
     - cost savings,
     - incremental costs.
3. Calculate:
   - net cash flows per period,
   - cumulative cash flows (for payback),
   - ROI over period,
   - NPV and/or IRR (for larger investments, using agreed discount rate).

Output:

- a **base‑case financial model** with key metrics.

---

#### Stage 5 – Analyze Risk, Uncertainty & Options

**Goal:** Understand **how fragile or robust** the case is, and compare options.

Activities:

1. Create **scenarios**:
   - conservative (lower adoption, slower rollout),
   - base (most likely),
   - aggressive (higher adoption, strong cross‑sell).
2. Perform **sensitivity analysis**:
   - vary 1–2 key drivers at a time:
     - adoption rate,
     - conversion,
     - implementation cost.
   - note impact on:
     - ROI,
     - NPV,
     - payback.
3. Compare **solution options** (see `17-option-and-scenario-comparison.md`):
   - MVP vs full scope,
   - single‑country vs multi‑country rollout,
   - internal build vs vendor.

Output:

- a set of **scenario views** and **option comparisons** with commentary.

---

#### Stage 6 – Draft & Review Business Case

**Goal:** Create a **clear document** and align stakeholders before formal decisions.

Activities:

1. Draft business case summary (see `10-documents-and-artifacts.md` and `23-templates.md`):
   - background & objectives,
   - benefits & costs,
   - financial metrics,
   - risks & dependencies,
   - recommendation.
2. Review iteratively with:
   - finance,
   - business owner,
   - strategy/portfolio,
   - tech leaders,
   - risk/compliance (where relevant).
3. Refine:
   - assumptions,
   - narrative,
   - clarity of scenarios and recommendations.

Output:

- a **reviewed business case document** ready for committee or leadership decision.

---

#### Stage 7 – Decision & Follow‑Up

**Goal:** Get a **formal decision** and set up tracking for later.

Activities:

1. Present to:
   - investment or portfolio committees,
   - executive leadership,
   - or equivalent decision bodies.
2. Capture:
   - decision (approve/adjust/reject),
   - conditions or stage‑gates,
   - funding envelope and phasing.
3. Translate into:
   - portfolio and roadmap updates,
   - high‑level KPIs and financial targets,
   - linkage to:
     - OKRs,
     - benefit realization tracking.

Output:

- **decision record** and:
  - initial targets for post‑launch evaluation.

---

### C. Example Timelines

#### 1. Lightweight 1–2 Week Business Case

Use when:

- initiative is small/moderate,
- decision is reversible,
- risk is limited.

Outline:

- Days 1–2:
  - intake & framing,
  - identify drivers,
  - rough estimations.
- Days 3–4:
  - build base‑case model,
  - quick scenarios.
- Days 5–7:
  - draft document,
  - fast reviews,
  - decision.

---

#### 2. Full 4–6 Week Business Case (Enterprise / Bank)

Use when:

- initiative is large,
- multi‑year,
- cross‑organizational,
- with regulatory or strategic significance.

Outline:

- Weeks 1–2:
  - intake,
  - driver identification,
  - data collection,
  - benefit/cost estimation.
- Weeks 3–4:
  - model building,
  - scenario & sensitivity analysis,
  - option comparisons.
- Weeks 5–6:
  - document drafting,
  - stakeholder reviews,
  - committee presentations and decision.
