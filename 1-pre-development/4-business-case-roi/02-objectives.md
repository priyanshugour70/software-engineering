## 02 – Objectives of Business Case & ROI Analysis

This file explains **what success looks like** for this phase.

---

### 1. Primary Objective (One Sentence)

The main objective is to create a **clear, evidence‑based, and decision‑ready investment case** that:

- quantifies benefits and costs,
- makes risks and assumptions explicit,
- and supports **transparent go/no‑go and prioritization decisions**.

---

### 2. Key Outcomes You Should Achieve

#### 2.1 Credible Benefit Estimates

You should have:

- **explicit, quantified estimates** (with ranges where appropriate) of:
  - revenue uplift,
  - cost savings,
  - risk reduction,
  - plus documented intangible benefits.
- clear explanation of:
  - key **drivers** (e.g., adoption, conversion, ARPU).

Outcome:

- stakeholders understand:
  - **how** benefits might materialize,
  - and **what needs to be true** for them to happen.

---

#### 2.2 Transparent Cost & Investment Profile

You should:

- list and estimate:
  - one‑time build costs,
  - ongoing run/support costs,
  - change management and training,
  - external vendors and licenses.
- show:
  - timing of major cost components.

Outcome:

- no major **hidden costs**;
- finance and technology teams agree on **order‑of‑magnitude**.

---

#### 2.3 Financial Metrics & Ranges

You should calculate:

- at least:
  - payback period,
  - high‑level ROI over a chosen time horizon.
- for larger initiatives:
  - NPV and/or IRR,
  - scenario‑based projections (conservative/base/aggressive).

Outcome:

- decision‑makers see:
  - **expected returns**,
  - and how they vary under different assumptions.

---

#### 2.4 Risks, Dependencies & Sensitivities Clearly Articulated

You should identify:

- key **risks**:
  - delivery,
  - adoption,
  - regulatory,
  - operational,
  - vendor.
- key **dependencies**:
  - on other projects,
  - on external events (e.g., regulatory deadlines).
- **sensitivity** of results to assumptions:
  - what happens if adoption is 50% of target?
  - what if costs overrun by 20%?

Outcome:

- leaders understand:
  - upside, downside,
  - and main uncertainty drivers.

---

#### 2.5 Clear Recommendation & Portfolio Context

You should deliver:

- a **recommendation**:
  - proceed now, proceed later, reduce scope, or stop,
  - with rationale.
- a view of:
  - where this initiative sits in the **wider portfolio**:
    - relative strategic importance,
    - relative ROI vs risk,
    - capacity constraints.

Outcome:

- portfolio and investment committees can:
  - position this initiative among others,
  - not just evaluate it in isolation.

---

### 3. Secondary Objectives

#### 3.1 Build a Reusable Model

The business case model should:

- be **updateable**:
  - as new data comes in (from pilots, experiments, market changes).
- be understandable to:
  - finance, PMs, and key stakeholders.

Outcome:

- you can:
  - refine the case over time,
  - track actuals vs forecasts.

---

#### 3.2 Improve Financial Literacy Across Product & Tech Teams

Through this work:

- PMs, designers, engineers should:
  - better understand:
    - unit economics,
    - cost drivers,
    - basic financial metrics.

Outcome:

- future product decisions can be made with:
  - more financial awareness,
  - more realistic expectations.

---

### 4. What “Good” vs “Bad” Business Cases Look Like

#### 4.1 “Good”

- Benefits:
  - tied to:
    - specific drivers (volume, price, conversion, cost per case),
    - and grounded in historical data or reasonable benchmarks.
- Costs:
  - include:
    - build,
    - run,
    - change,
    - vendor,
    not just “engineering effort”.
- Assumptions:
  - clearly listed,
  - with sources and rationale.
- Risks:
  - openly discussed,
  - with mitigation ideas.
- Recommendation:
  - clear, not just “maybe / depends”.

#### 4.2 “Bad”

- Single “hockey‑stick” revenue number with:
  - no explanation of drivers.
- Ignored or hand‑waved costs.
- No mention of:
  - downside scenarios,
  - adoption risk,
  - regulatory or operational risk.
- Highly optimistic story that:
  - doesn’t match historical experience.

---

### 5. Checklist: Have We Met the Objectives?

Before sending a business case for approval, check:

- **Benefits**
  - [ ] Are benefit categories clearly identified (revenue, cost, risk, intangible)?
  - [ ] Are key benefit numbers connected to clear drivers and assumptions?
- **Costs**
  - [ ] Do we cover:
    - build, run, change, vendor costs?
  - [ ] Are major uncertainties acknowledged?
- **Financial metrics**
  - [ ] Are ROI/payback (and NPV/IRR for large initiatives) calculated?
  - [ ] Are there at least 2–3 scenarios?
- **Risks & sensitivities**
  - [ ] Are top 5–10 risks identified with potential impact?
  - [ ] Are sensitivity analyses shown for key drivers?
- **Recommendation & portfolio**
  - [ ] Is there a clear recommended path?
  - [ ] Is the initiative positioned relative to others (if portfolio view is available)?

If many boxes are unchecked, refine the analysis before presenting it.
