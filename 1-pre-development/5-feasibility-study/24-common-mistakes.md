## 24 – Common Mistakes in Feasibility Studies

This file highlights **frequent pitfalls** and how to avoid them.

We group mistakes into:

- A. Mindset errors
- B. Process errors
- C. Collaboration and governance errors

---

### A. Mindset Errors

#### 1. Treating Feasibility as Formality

Pattern:

- assuming:
  - “it’s just a box to tick,”
  - and not challenging solution ideas.

Fix:

- treat Feasibility as:
  - a serious risk‑reduction step,
  - not rubber‑stamping.

---

#### 2. Solution Bias

Pattern:

- starting with **one chosen solution** and:
  - only asking “Is this feasible?”,
  without exploring alternatives.

Fix:

- explore at least:
  - 2–3 option variants,
  - compare feasibility and risk trade‑offs.

---

### B. Process Errors

#### 3. Over- or Under-Scoping

Pattern:

- analyzing:
  - either too narrow (missing major dependencies),
  - or too broad (boiling the ocean).

Fix:

- define:
  - clear Feasibility scope in a charter,
  - adjust if necessary but avoid uncontrolled sprawl.

---

#### 4. Skipping Current-State Analysis

Pattern:

- proposing:
  - future architectures or processes,
  - without understanding existing ones.

Fix:

- always:
  - map current systems and processes first.

---

#### 5. Failing to Capture Risks & Constraints Clearly

Pattern:

- discussing risks verbally,
  - but not recording them:
    - owners,
    - mitigations.

Fix:

- maintain:
  - a structured risk register and constraint log (`22-risk-register-and-mitigation.md`).

---

### C. Collaboration & Governance Errors

#### 6. Not Involving Security, Legal, or Risk Early

Pattern:

- bringing:
  - security or legal only at the end,
  - leading to late blockers.

Fix:

- include them:
  - from Stage 1 (intake) and Stage 2 (baseline).

---

#### 7. Ignoring Operational Reality

Pattern:

- assuming:
  - ops/support will “figure it out later”.

Fix:

- involve ops early,
- explicitly assess:
  - capacity,
  - process changes,
  - training needs.

---

#### 8. No Clear Decision or Next Steps

Pattern:

- Feasibility work ends with:
  - “some risks exist,”
  but:
  - no clear recommendation or plan.

Fix:

- always:
  - conclude with:
    - recommended option(s),
    - conditions and mitigations,
    - actions and owners.
