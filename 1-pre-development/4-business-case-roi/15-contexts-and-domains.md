## 15 – Contexts and Domains for Business Case & ROI

Business Case & ROI principles apply everywhere, but **context affects emphasis**.

This file highlights differences across:

- A. Banking & financial services
- B. SaaS and digital products
- C. Operations-heavy & regulated domains

---

### A. Banking & Financial Services

Characteristics:

- Capital‑intensive,
- heavily regulated,
- multiple risk types (credit, market, operational, compliance).

Business case focus:

- **Risk-adjusted returns**
  - not just profit, but risk‑weighted profit.
- **Regulatory & mandatory investments**
  - some initiatives are non‑discretionary (compliance).
- **Capital allocation**
  - constrained by:
    - capital requirements,
    - risk appetite,
    - regulatory limits.

Implications:

- risk and compliance teams deeply involved,
- strong expectation for:
  - scenario analysis,
  - stress testing,
  - clear linkage to risk reduction.

---

### B. SaaS and Digital Products

Characteristics:

- recurring revenue models,
- strong focus on:
  - growth,
  - retention,
  - unit economics (e.g., LTV/CAC).

Business case focus:

- **Growth and retention levers**
  - how initiatives impact:
    - activation,
    - expansion,
    - churn.
- **Scalability**
  - marginal costs of serving more customers.

Implications:

- CAC, LTV, gross margin, and churn are key metrics,
- experimentation (A/B testing) and cohort analyses often feed into cases.

---

### C. Operations-Heavy & Regulated Domains

Examples:

- utilities,
- telecoms,
- healthcare,
- transportation.

Characteristics:

- large physical and process footprints,
- service reliability and safety critical,
- regulatory service obligations.

Business case focus:

- **Operational efficiency** and reliability,
- **CapEx optimization** (infrastructure investments),
- **Regulatory compliance**.

Implications:

- strong involvement from:
  - operations,
  - engineering,
  - regulatory affairs.
- long time horizons and large investments,
- more emphasis on:
  - NPV,
  - scenario planning,
  - risk analysis.

---

### What Stays the Same

Regardless of domain:

- benefits, costs, and risks must be:
  - identified,
  - estimated,
  - and communicated clearly.
- decisions should be:
  - transparent about assumptions,
  - re‑evaluated when new data emerges.
