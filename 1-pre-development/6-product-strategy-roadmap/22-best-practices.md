## 22 – Best Practices for Product Strategy & Roadmaps

This file summarizes **best practices** for doing Product Strategy & Roadmap work well, especially in complex organizations and regulated environments.

Think of it as a **pattern library** of things you want to see in your ways of working.

---

### 1. Anchor Everything in Outcomes

- Define:
  - clear objectives and outcome metrics (see `05-strategic-themes-goals-and-okrs.md`).
- Map:
  - every initiative/epic to:
    - a theme,
    - and at least one outcome.
- Talk about:
  - “what changes for customers, business, or risk,”
  - more than “we will ship feature X.”

Result:

- roadmaps reflect **impact**, not just output.

---

### 2. Use Multi‑Horizon Roadmaps

- Separate:
  - near‑term commitments (H1),
  - mid‑term likely bets (H2),
  - longer‑term options and themes (H3).
- Precision:
  - high for near term,
  - decreasing for further horizons.
- Communicate:
  - uncertainty levels explicitly.

Result:

- honest plans,
- less fake precision,
- better expectation management.

---

### 3. Make Themes the Backbone of Planning

- Keep themes:
  - small in number (3–7),
  - stable for 1–2 years.
- Use themes to:
  - structure roadmaps,
  - allocate capacity,
  - define OKRs,
  - group initiatives and epics.
- Regularly review:
  - whether themes still match strategy and context.

Result:

- coherent, focused investments instead of scattered work.

---

### 4. Co‑Create Strategy with Key Partners

Include:

- product,
- engineering and architecture,
- business/P&L owners,
- risk/compliance,
- operations,
- data/analytics,
- sometimes finance.

Practices:

- joint workshops for:
  - vision and themes,
  - initiative shaping,
  - prioritization and capacity alignment.
- use whiteboards (`Miro`, `FigJam`) plus structured docs (`Confluence`, `Notion`).

Result:

- strategies and roadmaps that:
  - are realistic,
  - respect constraints,
  - have broad buy‑in.

---

### 5. Visualize Dependencies and Enablers

- Maintain:
  - cross‑team dependency maps (see `10-dependencies-and-sequencing.md`).
- Highlight:
  - enabling platform/risk/ops work,
  - as first‑class roadmap items.
- Use:
  - program boards,
  - swimlane roadmaps,
  - diagrams that show arrows and sequencing.

Result:

- fewer surprises,
- better sequencing,
- smoother delivery.

---

### 6. Treat Roadmaps as Living Artifacts

- Set:
  - clear review cadences (monthly/quarterly) (see `24-governance-alignment-and-cadence.md`).
- At each review:
  - examine:
    - outcomes vs plan,
    - new constraints or opportunities,
    - regulatory changes.
  - adjust:
    - priorities,
    - horizons,
    - initiatives.
- Document:
  - changes and rationales in decision logs (`12-documents-and-artifacts.md`).

Result:

- plans that adapt to reality,
- less frustration about “frozen” roadmaps.

---

### 7. Keep Docs Short, Clear, and Linked

- Strategy docs:
  - 3–5 pages of core content,
  - appendices for details and analysis.
- Roadmap docs:
  - focused on:
    - themes,
    - initiatives,
    - timelines/horizons.
- Use links:
  - to research,
  - business cases,
  - feasibility outputs,
  - detailed designs.

Result:

- easier to read and maintain,
- more likely to be used.

---

### 8. Integrate Risk & Compliance from the Start

- Invite:
  - risk/compliance to early discovery and strategy sessions.
- Ask:
  - “What guardrails and constraints do we need to design for?”
- Make:
  - risk and compliance work visible on the roadmap.

Result:

- fewer late blockers,
- strategies and roadmaps that can actually go live in regulated environments.

---

### 9. Balance Feature, Platform, and Risk Work

- Use capacity allocations (see `09-capacity-planning-and-funding-models.md`):
  - e.g., 50% growth, 25% platform, 25% risk & compliance.
- Ensure:
  - each category has clearly articulated initiatives and outcomes.
- Avoid:
  - “all growth, no foundations,”
  - or “all foundations, no user‑visible value.”

Result:

- sustainable progress,
- fewer crises from neglected platform or risk areas.

---

### 10. Start Simple, Then Evolve

- If your organization is new to structured strategy & roadmapping:
  - start with:
    - a simple vision statement,
    - 3–5 themes,
    - a single roadmap view.
- Over time:
  - add:
    - better metrics,
    - multiple views (portfolio vs product vs team),
    - more refined governance.

Result:

- less resistance,
- smoother cultural change,
- incremental improvement instead of big‑bang process overhauls.

---

### 11. Use Strategy & Roadmaps as Conversation Tools

- Treat documents and roadmaps as:
  - **starting points for discussion**, not final answers.
- Encourage:
  - feedback,
  - challenges,
  - alternative scenarios.
- Be ready to:
  - walk stakeholders through:
    - trade‑offs,
    - risks,
    - assumptions.

Result:

- better shared understanding,
- stronger decisions,
- more resilient plans.

---

### 12. Beginner Checklist

- [ ] Do our strategy and roadmap:
  - [ ] clearly express outcomes and metrics?
  - [ ] use multi‑horizon views?
  - [ ] show themes and capacity allocation?
- [ ] Are key partners (engineering, risk, ops, finance) truly involved?
- [ ] Are dependencies and enablers visible?
- [ ] Is there a regular review cadence where we adjust the plan?

If multiple answers are “no,” pick 1–2 best practices from this file to start implementing first.
