## 01 – Concepts: What Is User & Market Research?

### 1. Simple Definition (For Complete Beginners)

**User & Market Research** is how you **learn from real people and real data** so you’re not guessing when you build products.

It answers questions like:

- Who are our users and customers really?
- What are they trying to get done?
- How do they behave today, and why?
- What alternatives and competitors do they use?
- What evidence do we have that our problem framing is correct?

Without research, teams:

- rely on **opinions and assumptions**,
- build features that **miss the mark**,
- and are surprised later when adoption is low.

In the pre‑development flow:

1. `1-market-opportunity-discovery/`
   - Finds **where value might exist**.

So this phase is about **reducing uncertainty** and **increasing understanding** before committing to solutions.


### 3. Key Concepts You Must Know

#### 3.1 User vs Customer vs Stakeholder

- **User**
  - Person who **actually uses** the product or service.
  - Example: SME owner using online banking.

- **Customer**
  - Person or organization that **pays** or owns the contract.
  - May be same as user or different (e.g., a company vs employees).

- **Stakeholder**
  - Anyone with an interest in or impact on success:
    - internal teams (ops, risk, sales),
    - partners (accountants, resellers).

Research should cover:

- not only end users,
- but also **other stakeholders** who shape behavior and decisions.

---

#### 3.2 Qualitative vs Quantitative Research

- **Qualitative**
  - Small numbers of participants.
  - Deep conversations and observations.
  - Helps you understand **why** things happen.
  - Examples:
    - interviews,
    - shadowing,
    - usability tests.

- **Quantitative**
  - Larger samples or full datasets.
  - Numbers, metrics, statistics.
  - Helps you understand **how often, how many, how big**.
  - Examples:
    - product analytics,
    - surveys,
    - transaction data.

Good research uses **both**:

- Qualitative → generate and explain hypotheses.
- Quantitative → confirm and size them.

---

#### 3.3 Primary vs Secondary Research

- **Primary research**
  - You (or your team) collect data directly:
    - interviews,
    - surveys,
    - experiments,
    - analytics queries.

- **Secondary research**
  - You use existing sources:
    - previous studies,
    - internal reports,
    - public research,
    - industry/analyst reports.

You should usually:

- start with **secondary** (what do we already know?),
- then design **primary** research to fill the gaps.

---

#### 3.4 Descriptive vs Evaluative vs Generative Research

- **Descriptive**
  - Describes:
    - who users are,
    - what they do,
    - current journeys.

- **Evaluative**
  - Assesses:
    - how well a concept, prototype, or existing product works.

- **Generative**
  - Explores:
    - new ideas and opportunity spaces,
    - emergent needs and behaviors.

In this phase, you may use all three, but typically:

- earlier focus on **descriptive and generative** to understand the space,
- later **evaluative** to fine‑tune solutions.


### 4. What User & Market Research Is Not

It is **not**:

- “asking people what features they want” and building exactly that.
- a one‑off survey to justify a pre‑decided solution.
- a set of pretty personas that nobody uses.

Good research is:

- tied to **decisions**,
- **iterative**,
- and focused on understanding **problems, behaviors, and trade‑offs**, not just wish lists.


### 5. Example: Applying Concepts to SME Digital Lending

Given a Problem Definition like:

> “SME owners in Country X experience a slow, opaque loan application process, leading to delays and high abandonment.”

Research might ask:

- **Descriptive**:
  - How do SMEs currently apply?
  - Which steps cause confusion or delay?
  - What are their mental models of “loan approval” and risk?
- **Generative**:
  - What does an ideal experience look like to them?
  - How do they balance speed vs safety vs transparency?
- **Evaluative** (later):
  - Do proposed flows and messages actually improve understanding and trust?

Quantitative:

- How many SMEs start and complete applications?
- How many call support, and for what reasons?

Qualitative:

- Interviews and shadowing:
  - follow a few SMEs end‑to‑end,
  - talk to relationship managers and ops staff.


### 6. Mental Models for Beginners

#### 6.1 “Research as a Flashlight”

- It **does not** decide for you.
- It **shows you what’s there**:
  - user realities,
  - constraints,
  - unexpected patterns.

You still need:

- judgment,
- strategy,
- and prioritization to act on what you see.

---

#### 6.2 “Decision-Driven Research”

Before doing any research, always ask:

- “What **decision** will this help us make?”
- “What would we do **differently** depending on what we learn?”

If you cannot answer, your research may be:

- too generic,
- or not actionable.
