## 06 – Data Sources for Market Opportunity Discovery

This file lists **where to find data and signals** inside and outside your organization.  
Use it as a **catalog** and a **checklist** when planning discovery work.

We group data sources into:

- A. Internal customer & product data
- B. Internal business & operational data
- C. External market & competitor data
- D. Regulatory & policy data (especially for banks)
- E. How to prioritize and combine sources

---

### A. Internal Customer & Product Data

These sources help you understand **how customers behave** and **what they experience** with your current products.

#### 1. Product Analytics Platforms

Examples: Amplitude, Mixpanel, Pendo, in‑house tracking.

**What you can get**

- Funnel conversion rates (e.g., onboarding completion).
- Feature usage frequencies and patterns.
- Session lengths and event sequences.
- Retention and engagement metrics by cohort.

**How it helps discovery**

- Reveals **bottlenecks** (e.g., many users dropping at KYC step).
- Highlights **popular workarounds** (e.g., users exporting data often).
- Shows **differences by segment or region**.

---

#### 2. Support & Ticketing Systems

Examples: Zendesk, ServiceNow, Freshdesk, in‑house tools.

**What you can get**

- Volume of tickets by category / product / segment.
- Recurrent complaint types and top issues.
- Time‑to‑resolution and escalation patterns.

**How it helps**

- Identifies **painful parts of current journeys**.
- Surfaces **unmet expectations** (“I expected to do this online but couldn’t”).

---

#### 3. NPS / CSAT Surveys & Feedback

Examples: Medallia, Qualtrics, in‑house feedback forms.

**What you can get**

- NPS / CSAT scores by segment, product, or journey.
- Free‑text comments with rich qualitative insights.

**How it helps**

- Highlights **where customers are delighted vs frustrated**.
- Good source of **direct quotes** for opportunity briefs.

---

#### 4. CRM & Sales Systems

Examples: Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics, in‑house CRM.

**What you can get**

- Pipeline and win/loss data.
- Reasons for lost deals (when captured).
- Segmentation details and account attributes.

**How it helps**

- Shows **which needs cause customers to choose competitors**.
- Helps estimate **commercial impact** of solving certain problems.

---

### B. Internal Business & Operational Data

These sources help you understand **business performance** and **operational reality**.

#### 1. Financial & Performance Dashboards

Examples: Tableau, Power BI, Looker, in‑house BI.

**What you can get**

- Revenue and margin by product / segment / region.
- Cost‑to‑serve per customer or per transaction.
- Growth and churn rates.

**How it helps**

- Identifies **underperforming products** that might hide opportunities.
- Helps size **value potential** of opportunity areas.

---

#### 2. Operational Metrics

Includes:

- Processing times (e.g., loan approval time, payment settlement time).
- Error or failure rates.
- Backlog sizes and SLA breaches.

**How it helps**

- Reveals **inefficiencies** that may be opportunities (e.g., digitization, automation).
- Connects customer pain (waiting, uncertainty) with **internal process issues**.

---

#### 3. Risk, Compliance & Audit Reports

**What you can get**

- Findings from internal or external audits.
- Identified control gaps or process weaknesses.
- Areas where new controls or capabilities are needed.

**How it helps**

- Surfaces **regulatory‑driven opportunities**, such as:
  - building better monitoring systems,
  - providing transparency tools to customers.

---

### C. External Market & Competitor Data

These sources help you understand **the world outside your organization**.

#### 1. Analyst & Industry Reports

Examples: McKinsey, BCG, Gartner, Forrester, local industry associations.

**What you can get**

- Market size and growth projections.
- Emerging trends and technologies.
- Benchmarks and maturity models.

**How it helps**

- Supports **top‑down sizing** of opportunity spaces.
- Informs **timing** (e.g., “this space is heating up now”).

---

#### 2. Competitor Product Analysis

Sources:

- Competitor websites and product pages.
- Public documentation, demos, webinars.
- App store listings and reviews.

**What you can get**

- Feature sets and pricing models.
- Positioning and messaging.
- Customer complaints and praise in reviews.

**How it helps**

- Identifies **table stakes** vs potential **differentiators**.
- Spot **white spaces** competitors haven’t addressed.

---

#### 3. News, Blogs, and Social Media

Sources:

- Fintech / industry blogs.
- Professional networks (e.g., LinkedIn posts).
- Forums and community discussions.

**How it helps**

- Reveals **emerging topics** and **unmet needs discussed in public**.
- Shows where **market narrative** is heading.

---

### D. Regulatory & Policy Data (Critical in Banking)

Banks and regulated entities must closely track:

- Laws and regulatory acts (e.g., PSD2, GDPR, open banking rules).
- Central bank circulars and guidance.
- Industry consultation papers.

**Where to look**

- Regulator websites and official bulletins.
- Central bank publications.
- Legal / compliance summary memos.

**How it helps**

- Identifies **mandatory changes** (risks) that require solutions.
- Reveals **new permissioned activities** (opportunities), such as:
  - data access under open banking,
  - new payment scheme participation.

---

### E. How to Prioritize and Combine Sources

Because you cannot examine everything in depth, use a simple strategy:

1. **Start with internal data that is easy to access**
   - Product analytics dashboards
   - Support ticket dashboards
   - High‑level financial performance views
2. **Add targeted qualitative inputs**
   - 3–5 stakeholder interviews
   - A small number of customer conversations
3. **Overlay external context**
   - 1–2 key industry reports
   - Quick competitor scan
4. **Pull in regulatory input where relevant**
   - For any domain affecting compliance or licensing.

This combination will usually give you **enough evidence** to craft and prioritize strong opportunity hypotheses.

---

### F. Example “Data Package” for a Single Opportunity

Suppose you’re exploring an opportunity in **digital SME onboarding**. Your data package may include:

- From **product analytics**:
  - Funnel: only 35% of SMEs who start digital onboarding complete it.
  - Major drops at document upload and KYC verification steps.
- From **support tickets**:
  - Many tickets about “upload failed”, “unclear required documents”.
- From **sales CRM**:
  - Lost deals citing “too complex onboarding”.
- From **industry reports**:
  - SMEs strongly prefer providers with fully digital onboarding.
- From **regulator publications**:
  - New allowances for digital KYC methods.

Together, this paints a clear picture of a **high‑value, timely** opportunity.
