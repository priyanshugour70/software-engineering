## 18 – Case Studies (End‑to‑End Discovery Stories)

This file walks through **end‑to‑end scenarios** showing how Market Opportunity Discovery can play out in practice.  
These are **constructed examples** (not tied to real companies) but are realistic for enterprises and banks.

We cover:

- Case Study 1: Digital SME Credit Line (Retail Bank)
- Case Study 2: Cash‑Flow Forecasting for SMEs (SaaS + Bank Partnership)

Each case follows the stages in `03-workflow.md` and highlights **what a beginner should notice**.

---

### Case Study 1 – Digital SME Credit Line (Retail Bank)

#### Context

A large retail bank wants to grow **SME lending** but sees:

- slow application processes,
- low digital adoption,
- increasing competition from fintech lenders.

#### Stage 1 – Prepare & Scope

- Strategic question:
  - “What opportunities exist to grow profitable SME lending digitally in the next 2 years?”
- Scope:
  - SMEs with 0.5–5M annual revenue, in retail and services, in one country.
- Team:
  - PM (lead), Strategy Manager, UX Researcher, Data Analyst, Risk representative, Engineering Lead.

#### Stage 2 – Collect Signals

- **Analytics & BI**
  - Onboarding and loan application data:
    - 60% of SME loan applications are still branch‑initiated.
    - Average time from application to decision: 7–10 days.
- **Support & Sales**
  - Relationship managers report that SMEs complain about:
    - paperwork,
    - unclear requirements,
    - long waiting times.
- **Market & Competitors**
  - Two major fintechs offer online SME loans with same‑day decisions.
- **Regulation**
  - New guidelines allow use of more digital data for credit scoring.

#### Stage 3 – Synthesize Themes

Themes identified:

- “SMEs experience friction and delays in loan applications.”
- “Our bank has **data** but doesn’t fully use it for SME credit decisions.”
- “Competitors advertise **speed and simplicity** as key benefits.”

The team clusters signals under:

- Application **friction**,
- **Data usage** in risk decisions,
- **Perceived slowness** vs competition.

#### Stage 4 – Shape Opportunities

The team drafts three opportunity hypotheses:

1. **MOD‑001 – Digital Credit Line with Fast Decisions**
   - Use transaction and payment data to pre‑qualify SMEs and provide instant or near‑instant decisions.
2. **MOD‑002 – Pre‑Approved SME Offers**
   - Identify SMEs with strong profiles and proactively offer credit before they ask.
3. **MOD‑003 – Application Support and Guidance**
   - Improve the application journey with clear guidance, document checklists, and branch + digital hybrid support.

For each, they write a **1–2 page brief** (see `10-documents.md`).

#### Stage 5 – Evaluate & Prioritize

Using criteria from `07-opportunity-evaluation.md`:

- Strategic fit,
- Customer value,
- Business impact,
- Feasibility,
- Time sensitivity,
- Risk.

They score the three opportunities in a workshop with stakeholders.

Outcome:

- MOD‑001 (Digital Credit Line) scores highest on:
  - strategic fit,
  - customer value,
  - business impact,
  - urgency (due to fintech competition).
- MOD‑002 is high value but depends on capabilities of MOD‑001.
- MOD‑003 is lower value but easier to deliver; considered as a **short‑term quick win**.

Decision:

- Move MOD‑001 to Problem Definition and Feasibility study.
- Plan MOD‑003 as a parallel, shorter‑term improvement.

#### Stage 6 – Share & Hand Over

- PM and Strategy prepare a **readout deck**:
  - context, methods, themes,
  - 3 opportunities,
  - evaluation summary,
  - recommended focus on MOD‑001.
- Presented to:
  - SME business unit leadership,
  - risk and credit committees.
- They:
  - approve further work on MOD‑001,
  - assign a dedicated **cross‑functional squad**.

**Beginner takeaway**  
Notice how the team:

- didn’t jump straight to “build a new app”,
- built a **portfolio of opportunities**,
- used simple criteria and stakeholder input to focus on the **most strategic and impactful** one.

### Case Study 2 – Cash‑Flow Forecasting for SMEs (SaaS + Bank Partnership)

#### Context

A mid‑size SaaS company provides accounting software for SMEs. They partner with a bank to explore **joint opportunities** around financial management.

#### Stage 1 – Prepare & Scope

- Question:
  - “What financial insights or tools could we jointly offer to SMEs that increase engagement and product usage for both partners?”
- Scope:
  - SMEs using the SaaS accounting tool in two pilot markets.
- Team:
  - SaaS PM and UX Lead,
  - Bank PM and Strategy Rep,
  - Data Analysts from both sides.

#### Stage 2 – Collect Signals

- **Product analytics (SaaS)**
  - Many SMEs frequently export cash‑flow related reports.
  - Reports with outstanding invoices and payables are among the most used features.
- **Customer interviews**
  - SMEs describe stress about:
    - not knowing if they’ll have enough to cover future obligations.
    - unexpected dips from seasonal patterns.
- **Bank data**
  - Transaction histories show clear seasonal spikes in inflows and outflows.
- **Market scan**
  - Few tools offer **simple, understandable cash‑flow forecasts** tightly integrated with banking and accounting.

#### Stage 3 – Synthesize Themes

Key theme:

- “SMEs struggle to **predict and manage short‑term cash‑flow** and often react too late.”

Other themes:

- “They assemble their own view from multiple tools and spreadsheets.”
- “They would value proactive alerts about upcoming shortfalls.”

#### Stage 4 – Shape Opportunities

Main opportunity hypothesis:

- “If we provide SMEs with a **simple, visual cash‑flow forecast** that combines accounting and bank data, plus alerts for upcoming shortfalls, they can make better decisions and we can drive higher engagement with both products.”

An opportunity brief is created, including:

- segment definition,
- problem description,
- value for SMEs,
- value for SaaS and bank,
- evidence from interviews and analytics.

#### Stage 5 – Evaluate & Prioritize

Criteria used:

- Strategic fit with both companies.
- Customer value and differentiation.
- Feasibility of **secure data integration**.
- Potential to cross‑sell more products (e.g., overdrafts, credit lines).

Outcome:

- Scores high on:
  - strategic fit,
  - customer value,
  - differentiation,
  - long‑term business potential.
- Feasibility:
  - medium complexity,
  - but manageable via phased integration.

Decision:

- Treat cash‑flow forecasting as a **joint flagship opportunity**.
- Move forward to:
  - detailed Problem Definition,
  - joint design sprints,
  - technical feasibility and security review.

#### Stage 6 – Share & Hand Over

- Teams prepare a **joint concept note** and opportunity brief.
- Present to:
  - product leadership at SaaS company,
  - bank’s partnership and product heads.
- Approvals trigger:
  - a **formal partnership workstream**,
  - initial roadmap for a beta release to a subset of SMEs.

**Beginner takeaway**  
This case illustrates:

- how cross‑company discovery works,
- how **combined data and capabilities** open new opportunities,
- the need to align **two organizations’ strategies** and constraints.
