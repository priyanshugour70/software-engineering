## 01 – Concepts: Product Strategy & Roadmap

### 1. Simple Definitions (For Beginners)

**Product strategy** answers:

> “Where are we going with this product, and why?”

**Roadmap** answers:

> “What are we going to do next (and later) to get there?”

Strategy is:

- about **choices and focus**:
  - which users,
  - which problems,
  - which differentiators,
  - which markets.

Roadmap is:

- about **sequencing and communication**:
  - which initiatives / epics,
  - in what order,
  - with what dependencies,
  - under what constraints.

---

### 2. Relationship to Earlier Phases

By the time you reach this phase, you should already have:

- **Opportunities** (phase 1),
- **Problem definitions** (phase 2),
- **User & market insights** (phase 3),
- **Business cases & ROI** (phase 4),
- **Feasibility studies** (phase 5).

Product Strategy & Roadmap:

- **synthesizes** all of that into:
  - a **direction**,
  - a **portfolio of bets**,
  - and a **plan**.

Think of it as:

- moving from:
  - “What’s possible and valuable?”  
to:
  - “What will we actually do, in what sequence, given our reality?”

---

### 3. Strategy vs Tactics vs Execution

- **Strategy**
  - choices about:
    - where to play,
    - how to win (or be valuable),
    - what trade‑offs you accept.
- **Tactics**
  - specific moves and experiments:
    - how to roll out features,
    - how to test hypotheses.
- **Execution**
  - detailed planning and delivery:
    - user stories,
    - sprints,
    - releases.

Product strategy operates mostly at:

- the **“where and how”** level,
while the roadmap bridges into:

- **when and roughly what**.

---

### 4. Key Concepts You Must Know

#### 4.1 Vision

- A concise, inspiring statement of:
  - what the product aspires to be,
  - for whom,
  - and why it matters.

Example:

> “Enable SMEs to access tailored credit in minutes, with full transparency and control.”

---

#### 4.2 Strategic Themes / Pillars

High‑level areas of focus that:

- guide decisions and trade‑offs.

Examples:

- “Speed & Convenience”,
- “Transparency & Control”,
- “Risk Management & Compliance”,
- “Operational Efficiency”.

Themes:

- link to **objectives**, **initiatives**, and **roadmap items**.

---

#### 4.3 Objectives & Key Results (OKRs)

- **Objectives**
  - qualitative goals tied to themes.
- **Key Results**
  - measurable outcomes indicating progress.

Example:

- Objective:
  - “Increase SME adoption of digital lending.”
- Key Results:
  - “Digital share of SME loan applications from 30% → 60%.”
  - “Application completion rate from 60% → 80%.”

---

#### 4.4 Initiatives, Epics, Features

- **Initiative**
  - large body of work addressing a strategic objective.
  - e.g., “Digital SME Onboarding v2”.
- **Epic**
  - coherent slice of work within an initiative.
  - e.g., “Pre‑onboarding document preparation experience.”
- **Feature / Story**
  - concrete functionality pieces.
  - e.g., “Document checklist page with examples and export function.”

Roadmaps typically operate at:

- **initiative and epic levels**.

---

#### 4.5 Time Horizons

Common horizon model:

- **Horizon 1 (0–12 months)** – near term:
  - more detailed, committed items.
- **Horizon 2 (12–24 months)** – mid‑term:
  - directional, flexible.
- **Horizon 3 (24+ months)** – long‑term:
  - visions and themes, not fixed commitments.

Helps:

- avoid fake precision far into the future,
- while still communicating direction.

---

### 5. Why Strategy & Roadmap Matter in Enterprises/Banks

Without clear strategy and roadmaps, organizations experience:

- **random work intake**:
  - loudest voices win,
  - constant context‑switching.
- **misaligned expectations**:
  - business, tech, and risk have different pictures of the future.
- **missed dependencies and bottlenecks**:
  - teams block each other,
  - regulatory deadlines are discovered too late.

Good strategy and roadmaps:

- create:
  - **focus**,
  - **alignment**,
  - and **predictable progress**,
while leaving room for:

- learning,
- course corrections.

---

### 6. Common Misconceptions

- “Roadmap = fixed Gantt chart for 2–3 years.”
  - Reality:
    - roadmaps should be **living, outcome‑driven artifacts**.
- “Strategy = big document nobody reads.”
  - Reality:
    - strategy is:
      - a small set of **choices and principles** used every day.
- “We can have strategy without saying no.”
  - Reality:
    - strategy is as much about **what you won’t do** as what you will.
