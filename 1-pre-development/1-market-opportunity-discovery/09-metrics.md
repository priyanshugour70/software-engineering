## 09 – Metrics for Market Opportunity Discovery

This file explains **what to measure** in and around the discovery phase.  
It focuses on metrics that help you:

- understand opportunity value and impact,
- track discovery quality and throughput,
- communicate with leadership using numbers.

We group metrics into:

- A. Customer & usage metrics
- B. Business & financial metrics
- C. Discovery process metrics
- D. Example metric sets by opportunity type

---

### A. Customer & Usage Metrics

These metrics tell you whether an opportunity addresses **real customer behavior and pain**.

#### 1. Volume & Frequency Metrics

Examples:

- Number of users/accounts performing a key action per day/week/month.
- Number of transactions in a relevant flow (e.g., SME payments, loan applications).
- Frequency of specific behaviors (e.g., exporting data, calling support).

Why they matter:

- Help estimate **how many** customers are affected and **how often**.

---

#### 2. Funnel Metrics

Examples:

- Onboarding funnel:
  - Started → Completed identity verification → Submitted documents → Approved.
- Loan application funnel:
  - Started → Submitted → Approved → Drawn down.

Why they matter:

- Identify **drop‑off points** that may hide major opportunities.

---

#### 3. Engagement & Retention

Examples:

- Daily / Monthly Active Users (DAU/MAU) for specific features.
- Feature retention:
  - “% of users who used feature X in week 1 and again in week 4/8/12.”

Why they matter:

- Show whether existing solutions meet ongoing needs.
- Low engagement may suggest:
  - poor fit,
  - poor UX,
  - or that the opportunity is smaller than expected.

---

#### 4. Satisfaction & Sentiment

Examples:

- NPS/CSAT for journeys or segments.
- Number and sentiment of support tickets per segment or feature.

Why they matter:

- High negative sentiment around a journey often signals **ripe opportunities**.

---

### B. Business & Financial Metrics

These metrics help estimate **value potential** and justify investments.

#### 1. Revenue & Growth

Examples:

- Revenue by product / segment / region.
- Growth rate for specific products or segments.

Use in discovery:

- Identify **fast‑growing segments** that might warrant more investment.
- Spot **stagnant or declining areas** needing new opportunities.

---

#### 2. Profitability & Cost‑to‑Serve

Examples:

- Contribution margin by product/segment.
- Cost per onboarding, per transaction, per support interaction.

Use:

- Discover opportunities in:
  - **automation** (reduce manual work),
  - **self‑service** (reduce support costs),
  - **process redesign** (reduce rework and delays).

---

#### 3. Risk & Loss Metrics (Especially in Banking)

Examples:

- Default rates by segment or product.
- Fraud loss rates.
- Operational risk incidents (e.g., processing errors).

Use:

- Identify opportunities that:
  - **reduce risk**, or
  - give customers tools that **reduce their own financial risk** (e.g., cash‑flow insights).

---

#### 4. Regulatory / Compliance Metrics

Examples:

- % of processes compliant with new regulation.
- Number of audit findings related to a specific process.

Use:

- Opportunities may arise when:
  - new regulation requires **new capabilities**,
  - or when better tooling can **reduce compliance burden** or **improve transparency**.

---

### C. Discovery Process Metrics (Meta‑Metrics)

These don’t measure customer or business outcomes directly; they measure how well you are **running discovery itself**.

#### 1. Throughput of Opportunities

Examples:

- Number of opportunities:
  - identified this quarter,
  - with completed briefs,
  - that reached evaluation/prioritization.

Use:

- Shows whether discovery is **producing a healthy funnel** of candidates.

---

#### 2. Quality of Opportunity Definitions

Harder to quantify, but you can use:

- Simple checklists (see `24-checklists.md`) to rate:
  - clarity of **who/what/why now**,
  - link to **data/evidence**,
  - explicit assumptions and risks.

You might score each opportunity as:

- Bronze / Silver / Gold quality,
- or 1–5 on “definition quality”.

---

#### 3. Stakeholder Alignment

Proxy measures:

- % of key stakeholders who:
  - attended evaluation workshops,
  - agree on the **top 3–5 opportunities** (measured through short polls).

Use:

- Helps you see if discovery is actually **building shared understanding**, not just generating documents.

---

#### 4. Cycle Time

Examples:

- Time from:
  - idea → structured opportunity brief,
  - brief → evaluation decision,
  - decision → entry into next phase.

Use:

- Keep discovery cycles **timeboxed and efficient**. Very long cycles can lose momentum.

---

### D. Example Metric Sets by Opportunity Type

#### 1. Example: Digital SME Onboarding Opportunity

Key metrics to look at:

- Onboarding funnel:
  - % started vs % completed.
  - Average time to complete.
- Segmentation:
  - Differences by industry, region, or company size.
- Support:
  - Number of tickets related to onboarding per month.
  - Common reasons for failure or confusion.
- Business:
  - Conversion to active, revenue‑generating accounts.
  - Cost per onboarding (manual vs digital).

Insights you might derive:

- Huge drop‑off at document upload → opportunity in simplifying doc requirements or UX.
- Long average approval time → opportunity in process automation or decisioning.

---

#### 2. Example: Cash‑Flow Analytics for SMEs

Key metrics:

- % of SME customers who:
  - log into digital channels weekly/monthly.
  - export data to spreadsheets or external tools.
- Feature usage:
  - current use of reporting or basic analytics features.
- Business:
  - Correlation between existing analytics usage and:
    - product cross‑sell,
    - default rates,
    - churn.

Insights:

- Heavy use of exports may signal a need for **stronger in‑product analytics**.
- If analytics usage correlates with **better outcomes**, that strengthens the opportunity’s case.

---

#### 3. Example: New Cross‑Border Payment Offering

Key metrics:

- Transaction volume and value by corridor (e.g., country A → B).
- Average fees paid vs competitor benchmarks (if available).
- Settlement times.
- Complaints related to cross‑border payments.

Use:

- Justify opportunities to:
  - reduce fees,
  - speed up settlement,
  - improve transparency (tracking, FX visibility).

---

### E. How to Use Metrics in Discovery (Beginner Tips)

1. **Start with questions, not dashboards**
   - Example: “Are SMEs abandoning onboarding?” → then check funnel dashboards.

2. **Combine metrics with stories**
   - A number like “40% drop‑off” is powerful when paired with:
     - quotes from customers explaining *why* they dropped off.

3. **Avoid false precision**
   - Early estimates can be rough (e.g., “this could be a multi‑million impact vs. a small incremental improvement”).

4. **Update metrics as you learn**
   - As opportunities move forward, refine and improve your metric view.
