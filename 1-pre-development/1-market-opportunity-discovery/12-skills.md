## 12 – Skills Needed for Market Opportunity Discovery

This file describes the **capabilities individuals need** to perform discovery well.  
It is useful for:

- PMs and aspiring PMs planning their learning,
- managers designing teams,
- people preparing for interviews or role changes.

We cover:

- A. Core skill areas
- B. Skill breakdown by role
- C. Beginner learning path

---

### A. Core Skill Areas

#### 1. Customer Insight & Research Skills

Abilities:

- Conduct **interviews and discovery conversations**:
  - ask open, non‑leading questions,
  - dig into real stories and behavior.
- Map **customer journeys and pain points**.
- Use **Jobs‑To‑Be‑Done** and similar frameworks to understand underlying needs.

Why important:

- Opportunities must be grounded in **real customer reality**, not guesses.

---

#### 2. Analytical & Quantitative Skills

Abilities:

- Read and interpret **basic dashboards** (funnel, retention, cohorts).
- Ask good **data questions** (what to measure, why it matters).
- Work with analysts to design **simple cuts** of data (by segment, channel, region).
- Perform rough **opportunity sizing** and impact estimation.

Why important:

- You need to combine **numbers and narratives** to judge opportunity strength.

---

#### 3. Strategic Thinking

Abilities:

- Connect opportunities to **company strategy and OKRs**.
- Understand **market structure** and how your organization competes.
- See **second‑order effects** (how one opportunity affects others, cannibalization, risk).

Why important:

- Helps avoid chasing **local optimizations** that don’t support the bigger picture.

---

#### 4. Structured Problem Solving

Abilities:

- Break down vague problems into **manageable parts**.
- Use frameworks (3C, OST, Five Forces, etc.) to structure thinking.
- Separate **symptoms** from **root causes**.

Why important:

- Discovery often starts messy; structure prevents you from getting lost.

---

#### 5. Facilitation & Communication

Abilities:

- Run **workshops and group sessions**:
  - keep discussions focused,
  - ensure different voices are heard.
- Present findings clearly to **both technical and business** audiences.
- Write **concise, clear documents** (briefs, readouts).

Why important:

- Discovery is **highly collaborative**; no single person has all the answers.

---

#### 6. Stakeholder Management & Influence

Abilities:

- Identify **key stakeholders** and their interests.
- Build **trust and alignment** over time.
- Handle disagreements **constructively**, using data and clear reasoning.

Why important:

- Even the best opportunity fails if **decision‑makers are not bought in**.

---

#### 7. Risk & Regulatory Awareness (Especially for Banking)

Abilities:

- Understand basic concepts in:
  - credit risk, market risk, operational risk,
  - compliance and regulation relevant to your domain.
- Know **when to involve experts** (risk, legal, compliance).

Why important:

- Many opportunities in banking depend on **regulatory context**; ignoring this leads to rework or failure.

---

### C. Beginner Learning Path (Concrete Steps)

If you are **new to opportunity discovery**, here is a suggested progression.

#### Step 1 – Learn the Basics of Customer Discovery

- Read this folder’s:
  - `01-concepts.md`,
  - `03-workflow.md`,
  - `05-research-methods.md`.
- Practice:
  - Shadow **2–3 customer calls** or support calls.
  - Write notes focusing on:
    - customer goals,
    - steps,
    - pains.

Goal: become comfortable listening for **problems and jobs‑to‑be‑done**.

---

#### Step 2 – Get Comfortable with Simple Analytics

- Sit with a data analyst or product analyst and:
  - explore **1–2 key dashboards** (onboarding, usage).
  - ask “what if we segment by X?” type questions.
- Practice:
  - interpret **basic funnels**,
  - find **one surprising data pattern** and try to explain it.

Goal: be able to say, “we saw **this problem** in **this metric**, with **this approximate size**.”

---

#### Step 3 – Practice Writing Opportunity Briefs

- Take a **known problem area** and create a **mock opportunity brief** using `10-documents.md` and `23-templates.md`.
- Get feedback from:
  - your manager,
  - a senior PM,
  - or a strategy colleague.

Goal: learn to **summarize complex information** into a **clear, 1–2 page narrative**.

---

#### Step 4 – Facilitate a Small Workshop

- Run a 60–90 minute session with 3–5 colleagues:
  - use a whiteboard or Miro board,
  - cluster signals,
  - name themes.

Goal: practice **guiding a group** through structured thinking.

---

#### Step 5 – Participate in Evaluation & Prioritization

- Join or organize a session where:
  - **3–5 opportunities** are scored using criteria from `07-opportunity-evaluation.md`.
- Observe:
  - how stakeholders reason,
  - which arguments are persuasive.

Goal: understand how **decisions are actually made** in your organization.

---

### D. How to Grow from Beginner to Advanced

As you gain experience:

- Move from **copying frameworks** to **adapting them** for your context.
- Learn to **anticipate objections** (risk, capacity, alignment) in advance.
- Develop **pattern recognition**:
  - “this looks like the type of opportunity that usually fails/succeeds in our org because…”

Senior practitioners often:

- Mentor others in discovery skills.
- Shape **portfolio‑level views**, not just single opportunities.
- Influence **strategy itself** based on what they learn from discovery.
