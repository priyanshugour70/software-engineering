## 15 – Industries and Contexts for Market Opportunity Discovery

Market opportunity discovery **looks different across industries**, but the core ideas stay the same:  
find **where value can be created** at the intersection of customer needs, capabilities, and external conditions.

This file gives a **beginner-friendly overview** of how discovery plays out in:

- A. Banking and Financial Services (focus industry for this repo)
- B. SaaS and Software Products
- C. E‑commerce and Marketplaces
- D. Telecoms and Utilities (large, regulated incumbents)

Use it to understand how **context changes constraints and opportunities**.

---

### A. Banking and Financial Services

Banking is:

- **Highly regulated** – heavy compliance, licenses, capital rules.
- **Data‑rich** – banks see transactions, balances, and histories.
- **Trust‑critical** – customers care deeply about safety and reliability.

Common opportunity spaces:

- **Digital onboarding and self‑service**
  - Replace paper and branch visits with digital journeys.
  - Example: remote KYC, instant card issuance.
- **SME and corporate services**
  - Cash‑flow management, receivables/payables automation.
  - Cross‑border payments and FX.
- **Personal finance management**
  - Budgeting, savings nudges, debt optimization.
- **Embedded and open banking**
  - Banking services integrated into non‑bank platforms (e.g., marketplaces, ERPs).

Unique constraints:

- Strong involvement from **risk, legal, compliance** at every step.
- Need to consider **prudential rules, KYC/AML, data privacy** early in discovery.

Discovery must:

- Balance **innovation** with **risk control**.
- Look at **regulation as both constraint and opportunity** (e.g., open banking mandates).

---

### B. SaaS and Software Products

SaaS companies (B2B or B2C) often operate with:

- Faster release cycles,
- More flexible experimentation,
- Direct access to product analytics.

Common opportunity spaces:

- **New workflows and automations**
  - Replace manual spreadsheets or email processes.
- **Vertical expansion**
  - Tailoring generic tools to specific industries (e.g., SaaS for logistics, healthcare, etc.).
- **Upsell and cross‑sell**
  - Adding modules that deepen engagement (analytics, integrations, AI assistants).

Constraints:

- Often **less regulated** than banks, but may still face:
  - privacy rules (e.g., data protection),
  - sector‑specific rules (healthcare, education).

Discovery typically:

- Leans heavily on **product analytics** and **rapid customer feedback**.
- Can run **more experiments** before committing major investment.

---

### C. E‑Commerce and Marketplaces

E‑commerce platforms and marketplaces focus on:

- Matching **buyers and sellers** efficiently,
- Optimizing **conversion, basket size, and frequency**.

Common opportunity spaces:

- **Conversion funnel improvements**
  - Checkout friction, payment methods, delivery promises.
- **Assortment and catalog**
  - Gaps in product offering, long‑tail items, private labels.
- **Trust and safety**
  - Fraud prevention, dispute resolution, rating/review systems.
- **Logistics and fulfillment**
  - Delivery speed, reliability, and cost optimizations.

Constraints:

- Thin margins in many product categories.
- Heavy dependence on operations and logistics capabilities.

Discovery in this context:

- Uses **A/B testing and analytics** extensively.
- Often focuses on **micro‑optimizations** with big aggregate impact.

---

### D. Telecoms and Utilities (Large, Regulated Incumbents)

Telecoms and utilities resemble banks in some ways:

- Large, existing customer bases.
- Regulated environments.
- Heavy infrastructure investments.

Common opportunity spaces:

- **Digital self‑service and support**
  - Reducing call center load, enabling app‑based management.
- **Bundled services**
  - Combining offerings (e.g., mobile + broadband + content).
- **Enterprise and IoT services**
  - Connectivity, monitoring, and data platforms.

Constraints:

- Legacy systems and processes.
- Regulatory rules on pricing, competition, infrastructure sharing.

Discovery:

- Must factor in **complex technical dependencies**.
- Often coordinated with **long‑term capital planning** (network roll‑outs, upgrades).

---

### E. What Stays the Same Across Industries

Regardless of context:

- You still:
  - look for **pains, needs, and jobs‑to‑be‑done**,
  - scan **market, competition, and regulation**,
  - consider **company capabilities and constraints**,
  - create and prioritize **opportunity hypotheses**.

The **frameworks** in:

- `01-concepts.md`,
- `04-frameworks.md`,
- `07-opportunity-evaluation.md`,

remain useful; you just plug in **industry‑specific data and constraints**.

---

### F. How to Adapt Your Discovery Approach

As you move between industries:

- Learn the **basic regulatory environment**.
- Identify **key value drivers**:
  - in banking: risk, capital, deposits, fee income.
  - in SaaS: subscriptions, churn, ARPU.
  - in e‑commerce: conversion, margin, logistics.
- Understand where **innovation is easier vs harder**:
  - front‑end journeys vs deep infrastructure.

This will help you:

- Suggest opportunities that are **ambitious but realistic** for that industry.
