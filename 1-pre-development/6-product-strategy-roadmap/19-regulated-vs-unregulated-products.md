## 19 – Regulated vs Unregulated Products

This file explains how **regulation level** changes Product Strategy & Roadmap work.

We contrast:

- heavily regulated products (e.g., banking, payments, lending, insurance),
- lightly regulated or unregulated products (e.g., many consumer apps, general SaaS).

---

### 1. What “Regulated” Means Here

Regulated products:

- are subject to:
  - laws,
  - supervisory rules,
  - regulatory guidance.
- involve:
  - licenses,
  - reporting obligations,
  - audits and inspections.

Examples:

- SME lending, retail banking, payments:
  - KYC/AML requirements,
  - consumer credit rules,
  - anti‑fraud obligations,
  - data protection (GDPR and local laws).

Unregulated or lightly regulated:

- still subject to:
  - generic laws (contract, consumer protection, data laws),
  - but:
    - fewer specialized rules,
    - fewer direct supervisory bodies.

---

### 2. Implications for Strategy

#### 2.1 Regulated Products

Strategy must:

- explicitly account for:
  - risk appetite,
  - regulatory landscape and upcoming changes,
  - expected supervisory focus areas.

Themes may include:

- “Regulatory Readiness & Resilience,”
- “Risk Control & Auditability,”
- “Customer Fairness & Transparency.”

Vision and differentiators:

- often balance:
  - innovation and convenience,
  - with safety and compliance.

#### 2.2 Unregulated/Lightly Regulated Products

Strategy:

- can often:
  - move faster,
  - explore more aggressive experiments.

Themes may focus more heavily on:

- growth,
- engagement,
- network effects,
- platform expansion.

Risk is still present:

- but often:
  - more centered on:
    - market,
    - operational,
    - and reputational risk,
  - rather than direct regulatory enforcement.

---

### 3. Implications for Roadmaps

#### 3.1 Regulated Products

Roadmaps must:

- include:
  - non‑negotiable regulatory items:
    - new rules and guidelines,
    - remediation commitments,
    - audit or model review findings.
- carefully sequence:
  - risk and compliance work,
  - product and platform capabilities,
  - operational and reporting changes.

Time horizons:

- some items will have:
  - hard deadlines,
  - limited flexibility.

Communication:

- clear labelling of:
  - “must‑do” vs “discretionary” work,
  - risk implications of delays.

#### 3.2 Unregulated/Lightly Regulated Products

Roadmaps:

- may:
  - place greater weight on:
    - tests and learning,
    - rapid iteration,
    - new revenue streams.
- more flexibility:
  - in shifting priorities,
  - in deferring or killing initiatives.

Still important:

- to make:
  - ethical and user‑centric choices,
  - avoid harmful behaviors even without strict regulation.

---

### 4. Role of Risk, Compliance & Legal

In regulated products:

- risk, compliance, and legal are:
  - core strategy and roadmap partners (see `13-roles-and-positions.md`).
- they:
  - interpret regulations into product implications,
  - define “guardrails”,
  - co‑shape policies and controls.

In less regulated products:

- they may:
  - be involved for:
    - contracts,
    - privacy,
    - key policies;
  - but:
    - may not shape day‑to‑day roadmap as strongly.

Either way:

- early collaboration prevents:
  - late surprises,
  - blockers right before launch.

---

### 5. Risk & Compliance Work as First‑Class Roadmap Items

In regulated domains:

- treat risk and compliance work as:
  - **first‑class citizens** on the roadmap,
  - not hidden or “side” tasks.

Examples:

- “Implement Regulation XYZ changes across SME lending journey.”
- “Build audit trail and configuration history for risk rules.”
- “Enhance AML monitoring thresholds and workflows.”

Attach:

- clear objectives and metrics where possible, e.g.:
  - reduction in regulatory findings,
  - improved audit readiness,
  - shorter time to update rules safely.

---

### 6. Beginner Checklist

- [ ] Is your product:
  - [ ] heavily regulated,
  - [ ] moderately regulated,
  - [ ] lightly regulated?
- [ ] Does your strategy:
  - [ ] explicitly account for regulatory and risk constraints?
  - [ ] identify themes related to compliance and auditability if needed?
- [ ] Does your roadmap:
  - [ ] clearly show regulatory must‑dos and deadlines?
  - [ ] include risk/compliance initiatives as visible items?

If not, there is a risk that:

- strategy and roadmaps **look good on paper**,
- but are **not actually viable** in your regulatory environment.
