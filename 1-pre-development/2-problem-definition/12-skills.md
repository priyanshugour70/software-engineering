## 12 – Skills Needed for Strong Problem Definition

This file describes the **skills individuals need** to do Problem Definition well.

We cover:

- A. Core skill areas
- B. Emphasis by role
- C. Beginner learning path

---

### A. Core Skill Areas

#### 1. Structured Thinking & Framing

Abilities:

- Break vague topics into **clear, structured components**.
- Use frameworks (5 Whys, current vs desired state, JTBD) effectively.
- Distinguish:
  - symptoms vs root causes,
  - problem vs solution vs opportunity.

---

#### 2. Quantitative Literacy

Abilities:

- Understand basic metrics:
  - rates, averages, percent changes.
- Read and question simple dashboards and reports.
- Translate narrative complaints into:
  - “how often”,
  - “how many”,
  - “how long”.

---

#### 3. Qualitative Insight & Empathy

Abilities:

- Conduct **focused conversations** with users and stakeholders.
- Listen for:
  - pain points,
  - workarounds,
  - emotions behind behavior.
- Synthesize stories into:
  - patterns,
  - themes,
  - problem hypotheses.

---

#### 4. Facilitation

Abilities:

- Run workshops that:
  - stay on topic,
  - include different voices,
  - converge on decisions.
- Use whiteboards/boards to:
  - visualize journeys,
  - cluster themes,
  - refine statements.

---

#### 5. Stakeholder Management

Abilities:

- Identify who must:
  - be involved,
  - approve,
  - be informed.
- Handle **conflicting views** on what the problem is.
- Build agreement around a **shared problem definition**.

---

#### 6. Domain & Regulatory Awareness (Especially in Banking)

Abilities:

- Understand basics of:
  - your products and services,
  - key regulatory rules,
  - internal policies.
- Know **when to pull in experts** (risk, legal, compliance).

### C. Beginner Learning Path

If you are new to Problem Definition, you can grow through these stages:

#### Step 1 – Learn the Concepts

- Read:
  - `01-concepts.md`,
  - `02-objectives.md`,
  - `03-workflow.md`.
- Practice:
  - rewrite **one existing vague problem** in your team using the template in `23-templates.md`.

---

#### Step 2 – Practice Data & Metrics

- Sit with a data/BI colleague:
  - ask them to walk you through:
    - a funnel,
    - an SLA dashboard,
    - a simple cohort chart.
- Write down:
  - 2–3 metrics that describe a current pain in your area.

Goal:

- be able to say “the problem is **this big** and **this frequent**”.

---

#### Step 3 – Practice Framing with 5 Whys

- Take a known issue (e.g., “high refund rates”) and run:
  - 5 Whys on your own,
  - then with stakeholders.
- Compare outcomes:
  - see how root causes differ across people.

Goal:

- become comfortable going beyond surface symptoms.

---

#### Step 4 – Facilitate a Mini Workshop

- Run a 60–90 minute session with 3–5 colleagues:
  - present current state (data + stories),
  - use a problem statement template to draft and refine.

Ask for feedback on:

- clarity,
- inclusiveness,
- effectiveness.

---

#### Step 5 – Own a Small Problem Definition Sprint

- For a **narrow, low‑risk area**, volunteer to:
  - lead intake,
  - talk to stakeholders,
  - draft the problem statement,
  - present it in a review.

Goal:

- experience the **full loop** of Problem Definition, end‑to‑end.
