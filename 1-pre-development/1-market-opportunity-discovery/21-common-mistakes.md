## 21 – Common Mistakes in Market Opportunity Discovery

This file lists **frequent pitfalls** teams fall into when doing discovery, with explanations and tips for avoiding them.  
Read it together with `20-best-practices.md`.

We group mistakes into:

- A. Mindset and assumptions
- B. Process and execution
- C. Stakeholder and organizational issues

---

### A. Mindset and Assumption Mistakes

#### 1. Jumping Straight to Solutions

Pattern:

- Someone says, “We should build feature X,” and the team:
  - starts designing and planning,
  - without clarifying **who**, **what problem**, and **why now**.

Why it’s bad:

- You might solve the **wrong problem** or a **small problem**.
- Harder to change direction later because people are attached to the chosen solution.

How to avoid:

- Always start with a **problem/opportunity framing**:
  - “Who has what problem, and why is it important?”

---

#### 2. Treating Anecdotes as Universal Truth

Pattern:

- A senior person says:
  - “A big client complained about X.”
  - The team treats X as **the main opportunity**, without checking data.

Why it’s bad:

- You may overreact to **one or two loud voices**.

How to avoid:

- Combine:
  - stories with **metrics** (e.g., how many users are affected),
  - see `05-research-methods.md` and `06-data-sources.md`.

---

#### 3. Overconfidence in Early Ideas

Pattern:

- First few ideas feel exciting.
- Team stops searching and **locks in** too early.

Why it’s bad:

- You might miss **higher‑value opportunities** that need a bit more exploration.

How to avoid:

- Timebox:
  - “We will generate and explore **N** candidate opportunities before prioritizing.”

---

### B. Process and Execution Mistakes

#### 4. No Clear Scope or Question

Pattern:

- “Let’s do discovery” without:
  - domain boundaries,
  - clear objectives.

Result:

- Unfocused research,
- collection of random insights,
- difficulty turning findings into action.

How to avoid:

- Write a short **discovery scope and objectives** document (see `10-documents.md`).

---

#### 5. Endless Research with No Decisions

Pattern:

- Teams keep collecting data:
  - new reports,
  - more interviews,
  - more dashboards.
- But rarely:
  - synthesize,
  - evaluate,
  - decide.

Why it’s bad:

- Wastes time and delays impact.

How to avoid:

- Use **timeboxes** and **fixed milestones**:
  - “By week 4, we will have a shortlist of X opportunities and a prioritization proposal.”

---

#### 6. Weak or Inconsistent Opportunity Definitions

Pattern:

- Each opportunity is described differently or vaguely.
- Missing:
  - target segment,
  - problem,
  - evidence.

Why it’s bad:

- Difficult to **compare** opportunities.
- Stakeholders misunderstand or talk past each other.

How to avoid:

- Use a **standard opportunity brief template** (`10-documents.md` and `23-templates.md`).

---

#### 7. No Explicit Evaluation Criteria

Pattern:

- Prioritization discussions rely on:
  - loudest voice,
  - gut feeling.

Why it’s bad:

- Leads to **political decisions**.
- Hard to justify choices later.

How to avoid:

- Define and agree on **simple criteria** before scoring (see `07-opportunity-evaluation.md`).

---

#### 8. Poor Handover to Next Phases

Pattern:

- Discovery work ends with:
  - “Here’s a document,”
  - but no clear owners or next steps.

Why it’s bad:

- Opportunities stall.
- Later teams **redo work** because context is missing.

How to avoid:

- For each top opportunity:
  - assign an **owner**,
  - define **next actions and timeline**,
  - communicate decisions clearly (see `03-workflow.md`, Stage 6).

---

### C. Stakeholder and Organizational Mistakes

#### 9. Excluding Key Stakeholders

Pattern:

- Discovery done by a small group in isolation.
- Risk, compliance, operations, or engineering are brought in **too late**.

Why it’s bad:

- Opportunities may be:
  - infeasible,
  - non‑compliant,
  - misaligned with operational reality.

How to avoid:

- Involve:
  - risk/compliance,
  - engineering,
  - operations/support,
  early in **workshops and reviews** (see `11-roles-and-positions.md`).

---

#### 10. Ignoring Organizational Constraints

Pattern:

- Designing opportunities that:
  - require capacities or approvals that realistically **do not exist**,
  - ignore current priorities or regulatory deadlines.

Why it’s bad:

- Leads to frustration and **“paper strategies”** that never get implemented.

How to avoid:

- Factor in:
  - existing **strategic commitments**,
  - **resource availability**,
  - major **regulatory obligations**.
- Use **feasibility** as a criterion (even if high‑level).

---

#### 11. Treating Discovery as a One-Off Exercise

Pattern:

- Big discovery project once a year,
  - then no structured opportunity work until next year.

Why it’s bad:

- Opportunities and context **change continually**.

How to avoid:

- Run **smaller, regular cycles** (e.g., quarterly reviews, focused sprints).
- Maintain a **living opportunity backlog** (see `03-workflow.md` and `10-documents.md`).

---

#### 12. Hiding or Ignoring Negative Findings

Pattern:

- Teams feel pressure to:
  - show only **positive** outcomes,
  - avoid saying an idea is weak.

Why it’s bad:

- Weak opportunities persist,
  - consuming resources,
  - hurting trust.

How to avoid:

- Celebrate:
  - **learning that an opportunity is weak**,  
  - as a success of the discovery process (it saved future waste).
