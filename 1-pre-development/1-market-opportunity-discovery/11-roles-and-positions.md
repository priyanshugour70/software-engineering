## 11 – Roles and Positions in Market Opportunity Discovery

This file explains **who is involved** in Market Opportunity Discovery, what they do, and how they collaborate. It is written for both **beginners** (new PMs, analysts) and **experienced leaders** who want to formalize responsibilities.

We cover:

- A. Core product and business roles
- B. Supporting roles (UX, data, engineering, risk, compliance)
- C. Example team structures (startup vs enterprise/bank)
- D. RACI-style view (who owns what)

---

### A. Core Product and Business Roles

#### 1. Product Manager (PM) / Product Director

**Typical titles**: Product Manager, Senior PM, Product Lead, Product Director, Head of Product (in smaller orgs).

**Primary responsibilities**

- Own the **opportunity discovery process** for their domain.
- Define **scope and objectives** for discovery cycles.
- Lead the creation of **opportunity briefs** and backlogs.
- Facilitate **evaluation and prioritization** with stakeholders.
- Ensure strong **handover** into Problem Definition, Research, and later phases.

**Day‑to‑day tasks in discovery**

- Organize and run workshops and interviews.
- Synthesize inputs into **themes and opportunities**.
- Write or co‑write key documents (see `10-documents.md`).
- Present discovery findings to leadership and teams.

**Beginner note**  
If you are a new PM, you don't need to be the smartest person in the room. Your main job is to **orchestrate** the process and make sure all the right perspectives are heard and captured.

---

#### 2. Business Strategist / Portfolio Manager

**Typical titles**: Strategy Manager, Business Strategist, Portfolio Manager, Segment Lead, Tribe Lead.

**Primary responsibilities**

- Connect discovery to **business strategy, P&L, and portfolio goals**.
- Provide **top‑down** perspective on market trends and financial targets.
- Help define **evaluation criteria** and **value estimates**.
- Support **prioritization** decisions across products and initiatives.

**In banks/enterprises**

- Often the **“voice of the business”** in discovery.
- May chair or strongly influence **portfolio boards** or investment councils.

---

### B. Supporting Roles

#### 1. UX Researcher / Service Designer

**Responsibilities**

- Plan and run **customer research** (interviews, usability studies, journey mapping).
- Design and facilitate **co‑creation and ideation workshops**.
- Visualize **end‑to‑end customer journeys** and pain points.

**Value in discovery**

- Ensures opportunities are **rooted in real customer behavior**, not just internal opinions.
- Brings a **human‑centered** view, which is crucial in financial services.

If there is no dedicated researcher, PMs often take on a **lightweight version** of this role.

---

#### 2. Data Analyst / Data Scientist

**Responsibilities**

- Explore **product analytics and BI data** (see `06-data-sources.md`).
- Produce **dashboards and ad‑hoc analyses** for discovery.
- Help quantify **size, frequency, and impact** of problems.

**Value in discovery**

- Validates or challenges **anecdotal claims** with numbers.
- Supports **opportunity sizing** and early business cases.

Beginner tip: don't just ask them for “all the data”. Come with **specific questions** tied to your discovery themes.

---

#### 3. Engineering Lead / Architect

**Responsibilities**

- Provide **technical feasibility and constraints** input early.
- Explain **platform capabilities** and reusable building blocks.
- Flag **dependencies** and **risks** (e.g., legacy systems, performance, scalability).

**Value in discovery**

- Avoids promoting opportunities that are **clearly infeasible** in the near term.
- Reveals alternate, **more feasible ways** to realize an opportunity.

They are not responsible for **detailed design** here, but for **early, directional guidance**.

---

#### 4. Risk & Compliance / Legal (Critical in Banking)

**Responsibilities**

- Explain **regulatory requirements** and constraints.
- Identify **emerging regulations** that may create obligations or new opportunities.
- Help assess **risk profile** of candidate opportunities.

**Value in discovery**

- Prevents teams from pursuing ideas that are **not allowed** or **high‑risk**.
- Surfaces **regulation‑driven opportunities** (e.g., improved reporting tools).

In highly regulated environments, involving them **early** reduces friction later.

---

#### 5. Operations & Customer Support Leaders

**Responsibilities**

- Represent **day‑to‑day operational reality**:
  - manual processes,
  - bottlenecks,
  - typical incidents.
- Bring **frontline insights** into customer issues.

**Value in discovery**

- Often see **patterns of recurring problems** before anyone else.
- Help ensure opportunities are **operationally realistic**.

---

#### 6. Sales / Relationship Managers / Account Managers

**Responsibilities**

- Provide insights into:
  - what prospects **ask for in pitches**,
  - why deals are **won or lost**,
  - what differentiates competitors in customers’ eyes.

**Value in discovery**

- Helps identify **commercially attractive** opportunities.
- Reveals where value propositions are **strong or weak**.

---

### C. Example Team Structures

#### 1. Startup / Small Team

- **PM or Founder**:
  - leads discovery, writes briefs, runs most activities.
- **Engineer / Tech Lead**:
  - provides feasibility input and access to basic analytics.
- **Designer / UX Generalist**:
  - does interviews, journey mapping, early concept sketches.

Other functions (support, sales) are consulted **informally**.

---

#### 2. Mid‑Size Product Organization

Typical **cross‑functional discovery squad**:

- 1 Product Manager (lead)
- 1 UX Researcher / Designer
- 1 Data Analyst (shared)
- 1 Engineering Lead
- 1 Business/Strategy representative
- Ad‑hoc participation from:
  - Sales,
  - Operations,
  - Risk/Compliance.

They might run a **timeboxed discovery cycle** for a domain (e.g., SME segment).

---

#### 3. Large Enterprise / Bank

Pattern often looks like:

- **Central Strategy / Portfolio team**:
  - Sets themes, coordinates cross‑segment discovery.
- **Domain Product Teams** (e.g., SME Lending, Retail Payments):
  - Run discovery within their domain, informed by central themes.
- **Shared Services**:
  - Research, Analytics, Risk, Compliance act as internal “consultants” to teams.

There may be **formal governance**:

- Portfolio councils or investment boards that **review and approve** top opportunities to move forward.

---

### D. RACI-Style View (Who Does What)

RACI = **Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed**.  
This is a simplified view; adapt for your org.

| Activity                                  | PM | Strategy | UX/Research | Data | Eng Lead | Risk/Compliance | Ops/Support | Sales |
|-------------------------------------------|----|----------|-------------|------|----------|-----------------|-------------|-------|
| Define discovery scope & objectives       | A  | C        | C           | C    | C        | C               | C           | C     |
| Collect internal data & signals           | R  | C        | C           | R    | C        | C               | C           | C     |
| Conduct customer interviews               | R  | C        | R           | C    | C        | C               | C           | C     |
| Synthesize themes & opportunities         | R  | C        | R           | C    | C        | C               | C           | C     |
| Draft opportunity briefs                  | R  | C        | C           | C    | C        | C               | C           | C     |
| Evaluate & prioritize opportunities       | R  | A        | C           | C    | C        | C               | C           | C     |
| Decide what moves to next phase           | C  | A        | C           | C    | C        | C               | C           | C     |
| Communicate outcomes to wider org         | R  | A        | C           | C    | C        | C               | C           | C     |

- **R** – Responsible: does the work.
- **A** – Accountable: final decision or ownership.
- **C** – Consulted: provides input.
- **I** – Informed: kept in the loop (not shown explicitly in this simplified table).

In practice, **PM and Strategy/Portfolio** typically share most of the **A/R roles**, with strong support from UX, Data, and Engineering.
