## 25 – Glossary of Terms (User & Market Research)

This file defines key research terms used in this folder.

---

### A

**Analytics**  
Data about user behavior in products (events, funnels, cohorts).

**Assumption**  
Something considered true without proof; should be stated and later tested.

---

### B

**Bias (Research)**  
Systematic error that skews findings – e.g., leading questions, unrepresentative samples.

**B2B / B2C**  
Business‑to‑Business vs Business‑to‑Consumer – affects who users/customers are and how to research them.

---

### C

**Contextual Inquiry**  
Research method where you observe users in their real environment while they perform tasks.

**Cohort**  
Group of users who share a characteristic (e.g., first used product in the same month).

---

### D

**Diary Study**  
A study where participants log activities or experiences over time.

**Decision-Driven Research**  
Research planned around specific decisions it should inform.

---

### E

**Ethnographic Research**  
In‑depth observational research of users in their natural context, often over extended time.

**Experiment (A/B Test)**  
Controlled comparison of two or more variants to measure impact on metrics.

---

### F

**Funnel**  
A stepwise representation of a journey, showing progression and drop‑offs.

---

### H

**Hypothesis**  
A testable statement about users, behavior, or outcomes used to guide research.

---

### I

**Insight**  
An evidence‑based, meaningful conclusion about users or markets that changes understanding or decisions.

**Interview (Semi-Structured)**  
Conversation guided by an outline but open to follow‑up questions.

---

### J

**Job-To-Be-Done (JTBD)**  
The underlying task or goal a user is trying to accomplish in a particular context.

---

### M

**Moderation**  
The act of leading research sessions (e.g., interviews, usability tests) in a neutral, structured way.

**Mixed Methods**  
Using both qualitative and quantitative research approaches in a study or program.

---

### P

**Persona**  
An evidence‑based archetype representing a user segment’s goals, behaviors, and constraints.

**Primary Research**  
Research where you collect new data directly from users or systems.

---

### Q

**Qualitative Research**  
Research focused on understanding ideas, feelings, and behaviors in depth (e.g., interviews, observation).

**Quantitative Research**  
Research focused on numeric data and statistical patterns (e.g., surveys, analytics).

---

### R

**Recruitment**  
Process of finding and scheduling participants for research.

**Representativeness**  
How well a sample reflects the population of interest.

---

### S

**Sample**  
The group of participants or data points you study.

**Segmentation**  
Dividing users/customers into meaningful groups based on traits or behaviors.

**Service Blueprint**  
A diagram that shows user journeys along with backstage processes and systems.

---

### T

**Triangulation**  
Using multiple methods or data sources to cross‑check and strengthen findings.

---

### U

**Usability Test**  
A method where users attempt tasks with a product or prototype while researchers observe.

**User Research**  
Research focused on understanding users’ behaviors, needs, motivations, and contexts.

---

### V

**Validity**  
The degree to which research actually measures or uncovers what it intends to.
