## 12 – Skills Needed for User & Market Research

This file describes the **capabilities** required to plan, execute, and use research effectively.

We cover:

- A. Core research skill areas
- B. Emphasis by role
- C. Beginner learning path

---

### A. Core Research Skill Areas

#### 1. Question Framing

Abilities:

- turn vague interests into:
  - clear research questions,
  - testable hypotheses.
- keep research:
  - **decision‑driven**, not curiosity‑only.

---

#### 2. Qualitative Research Skills

Abilities:

- plan and run:
  - semi‑structured interviews,
  - contextual inquiries,
  - usability tests.
- avoid leading questions,
- build rapport,
- probe for detail.

---

#### 3. Quantitative Literacy

Abilities:

- understand:
  - basic stats (percentages, distributions, correlations),
  - dashboards and funnels.
- interpret:
  - survey results,
  - analytics.

You don’t need to be a data scientist, but you do need to:

- read charts,
- ask good questions about data.

---

#### 4. Synthesis & Modeling

Abilities:

- turn many data points into:
  - themes,
  - personas,
  - journeys,
  - clear insights.
- distinguish:
  - signal vs noise,
  - anecdote vs pattern.

---

#### 5. Communication & Storytelling

Abilities:

- present findings in:
  - clear,
  - engaging,
  - concise ways.
- use:
  - quotes,
  - visuals,
  - data points,
to support key messages.

Goal:

- help teams **remember and act** on insights.

---

#### 6. Ethics & Empathy

Abilities:

- conduct research:
  - respectfully,
  - with informed consent,
  - protecting privacy.
- understand participants’ context:
  - especially in sensitive domains (e.g., finance, health).

---

### C. Beginner Learning Path

If you’re new to User & Market Research:

#### Step 1 – Learn Basics of Methods & Concepts

- Read:
  - `01-concepts.md`,
  - `05-methods-overview.md`,
  - `06-recruitment-and-sampling.md`.
- Observe:
  - at least 2–3 existing research sessions (interviews/tests).

---

#### Step 2 – Practice Note‑Taking & Observation

- Join sessions as **observer**:
  - take detailed notes who/what/why,
  - record quotes and behaviors,
  - avoid speaking unless asked.

Goal:

- get comfortable seeing real user behavior and capturing it accurately.

---

#### Step 3 – Co‑Moderate a Few Sessions

- Lead small parts of an interview or usability test with:
  - an experienced moderator present.
- Focus on:
  - asking open questions,
  - listening,
  - probing for detail.

---

#### Step 4 – Participate in Synthesis

- Join a synthesis workshop:
  - group notes into themes,
  - help create a journey map or persona.
- Learn:
  - how insights are identified and named.

---

#### Step 5 – Present a Small Insight Summary

- Create a short summary (1–3 slides or a short doc) covering:
  - a few key findings,
  - evidence,
  - implications.
- Present to:
  - your team,
  - or a small stakeholder group.

Goal:

- build confidence in **telling the research story**.
