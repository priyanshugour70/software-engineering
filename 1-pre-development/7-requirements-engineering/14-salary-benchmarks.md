## 14 – Salary Benchmarks & Compensation Considerations

This file gives **conceptual guidance** on how Requirements Engineering responsibilities influence compensation.

It does not list specific numbers (they vary by region, company, and year), but explains:

- compensation components,
- how requirements responsibility maps to seniority,
- what tends to increase earning potential.

---

### 1. Compensation Components (High Level)

Common components:

- **Base salary** – fixed yearly pay.
- **Bonus / Variable pay** – based on:
  - company, team, and individual performance.
- **Long‑term incentives / Equity** – more common in:
  - tech,
  - startups/scaleups,
  - some large public companies.
- **Benefits** – healthcare, pension, allowances, training budgets, etc.

Roles with strong Requirements Engineering responsibility:

- often sit in:
  - mid to senior bands,
  - especially when:
    - they handle complex or regulated environments.

---

### 2. How Requirements Responsibility Relates to Seniority

As you move from:

- junior/associate level → mid level → senior/lead → principal/head,

you typically:

- own:
  - larger and more complex scopes,
  - requirements for more critical systems or journeys,
  - interactions with more senior stakeholders (risk, regulators, executives).

This increased:

- impact,
- accountability,
- and risk handling

often justifies:

- higher compensation bands.

---

### 3. Enterprise/Bank vs Startup/Scaleup Patterns

#### 3.1 Enterprises/Banks

Features:

- structured salary bands and titles,
- stronger linkage to:
  - job grade,
  - years of experience,
  - formal responsibilities (e.g., sign‑off, audit ownership).

Requirements‑heavy roles:

- Business Analysts/Requirements Engineers,
- Product Owners in regulated domains,
- are often:
  - at professional/senior levels,
  - with bonus components tied to:
    - project outcomes,
    - compliance and risk metrics,
    - operational performance.

#### 3.2 Startups/Scaleups

Features:

- more variation between companies,
- titles may not reflect scope consistently,
- equity may be a larger component.

Requirements responsibility:

- is often:
  - bundled within Product Manager/Founder/Lead roles,
  - compensated as part of overall product scope and impact.

---

### 4. Factors That Typically Increase Compensation

Non‑exhaustive list:

- **Complexity of Scope**:
  - number of systems, teams, and stakeholders coordinated through requirements.
- **Regulatory & Risk Impact**:
  - dealing with high‑impact, high‑scrutiny areas.
- **Strategic Importance**:
  - working on core journeys (e.g., lending, payments) vs purely internal tools.
- **Leadership & Influence**:
  - leading cross‑team requirements work,
  - coaching others,
  - driving improvements in processes and quality.

Strong Requirements Engineering proficiency:

- reinforces these factors,
- and can be a strong argument in promotion and compensation discussions.

---

### 5. Using External Benchmarks Responsibly

To get actual figures, people typically use:

- salary reports,
- public compensation databases,
- job descriptions and posted ranges,
- internal HR band information.

When using them:

- consider:
  - geography and cost of living,
  - company size and sector,
  - your specific mix of:
    - product,
    - analysis,
    - technical,
    - and risk skills.
