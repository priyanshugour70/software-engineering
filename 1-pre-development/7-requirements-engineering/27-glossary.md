## 27 – Glossary: Requirements Engineering Terms

This file defines key terms used across the **Requirements Engineering** module in beginner‑friendly language.

Use it whenever you are unsure about a term.

---

### A–C

**Acceptance Criteria**  
Conditions that must be met for a user story or requirement to be considered “done” and acceptable. Often written in Given/When/Then format.

**ALM (Application Lifecycle Management)**  
Tools and processes that manage the lifecycle of software: from requirements through design, development, testing, and operations.

**Auditability**  
The ability to show evidence of what happened, when, and by whom, usually for regulatory or risk purposes.

**BA (Business Analyst)**  
Role that focuses on understanding business needs and expressing them as structured requirements and models.

**Baseline (Requirements)**  
A version of requirements agreed as the reference for a specific release or increment, after which changes follow a defined process.

**Business Rule**  
A specific condition or logic derived from business policy or regulation that determines how the system should behave (e.g., eligibility thresholds).

---

### D–F

**Decision Log**  
Document or table that records important decisions, their rationale, approvers, and impacts.

**Document Metadata**  
Information at the top of a document, such as owner, version, status, and last updated date, helping others understand its context.

**FR (Functional Requirement)**  
Requirement describing what the system should do — its behaviours, flows, and features.

**Feasibility**  
Assessment of whether something can realistically be done given technical, operational, regulatory, and organisational constraints.

**Flow / Journey**  
Sequence of steps a user or system goes through to achieve an outcome (e.g., “apply for a loan”).

---

### G–I

**Given / When / Then**  
Common structure for acceptance criteria and test cases: Given a starting context, When an action occurs, Then an expected result must happen.

**Governance**  
Structures and processes through which decisions are made and overseen, including approvals, reviews, and forums.

**Impact Analysis**  
Process of understanding the consequences of a proposed change: what requirements, systems, tests, or documents will be affected.

**Increment**  
Coherent chunk of work (e.g., release, PI, or milestone) delivering a meaningful step towards broader goals.

---

### J–L

**Journey Map**  
Visual representation of a user’s steps and experiences while interacting with a product or service.

**Legacy System**  
Older system still in use that may be hard to change but must be integrated with.

**Logging**  
Recording system events (e.g., actions, errors, decisions) for debugging, monitoring, and audit purposes.

---

### M–O

**MVP (Minimum Viable Product)**  
Smallest version of a product or feature that delivers real value and allows learning, with minimal but sufficient requirements.

**NFR (Non‑Functional Requirement)**  
Requirement describing how the system should perform or behave in terms of quality attributes (e.g., performance, security, availability).

**Non‑Goal**  
An item explicitly stated as out of scope for a particular effort, to avoid misunderstanding and scope creep.

**Objective**  
Qualitative description of what you are trying to achieve (often paired with metrics).

**Operational Readiness**  
State in which a system has the necessary monitoring, runbooks, alerts, and support processes to be safely run in production.

---

### P–R

**Persona**  
Simplified profile representing a group of users with similar goals and behaviours (e.g., SME owner, relationship manager).

**PRD (Product Requirements Document)**  
Structured document that describes context, goals, personas, functional and non‑functional requirements, dependencies, and risks for a product or feature.

**Requirement**  
Statement of a need or constraint that the system must satisfy.

**Risk (in Requirements)**  
Potential problem or uncertainty that could negatively affect outcomes if not addressed (e.g., performance risk, compliance risk).

**Roadmap**  
Time‑ or horizon‑based view showing when initiatives or epics are expected to be delivered.

---

### S–U

**Scope**  
Definition of what is included (and not included) in a particular project, release, or increment.

**Story (User Story)**  
Small unit of work expressed from a user’s perspective (“As a \<persona>, I want \<goal>, so that \<benefit>.”).

**System Boundary**  
Conceptual line separating what is inside the system (in scope of implementation) from external actors, systems, or environments.

**Test Case**  
Specific set of inputs, conditions, and expected results used to verify a requirement or behaviour.

**Traceability**  
Ability to connect requirements to related objectives, design elements, implementation items (stories/code), and tests, and to follow these links in both directions.

**Usability**  
How easy and efficient it is for intended users to achieve their goals with the system.

---

### V–Z

**Versioning**  
Managing different versions of documents or artifacts over time and labelling them clearly (e.g., v0.9, v1.0 Baseline).

**Workflow (in Requirements Engineering)**  
The sequence of steps followed to go from roadmap items to detailed requirements and backlogs (see `03-workflow.md`).
