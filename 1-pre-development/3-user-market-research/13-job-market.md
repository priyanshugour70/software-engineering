## 13 – Job Market for User & Market Research Skills

This file explains how **User & Market Research skills** show up in real job roles and career paths.

We cover:

- A. Roles that rely heavily on these skills
- B. How job descriptions mention research
- C. Career progression

---

### A. Roles That Rely Heavily on Research Skills

#### 1. UX / Design Researcher

Titles:

- UX Researcher, Design Researcher, User Researcher, Service Designer (research‑heavy).

Responsibilities:

- Plan and conduct user research,
- synthesize findings,
- support product/design decisions.

Research skills are the **core function**.

---

#### 2. Product Manager / Senior Product Manager

Responsibilities:

- Define and prioritize problems and opportunities,
- ensure decisions are based on evidence,
- lead or co‑lead research efforts.

While not always doing all research, PMs must:

- be strong **consumers of research**,
- often act as **co‑researchers**.

---

#### 3. Product Designer / Service Designer

Responsibilities:

- Use research to:
  - inform designs,
  - validate flows,
  - refine UX.
- Sometimes lead or conduct:
  - usability tests,
  - lightweight discovery interviews.

---

#### 4. Strategy & Insights Roles

Titles:

- Customer Insights Manager,
- Market Researcher,
- Strategy & Insights Analyst.

Responsibilities:

- run larger‑scale or recurring:
  - surveys,
  - market studies,
  - segmentation projects.

These roles may lean more on **quantitative and market research**.

---

### B. How Job Descriptions Mention Research

Look for phrases like:

- “Conduct user research to understand customer needs, behaviors, and motivations.”
- “Plan and execute qualitative and quantitative studies.”
- “Define research questions, select methods, and communicate findings.”
- “Partner with product, design, and engineering to turn insights into action.”
- “Collaborate with analytics and data science teams.”

In banking/enterprise contexts:

- “Ensure customer research complies with data privacy and regulatory requirements.”
- “Work closely with operations and risk stakeholders to understand processes and constraints.”

---

### C. Career Progression

#### 1. Research Track

- Junior/Associate Researcher:
  - supports studies,
  - analyzes data,
  - shadows senior researchers.
- Mid‑level Researcher:
  - leads end‑to‑end projects,
  - influences product decisions,
  - mentors juniors.
- Senior/Lead/Principal Researcher:
  - shapes research strategy,
  - manages portfolios,
  - works directly with senior leadership.

---

#### 2. Product & Design Track

- PM or Designer with strong research skills can grow into:
  - Senior PM / Lead Designer,
  - Group PM / Design Manager,
  - Product Lead / Head of Product / Head of UX.

Research capability:

- shows **strategic thinking** and **user‑centricity**, which are valued for leadership roles.

---

#### 3. Strategy & Insights Track

- Customer Insights / Market Research roles can lead to:
  - Strategy Manager,
  - Head of Insights,
  - roles in corporate strategy, innovation labs, or venture building.

---

### D. Demonstrating Research Skills in CVs & Interviews

Instead of just listing “did user research”, emphasize:

- **Context & goal**
  - “Led research to understand why SME onboarding completion was low.”
- **Methods**
  - “Conducted 15 interviews and 3 shadowing sessions; analyzed funnels and ticket data.”
- **Findings**
  - “Identified unclear document requirements and lack of status visibility as main pain points.”
- **Impact**
  - “Findings led to redesign that reduced abandonment by X% and cut status calls by Y%.”

This shows:

- end‑to‑end research capability,
- outcome orientation,
- collaboration with other functions.
