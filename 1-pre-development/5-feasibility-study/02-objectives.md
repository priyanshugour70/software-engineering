## 02 – Objectives of the Feasibility Study

This file explains **what success looks like** for the Feasibility Study phase.

---

### 1. Primary Objective (One Sentence)

The main objective is to produce a **clear, evidence‑based view of whether and how an initiative can be delivered and operated safely within constraints and risk appetite**, so that:

- architecture can proceed with confidence,
- delivery plans are realistic,
- and major risks are visible and actively managed.

---

### 2. Key Outcomes You Should Achieve

#### 2.1 Confirmed Viable Solution Direction(s)

You should clearly answer:

- which **solution options** are:
  - feasible,
  - partially feasible (with conditions),
  - or not feasible,
and why.

Outcome:

- a recommended **solution direction** with:
  - rationale,
  - known limitations,
  - alternative options (if any).

---

#### 2.2 Early Identification of Major Risks & Constraints

You should:

- identify:
  - key technical, operational, security, legal/regulatory, and organizational risks.
- categorize:
  - hard constraints (must not be violated),
  - soft constraints (can be relaxed with approvals/mitigations).

Outcome:

- a **risk register** and **constraint log** that:
  - inform design decisions,
  - guide mitigation work,
  - and support governance reviews.

---

#### 2.3 High-Level Architecture & Operating Model Direction

You should:

- outline:
  - reference architecture or architecture options,
  - integration patterns and key systems involved,
  - high‑level data flows and security boundaries.
- describe:
  - proposed operating model:
    - who does what,
    - support models,
    - key processes.

Outcome:

- enough clarity for:
  - architecture design (e.g., RFCs, HLDs),
  - operational design,
  - infrastructure and platform planning.

---

#### 2.4 Impact on Business Case & Delivery Plan

Feasibility work should:

- refine:
  - build and run cost estimates,
  - delivery risk assessments,
  - potential phasing and scope options.

Outcome:

- updated:
  - **Business Case & ROI** (if needed),
  - **delivery roadmap**,
  - with:
    - more realistic timelines,
    - clearer phase‑gates tied to resolving specific risks.

---

#### 2.5 Alignment with Governance & Risk Frameworks

You should:

- ensure:
  - solution directions and risks are compatible with:
    - architecture standards,
    - security policies,
    - regulatory frameworks,
    - risk appetite.

Outcome:

- smoother approvals in:
  - architecture boards,
  - risk/compliance reviews,
  - investment committees.

---

### 3. Secondary Objectives

#### 3.1 Shared Understanding Across Functions

The feasibility study should:

- create a shared picture among:
  - product,
  - tech,
  - ops,
  - risk,
  - security,
  - legal.

Outcome:

- fewer surprises later,
- faster decisions during design and delivery.

---

#### 3.2 Reusable Knowledge for Future Initiatives

Feasibility work often:

- uncovers:
  - system limitations,
  - regulatory interpretations,
  - best‑practice patterns.

These should be documented so:

- future projects can:
  - build on them,
  - avoid re‑discovering the same constraints.

---

### 4. What “Good” vs “Bad” Feasibility Looks Like

#### 4.1 “Good”

- Clearly articulated:
  - feasible and non‑feasible aspects,
  - open questions with owners.
- Risks and constraints:
  - surfaced early,
  - owned with mitigation plans.
- Architecture and ops direction:
  - specific enough to guide design,
  - high‑level enough to adapt as needed.
- Tightly linked to:
  - problem and business case,
  - governance processes.

#### 4.2 “Bad”

- Vague statements like:
  - “We’ll figure out the tech later.”
- No mention of:
  - security, privacy, or regulatory constraints.
- “Green‑lighting” initiatives without:
  - understanding dependencies,
  - or required platform/infra changes.
- Feasibility assumed rather than:
  - examined and documented.

---

### 5. Checklist: Have We Met the Objectives?

Before closing Feasibility for an initiative, ask:

- **Solution direction**
  - [ ] Do we have a recommended solution option with rationale?
  - [ ] Are alternative options and trade‑offs documented?
- **Risks & constraints**
  - [ ] Are key risks and constraints captured in a register?
  - [ ] Are owners and proposed mitigations assigned?
- **Architecture & operations**
  - [ ] Do we have a high‑level architecture and operating model outline?
  - [ ] Are key systems, data flows, and processes identified?
- **Business case & plan impact**
  - [ ] Have we revisited costs, risks, and phasing based on feasibility findings?
- **Governance alignment**
  - [ ] Have relevant governance bodies (architecture, security, risk, legal) been engaged and their feedback incorporated?

If many boxes are unchecked, continue or deepen Feasibility work before committing to detailed design and full delivery.
