## 13 – Job Market for RFC‑Heavy Roles

This file gives a high‑level view of the **job market** for roles that heavily use RFCs and architectural decision‑making.

---

### 1. Roles Where RFC Skills Are Central

- Senior / Staff / Principal Engineer,
- Solution / Domain Architect,
- Platform or Infrastructure Engineer (senior),
- Technical Lead / Engineering Manager,
- Security Architect (for security‑focused RFCs).

In many product orgs:

- mid‑senior Product Engineers also:
  - participate in RFCs,
  - and are assessed partly on this capability.

---

### 2. Industries with Strong RFC Culture

- **Technology & SaaS**:
  - especially infrastructure, platforms, and large codebases.
- **Financial Services / Banking / Payments**:
  - complex domains,
  - regulatory oversight,
  - long‑lived systems.
- **Large Enterprises & Telcos**:
  - many teams and systems,
  - need structured decision‑making.

RFC‑like practices also appear under other names:

- “Design docs,”
- “Technical decision records,”
- “Proposals,”
- “Architecture review documents.”

---

### 3. Career Signals Employers Look For

- evidence of:
  - leading significant technical decisions,
  - driving consensus across teams,
  - capturing decisions in written form.
- ability to:
  - explain trade‑offs,
  - justify technology or architecture choices,
  - connect them to business outcomes.
- experience:
  - operating in architecture boards or design authorities,
  - working with security and risk stakeholders.

These often show up as:

- interview questions about:
  - “most important technical decision you drove,”
  - “a time you changed course after learning new information.”

---

### 4. Building Experience If You’re New

- volunteer to:
  - co‑author small RFCs or design docs,
  - take notes in architecture discussions,
  - write decision summaries after meetings.
- over time:
  - progress to authoring full RFCs,
  - especially for:
    - service refactors,
    - new integrations,
    - internal platform capabilities.

Capture examples:

- before/after diagrams,
- options considered,
- final decisions and impact.

These become strong portfolio material.

---

### 5. Beginner Checklist

- [ ] Do you know:
  - [ ] which roles in your org are expected to write RFCs?
  - [ ] what “good RFC work” looks like for your level?
- [ ] Can you:
  - [ ] give at least one example where you contributed to or led an RFC?
  - [ ] explain the trade‑offs and decisions from that RFC?

If not, use this module to:

- identify opportunities to be more involved,
- and to practice.
