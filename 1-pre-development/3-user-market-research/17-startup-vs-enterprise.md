## 17 – Startup vs Enterprise in User & Market Research

User & Market Research follows the same foundations, but **how** you do it changes by context.

This file compares:

- A. Research in startups
- B. Research in enterprises/banks
- C. Moving between the two

---

### A. Research in Startups

Characteristics:

- high uncertainty,
- limited resources,
- strong pressure for speed.

Research style:

- often led by:
  - founders,
  - PMs,
  - designers.
- methods:
  - lightweight interviews,
  - quick usability tests,
  - lean surveys,
  - scrappy analytics.

Strengths:

- fast iteration,
- close proximity to users,
- easier to act quickly on findings.

Risks:

- under‑documented insights,
- over‑reliance on small circles (early adopters/friends),
- skipping quantitative validation entirely.

How to strengthen:

- use simple templates and repositories,
- write down:
  - who you talked to,
  - what they said,
  - what changed as a result.

---

### B. Research in Enterprises / Banks

Characteristics:

- multiple layers of stakeholders,
- complex processes and systems,
- governance and compliance requirements.

Research style:

- often a mix of:
  - centralized research teams,
  - embedded researchers/designers,
  - analytics/BI teams.
- methods:
  - larger programs,
  - more stakeholders to bring along,
  - more formal documentation.

Strengths:

- access to:
  - rich historical data,
  - diverse user bases,
  - domain experts.

Risks:

- long lead times for approvals,
- research becoming:
  - slow,
  - over‑general,
  - detached from decisions.

How to strengthen:

- keep studies:
  - scoped and timeboxed,
  - anchored to specific problem statements and decisions.
- use:
  - short, focused cycles,
  - followed by integration into strategy and delivery.

---

### C. Moving Between Contexts

#### From Startup → Enterprise

Bring:

- bias for:
  - speed,
  - direct user contact,
  - iteration.

Adapt:

- learn:
  - compliance and governance processes,
  - data privacy requirements,
  - stakeholder maps.
- accept:
  - need for more sign‑offs and coordination.

Focus:

- using your startup mindset to:
  - simplify research questions,
  - keep studies decision‑oriented,
  - prevent analysis paralysis.

---

#### From Enterprise → Startup

Bring:

- strengths in:
  - structured research,
  - documentation,
  - connecting findings to strategy and roadmaps.

Adapt:

- do:
  - leaner, faster studies,
  - fewer formal deliverables,
  - more direct collaboration with small teams.

Focus:

- adding **just enough structure** to ensure:
  - learnings are captured,
  - decisions traceable,
without slowing innovation.
