## 07 – Data Sources for User & Market Research

This file lists **where to find data** for both qualitative and quantitative sides of research.

We group sources into:

- A. Internal product & customer data
- B. Internal operational & business data
- C. Research repositories & prior studies
- D. External market & competitive data

---

### A. Internal Product & Customer Data

#### 1. Product Analytics

Tools:

- Amplitude, Mixpanel, GA, in‑house platforms.

Data:

- funnels,
- event streams,
- cohorts,
- retention and engagement metrics.

Use:

- identify:
  - high‑value segments,
  - drop‑off points,
  - behavioral patterns.

---

#### 2. CRM & Account Data

Sources:

- Salesforce, Dynamics, internal CRM.

Data:

- customer segments,
- account types,
- revenue bands,
- lifecycle stages.

Use:

- define:
  - **who** to recruit,
  - which segments to focus on.

---

#### 3. Support & Ticketing Systems

Sources:

- Zendesk, ServiceNow, internal tools.

Data:

- issue categories,
- volumes over time,
- free‑text complaints,
- escalation patterns.

Use:

- discover:
  - recurring pain points,
  - language customers use,
  - operational impacts.

---

### B. Internal Operational & Business Data

#### 1. BI Dashboards & Reports

Tools:

- Tableau, Power BI, Looker, Qlik, internal BI.

Data:

- revenue and margin,
- volume trends,
- KPIs and SLAs by segment or journey.

Use:

- add business context:
  - size of segments,
  - financial importance of certain behaviors.

---

#### 2. Process & Journey Documentation

Sources:

- process maps,
- SOPs,
- service blueprints.

Use:

- understand:
  - official flows,
  - backstage processes,
  - where to observe or interview.

---

### C. Research Repositories & Prior Studies

#### 1. Internal Research Repositories

Tools:

- Dovetail, EnjoyHQ, Notion, Confluence spaces.

Data:

- previous:
  - interview notes,
  - usability reports,
  - personas,
  - journey maps.

Use:

- avoid re‑learning known insights,
- build on:
  - existing patterns,
  - established personas.

---

#### 2. External Vendor / Consultant Reports

Sources:

- prior engagements with:
  - consulting firms,
  - UX agencies,
  - market researchers.

Use:

- quickly access:
  - structured insights,
  - benchmark data,
  - user typologies.

Important:

- check relevance and date,
- confirm methods and sample.

---

### D. External Market & Competitive Data

#### 1. Competitor Product Analysis

Sources:

- competitor websites and apps,
- sign‑up flows,
- demos and documentation,
- app store reviews.

Use:

- derive:
  - patterns,
  - table‑stakes functionality,
  - areas of differentiation.

Pair with:

- interviews and surveys about why users choose or leave competitors.

---

#### 2. Industry & Analyst Reports

Sources:

- McKinsey, BCG, Bain,
- Gartner, Forrester,
- local regulators and central banks (for banking context),
- industry associations.

Use:

- understand:
  - macro trends,
  - market sizes,
  - adoption levels.

---

#### 3. Public Data & Social Signals

Sources:

- social media conversations (e.g., LinkedIn, X),
- discussion forums,
- public review sites (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot),
- Q&A sites.

Use:

- observe:
  - how users talk about problems,
  - competitor reputation,
  - emerging needs.

---

### How to Combine Sources

Practical pattern:

1. Start with **internal analytics and tickets**:
   - find where problems manifest at scale.
2. Add **qualitative user research**:
   - interviews, shadowing.
3. Overlay **market and competitive data**:
   - see how others address similar issues.
4. Consult **prior research**:
   - reuse existing findings where relevant.

Always tie data back to:

- research questions and decisions (`04-research-questions-and-hypotheses.md`).
