## 05 – Research Methods for Problem Definition

This file focuses on **how to refine and validate problem statements**, not on broad opportunity discovery (which lives in `1-market-opportunity-discovery`).

We cover:

- A. Principles for research in this phase
- B. Qualitative methods
- C. Quantitative methods
- D. Example research plans

---

### A. Principles (Problem Definition vs Opportunity Discovery)

1. **Narrower focus**  
   - You are not exploring the whole market anymore.
   - You are checking **whether your understanding of this specific problem is accurate and complete**.

2. **Depth over breadth**  
   - Fewer interviews or data cuts, but more focused on:
     - current state,
     - pain severity,
     - constraints.

3. **Tie research directly to the draft problem statement**  
   - Every question you ask should help:
     - confirm or refine **who/what/why/where/when**,
     - clarify **impact** and **metrics**.

4. **Involve both users and internal experts**  
   - Users: how they experience the problem.
   - Internal experts: process realities, technical and regulatory constraints.

---

### B. Qualitative Methods

#### 1. Focused Stakeholder Interviews

Goal:

- Understand **how internal teams see the problem** and where their pain lies.

Participants:

- Business owner, operations, support, risk/compliance, engineering, data.

Questions:

- “Describe the last time this problem caused an issue.”
- “What happens when it goes wrong?”
- “What workarounds do you or the team use?”
- “What constraints must we respect here?”

Tips:

- Bring your **draft problem statement** and ask:
  - “Does this reflect your reality? What’s missing or wrong?”

---

#### 2. Focused Customer / User Interviews

Goal:

- Validate:
  - whether the problem you’ve written truly matches user experience,
  - which aspects are most painful.

Format:

- Short calls (30–45 minutes).

Questions:

- “Tell me about the last time you experienced **[situation]**.”
- “What happened step by step?”
- “What was most frustrating?”
- “How did you try to solve it? What did you do next?”
- “If this were fixed, what would be different for you?”

Tips:

- Avoid presenting a full “scripted” problem statement too early.
- Let them describe their reality first, then compare with your draft:
  - “If I summarize it like this… does it sound right?”

---

#### 3. Shadowing & Process Walkthroughs

Goal:

- See **how the problem shows up in day‑to‑day work** for internal staff.

Activities:

- Sit with:
  - call center agents,
  - operations staff,
  - branch staff,
while they handle the relevant journey (onboarding, payments, etc.).

Observe:

- where they:
  - struggle,
  - re‑enter data,
  - improvise,
  - escalate.

Record:

- notes and anonymized examples.

Use:

- to refine:
  - process maps,
  - scope,
  - constraints.

---

#### 4. Workshops (Problem Framing Sessions)

Goal:

- Bring stakeholders together to:
  - align on **current state**,
  - improve **problem statements and scopes**.

Structure:

1. Present:
   - opportunity context,
   - draft problem,
   - key data.
2. Small‑group activity:
   - use frameworks from `04-frameworks.md` (5 Whys, current/desired).
3. Plenary:
   - converge on 1–2 **sharpened statements and scopes**.

Outcome:

- Shared understanding and updated documents.

---

### C. Quantitative Methods

#### 1. Deep Dive on Relevant Metrics

Goal:

- Quantify **severity and scope** of the problem.

Activities:

- Narrow down metrics to those directly tied to the problem:
  - e.g., for “slow onboarding”:
    - time to complete,
    - abandonment rates by segment,
    - number of back‑and‑forth interactions.
- Segment data:
  - by region, segment, channel, product.

Use:

- In your problem statement:
  - to define **baseline and targets**,
  - to decide **which segments are in scope first**.

---

#### 2. Incident & Ticket Analysis

Goal:

- Understand **operational impact** (frequency, severity, patterns).

Activities:

- Pull:
  - incidents related to the journey,
  - support tickets tagged with relevant issues.
- Analyze:
  - volume over time,
  - correlation with specific steps or channels,
  - typical resolution paths.

Use:

- To:
  - strengthen the “why it matters” section,
  - highlight **internal cost and risk**.

---

#### 3. Simple Surveys (If Needed)

Goal:

- Quickly validate some **assumptions** about:
  - frequency,
  - pain severity,
  - preferences.

Consider:

- Short, targeted surveys to:
  - existing customers,
  - frontline staff.

Examples:

- “Which parts of onboarding are most frustrating?”
- “How often do you need to contact us for this issue?”

Use sparingly:

- Only when you need **quantitative support** and don’t already have it in existing data.

---

### D. Example Research Plans

#### 1. Quick 3–5 Day Plan (Small Adjustment / Low-Risk Area)

- Day 1:
  - 2–3 stakeholder interviews,
  - quick metrics pull.
- Day 2–3:
  - 3–5 brief user calls,
  - confirm main pain points and context.
- Day 4:
  - internal workshop to refine problem statement and scope.
- Day 5:
  - finalize document with updated baselines and targets.

Use when:

- scope is narrow,
- stakes are moderate,
- existing data is good.

---

#### 2. 2–3 Week Plan (Complex, Cross-Functional Problem)

- Week 1:
  - 5–10 stakeholder interviews across:
    - business,
    - ops,
    - risk,
    - engineering.
  - Detailed metrics & incident review.
- Week 2:
  - 8–12 customer/user interviews,
  - shadowing frontline where relevant.
- Week 3:
  - Synthesis workshop,
  - draft and refine problem statements and scopes,
  - finalize baselines and target outcomes.

Use when:

- many systems and teams are involved,
- you’ll be making **significant investments** based on this problem definition.
