## 03 – Workflow: Step‑by‑Step Strategy & Roadmap

This file describes a **practical workflow** for creating and evolving product strategy and roadmaps.

We cover:

- A. High‑level stages
- B. Detailed steps
- C. Example annual/quarterly cadences

---

### A. High‑Level Stages

1. **Intake & Context Gathering**
2. **Vision & Strategy Refinement**
3. **Theme & Goal Definition**
4. **Initiative & Epic Shaping**
5. **Prioritization & Capacity Alignment**
6. **Roadmap Construction**
7. **Alignment, Communication & Review**

This cycle repeats at different levels (company, portfolio, product, team) with different cadences.

---

### B. Detailed Steps

#### Stage 1 – Intake & Context Gathering

**Goal:** Ground strategy work in **current reality** rather than opinions.

Inputs:

- opportunities and problems (phases 1–3),
- business cases and feasibility outputs (phases 4–5),
- performance and risk metrics,
- company/segment strategy and OKRs,
- regulatory and market changes.

Activities:

1. Collect a **snapshot of reality**:
   - where we are winning / losing,
   - key metrics (growth, NPS, risk incidents, ops costs).
2. Understand **constraints**:
   - capacity per team,
   - committed regulatory or platform work,
   - time‑sensitive opportunities.
3. Align on **planning scope and horizon**:
   - which product/segment,
   - what time horizon (e.g., 12–24 months),
   - which decisions must come out of this cycle.

Output:

- a short **strategy & roadmap input brief**.

---

#### Stage 2 – Vision & Strategy Refinement

**Goal:** Ensure there is a **crisp, current vision** and strategy.

Activities:

1. Revisit vision:
   - Does it still hold given:
     - new market realities,
     - tech changes,
     - regulation?
2. Clarify strategy questions:
   - Which segments are most important now?
   - Which problems and value props matter most for the next horizon?
   - How do we want to position vs competitors?
3. Update:
   - vision statement,
   - strategic pillars/themes.

Output:

- updated **vision & strategy** (see `04-vision-and-product-strategy.md`).

---

#### Stage 3 – Theme & Goal Definition

**Goal:** Translate strategy into **themes and outcome goals**.

Activities:

1. Group:
   - problems, opportunities, and initiatives into:
     - a small set of **strategic themes** (usually 3–7).
2. For each theme, define:
   - objectives and 2–5 key results (or equivalent metrics).
3. Map:
   - existing and proposed work to themes.

Output:

- **theme → objectives → metrics** map (`05-strategic-themes-goals-and-okrs.md`).

---

#### Stage 4 – Initiative & Epic Shaping

**Goal:** Turn themes into **workable units** (initiatives/epics) with clear outcomes.

Activities:

1. Identify:
   - major initiatives needed per theme.
2. Break each initiative into epics:
   - each epic linked to:
     - specific problems,
     - segments,
     - and outcomes.
3. For each initiative/epic, capture:
   - value hypothesis,
   - rough scope,
   - dependencies,
   - risk level.

Output:

- a **portfolio of initiatives and epics** ready for prioritization.

---

#### Stage 5 – Prioritization & Capacity Alignment

**Goal:** Decide **what to do first** (and what not to do) based on value, risk, and constraints.

Activities:

1. Estimate:
   - relative value (from business cases),
   - size/effort (with engineering/ops),
   - risk/urgency.
2. Use frameworks (`06-prioritization-frameworks.md`):
   - value vs effort,
   - RICE,
   - WSJF,
   - risk‑adjusted ROI.
3. Align with capacity:
   - per product area or team (see `09-capacity-planning-and-funding-models.md`).
4. Create:
   - ranked backlogs per theme,
   - initial allocation of capacity across themes (e.g., 50% growth, 30% platform, 20% risk/compliance).

Output:

- **prioritized initiative/epic lists**, consistent with real capacity.

---

#### Stage 6 – Roadmap Construction

**Goal:** Turn priorities into **time‑phased plans** at appropriate levels.

Activities:

1. Multi‑horizon roadmap:
   - place top initiatives into:
     - Horizon 1 (committed),
     - Horizon 2 (tentative),
     - Horizon 3 (exploratory).
2. Define:
   - MVP slices,
   - subsequent release phases.
3. Map dependencies and sequencing:
   - technical/platform enablers,
   - cross‑team dependencies,
   - regulatory deadlines.
4. Create:
   - different views:
     - executive,
     - product/portfolio,
     - team‑level (see `07-roadmap-levels-and-views.md`).

Output:

- **roadmap artifacts** for different audiences.

---

#### Stage 7 – Alignment, Communication & Review

**Goal:** Ensure the roadmap is **understood, agreed (enough), and revisitable**.

Activities:

1. Run alignment workshops:
   - with:
     - business leaders,
     - engineering & ops,
     - risk/compliance,
     - other impacted functions.
2. Capture:
   - agreements,
   - disagreements,
   - assumptions and risks.
3. Communicate:
   - final roadmap in appropriate formats,
   - clearly label:
     - commitments vs intents,
     - known risks and open questions.
4. Define:
   - review cadence (e.g., quarterly),
   - how adjustments are proposed and decided.

Output:

- **aligned and living roadmap**, with governance rules.

---

### C. Example Cadences

#### 1. Annual Strategic Planning Cycle

- Q4–Q1:
  - refresh vision, themes, and multi‑year roadmap.
- Outputs feed:
  - budget and capacity planning,
  - regulatory and platform commitments.

#### 2. Quarterly Roadmap & OKR Cycle

- Every quarter:
  - review outcomes vs plan,
  - adjust priorities and near‑term roadmap,
  - set/refresh OKRs.

#### 3. Monthly / Sprint-Level Adjustments

- Adjust epics and stories:
  - within the boundaries of quarterly roadmaps,
  - based on learning and delivery realities.
