## 05 – Research Methods for Market Opportunity Discovery

This file explains **how to collect the signals and evidence** you need in discovery.  
It focuses on **practical methods** that work in enterprises and banks, not academic research.

We’ll cover:

- A. Principles for research in this phase
- B. Qualitative methods
- C. Quantitative methods
- D. Example research plans (lite vs deep)

---

### A. Principles for Research in Discovery

1. **Breadth first, then depth**  
   - Start wide: look across many data sources and conversations.  
   - Then go deeper on **promising themes**.

2. **Mix qualitative and quantitative**  
   - Qualitative = interviews, open‑ended feedback → explains **why**.  
   - Quantitative = metrics, dashboards, reports → shows **how big / how often**.

3. **Use what already exists first**  
   - Your org probably has **data, reports, and insights** that are underused.
   - Don’t start with a long new survey if dashboards already show the pattern.

4. **Timebox**  
   - Avoid endless research. Decide **how many weeks** and **how many interviews** you can realistically do.

5. **Document as you go**  
   - Capture notes in a structured format so they can feed into synthesis later.

---

### B. Qualitative Methods

#### 1. Stakeholder & Frontline Interviews

**Who to talk to**

- Sales and relationship managers
- Customer success and support agents
- Implementation / onboarding teams
- Compliance, risk, operations experts

**Why it helps**

- They see **patterns** in customer complaints and obstacles.
- They often know **which segments** struggle most and why.

**Example questions**

- “What are the top 3 recurring pain points you hear from SMEs?”
- “Which customers seem most at risk of churn, and why?”
- “When a deal is lost, what are the most common reasons?”

**Beginner tips**

- Do **short, focused 30–45 min interviews**.
- Ask for **specific recent examples**, not general opinions.

---

#### 2. Customer Interviews (Exploratory)

**Goal**: understand **jobs‑to‑be‑done**, context, and pain points at a deeper level.

**Who to recruit**

- Existing customers in target segments.
- Churned customers (if possible).
- Prospects who considered but did not adopt your product.

**Interview structure**

1. **Context**
   - “Tell me about your role and responsibilities.”
   - “Walk me through a typical week.”
2. **Specific job / scenario**
   - “Tell me about the last time you applied for a loan / made a big payment / reconciled accounts.”
3. **Process walkthrough**
   - “Step by step, what did you do?”
   - “What was easy? What was hard? Where did you feel frustrated?”
4. **Tools & alternatives**
   - “What tools or services are you using?”
   - “Have you tried other solutions?”
5. **Outcomes & desires**
   - “If you could wave a magic wand, what would be different?”

**Beginner tips**

- Avoid asking “Would you use X feature?” in this phase.
- Focus on **real past behavior**, not hypothetical future behavior.

---

#### 3. Workshop‑Style Discovery Sessions

These are **group sessions** with internal stakeholders and sometimes customers.

**Types of workshops**

- **Signal dump & clustering**
  - Everyone brings signals (stories, tickets, data) and posts them on a wall/board.
  - Group clusters them into themes.
- **Journey mapping**
  - Map end‑to‑end customer journeys for key use cases.
  - Mark pain points, delays, and confusion.

**Benefits**

- Fast way to **share knowledge** across functions.
- Helps build **shared understanding** of the problem space.

---

#### 4. Desk Research & Document Review

**Sources**

- Existing internal research reports
- Previous business cases and strategy decks
- Risk/compliance memos and regulatory updates
- Industry and analyst reports

**Goal**

- Extract **facts and insights** that impact your opportunity landscape:
  - “Regulation X will require banks to Y by 2027.”
  - “Segment Z is expected to grow by N% per year.”

### C. Quantitative Methods

#### 1. Product Analytics

Use tools like **Amplitude, Mixpanel, in‑house BI**, or SQL queries.

**What to look for**

- **Funnel analysis** – where do users drop off in key flows?
- **Feature usage** – which features are heavily used vs underused?
- **Segmentation** – how do different segments behave differently?
- **Cohort analysis** – retention patterns by segment, feature usage, or cohort.

**Example questions**

- “What is the onboarding completion rate for SMEs vs other segments?”
- “Which actions correlate with long‑term retention?”
- “Are there patterns of repeated error states or retries?”

---

#### 2. Business & Financial Data

Work with finance / BI teams to access:

- Revenue by segment / product
- Profitability metrics (e.g., cost‑to‑serve)
- Growth rates and churn
- Risk / loss metrics (e.g., default rates)

**Why it matters**

- Helps estimate **value potential** for opportunities.
- Highlights **underperforming products** that may hide opportunities.

---

#### 3. Surveys (Use Sparingly in Discovery)

Surveys can be useful if:

- You already understand themes qualitatively.
- You need to **size** or **prioritize** among them.

**Good survey uses**

- “Rank these 5 pains by severity.”
- “How often do you experience X?”

**Bad survey uses**

- Asking about **hypothetical feature usage** (“Would you use feature Y?”).

### D. Example Research Plans

#### 1. Lightweight 2‑Week Research Plan (Startup / Small Team)

- **Day 1–2**
  - Review existing product analytics dashboards.
  - Pull key metrics for target segments.
- **Day 3–5**
  - 3–5 stakeholder interviews (sales, support).
  - 3–4 quick customer calls (30 mins each).
- **Day 6–7**
  - Desk research on 2–3 competitors and 1–2 industry reports.
- **Day 8–10**
  - Synthesize signals into themes (see `03-workflow.md`).

Outcome:

- A **basic map of themes and opportunities** informed by both data and stories.

---

#### 2. Deeper 4–6 Week Plan (Enterprise / Bank)

- **Weeks 1–2**
  - Analyze product and BI dashboards for key journeys (onboarding, lending, payments).
  - 5–10 stakeholder interviews across sales, operations, risk, compliance.
  - Desk research on regulatory changes and existing internal analyses.
- **Weeks 3–4**
  - 10–15 customer interviews across priority segments.
  - Begin journey mapping and theme clustering workshops.
- **Weeks 5–6**
  - Optional: targeted survey to validate pain area prevalence.
  - Deep‑dive analytics on a few candidate opportunity areas.

Outcome:

- Rich **qualitative and quantitative evidence** to support opportunity briefs and prioritization.

### E. How to Store and Organize Findings

- Use a **central space** (e.g., Notion, Confluence, shared drive) for:
  - Interview notes (with consistent templates).
  - Analytics screenshots or queries.
  - Summaries of external reports (not just links).
- Tag items by:
  - segment, journey, product area, region, date.

This structure makes it easier to feed into:

- `03-workflow.md` (synthesis)
- `06-data-sources.md` (catalog of sources)
- `07-opportunity-evaluation.md` (scoring and justification)
