## 17 – Startup vs Enterprise: How Discovery Differs

Market opportunity discovery follows the **same principles** in startups and large enterprises/banks, but the **environment and constraints** are very different.

This file explains:

- A. Key differences between startup and enterprise contexts
- B. How process, speed, and risk appetite change
- C. Practical advice if you move from one context to the other

---

### A. Key Differences in Context

#### 1. Governance and Decision-Making

- **Startups**
  - Few decision‑makers; often the founders + small leadership team.
  - Decisions can be **quick and informal**.
  - Less formal documentation; more emphasis on **speed and experiments**.

- **Enterprises / Banks**
  - Multiple stakeholders (product, business, risk, compliance, IT, operations).
  - Formal **approval processes** (committees, boards, funding gates).
  - Decision cycles can be **slower**, with more documentation.

Impact on discovery:

- In startups, discovery can be **lightweight and iterative**.
- In enterprises, discovery needs to produce **artifacts that support governance**.

---

#### 2. Risk Appetite

- **Startups**
  - More willing to **experiment and iterate** quickly.
  - May accept higher product or market risk to find product‑market fit.

- **Enterprises / Banks**
  - Lower tolerance for operational, regulatory, and reputational risk.
  - Need strong controls and sign‑offs even at early stages.

Impact:

- Banks, for example, may:
  - require early involvement from **risk/compliance**,
  - prefer opportunities that align with **established risk frameworks**.

---

#### 3. Resources and Capabilities

- **Startups**
  - Limited people and money.
  - Fewer existing systems and data sources.
  - But may adopt **modern tooling and architectures** from day one.

- **Enterprises**
  - Many systems, data sources, and domain experts.
  - Complex legacy technology.
  - More resources, but also more competition for those resources.

Impact:

- In startups, the challenge is **capacity**.
- In enterprises, the challenge is **coordination and alignment**.

---

### B. Discovery in Startups

Characteristics:

- **Very close to customers**:
  - founders and PMs often talk directly to users and buyers.
- Heavy use of:
  - **qualitative interviews**,
  - **lightweight analytics**,
  - rapid iteration of product.

Typical pattern:

1. Observe a problem or pattern among early users.
2. Form a hypothesis and test quickly (e.g., landing page, prototype).
3. Adjust direction rapidly based on learning.

Artifacts:

- Fewer formal documents, more:
  - short memos,
  - shared notes,
  - experiments logged in tools (e.g., product management boards).

Biggest risk:

- Running in too many directions without **focus**.

Discovery discipline helps startups:

- choose **which opportunities to pursue** with limited runway.

---

### C. Discovery in Enterprises / Banks

Characteristics:

- Must link opportunities to:
  - **strategy themes**,  
  - **risk frameworks**,  
  - **multi‑year roadmaps**.
- Many **specialized stakeholders**:
  - each has partial information and distinct concerns.

Typical pattern:

1. Central strategy or product teams define **themes**.
2. Cross‑functional teams run **discovery cycles** within those themes.
3. Outputs feed into:
   - portfolio boards,
   - investment decisions,
   - multi‑year planning.

Artifacts:

- More formal:
  - opportunity briefs,
  - evaluation matrices,
  - readout decks,
  - traceable decisions (for audit and governance).

Biggest risk:

- Spending months analyzing without **moving opportunities forward** (analysis paralysis).

Discovery discipline helps enterprises:

- create **focused, evidence‑based portfolios**, not just lists of ideas.

---

### D. Practical Advice: Moving Between Contexts

#### From Startup → Enterprise / Bank

What to keep:

- Customer obsession and **bias for action**.
- Comfort with **uncertainty and iteration**.

What to adapt:

- Learn:
  - the governance structure (committees, approvals),
  - the **language of risk and regulation**.
- Expect:
  - more emphasis on **documentation**,  
  - the need to socialize and align more people.

Tips:

- Use your startup mindset to **simplify** complex discussions.
- But respect the **constraints** that exist for good reasons (e.g., compliance).

---

#### From Enterprise / Bank → Startup

What to keep:

- Strategic thinking and **structured analysis**.
- Experience working with complex stakeholders.

What to adapt:

- Accept:
  - fewer formal processes,
  - less defined roles.
- Move faster with:
  - lighter documentation,
  - quicker customer contact,
  - rapid experiments.

Tips:

- Avoid over‑engineering process for a small team.
- Use your structure skills to **bring just enough discipline** without slowing the team.

---

### E. Common Mistakes in Each Context

- **Startups**
  - Ignoring risk and compliance completely when entering regulated spaces.
  - Pivoting too often without deeply understanding any opportunity.

- **Enterprises**
  - Treating discovery as a **one‑time project** instead of an ongoing practice.
  - Allowing governance to become **a barrier to learning**, instead of a support.
