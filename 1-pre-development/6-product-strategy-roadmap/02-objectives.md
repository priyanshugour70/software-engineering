## 02 – Objectives of Product Strategy & Roadmap

This file describes **what success looks like** for this phase.

---

### 1. Primary Objective (One Sentence)

The main objective is to create a **clear, outcome‑driven product strategy and roadmap** that:

- aligns with company and segment strategy,
- sequences work realistically given constraints,
- and provides a **living framework** for making and revisiting prioritization decisions.

---

### 2. Key Outcomes You Should Achieve

#### 2.1 Clear Product Vision & Strategy

You should have:

- a concise **vision statement**,
- a set of **strategic themes/pillars**,
- a description of:
  - target users and markets,
  - differentiators and value proposition,
  - boundaries (what you are **not** doing).

Outcome:

- teams can answer:
  - “What are we building?”
  - “For whom?”
  - “What makes it valuable or different?”

---

#### 2.2 Strategic Themes & Outcome Goals

You should:

- define themes that:
  - connect strategy to roadmap (e.g., “Onboarding Experience”, “Risk & Compliance”, “Platform Resilience”).
- for each theme, define:
  - **objectives and key results (OKRs)**,
  - or equivalent outcome metrics.

Outcome:

- a **bridge** from high‑level strategy to:
  - measurable outcomes,
  - and concrete bodies of work.

---

#### 2.3 Prioritized Portfolio of Initiatives & Epics

You should:

- group problems and opportunities into:
  - initiatives and epics,
  - each linked to:
    - themes,
    - problems,
    - business cases.
- prioritize them using transparent criteria:
  - value, cost, risk, urgency, dependencies, capacity.

Outcome:

- a **ranked list** of major bets and epics,
- with clear rationale for ordering.

---

#### 2.4 Realistic Multi-Horizon Roadmap

You should:

- build:
  - a multi‑horizon roadmap:
    - near term (0–12 months) – more specific and committed,
    - mid term (12–24 months) – directional,
    - long term (24+ months) – themes and visions.
- show:
  - key milestones and release slices (MVP, v2, expansions),
  - dependencies and sequencing.

Outcome:

- stakeholders see:
  - **what to expect and when**, with appropriate precision.

---

#### 2.5 Alignment & Governance Framework

You should:

- align:
  - product,
  - engineering,
  - business,
  - risk/compliance,
  - and other key stakeholders,
around the strategy and roadmap.
- define:
  - how often roadmaps are reviewed,
  - how changes are proposed and approved,
  - how conflicts are resolved.

Outcome:

- strategy and roadmaps are:
  - **used in decision‑making**,
  - updated in an orderly way,
  - not stale documents.

---

### 3. Secondary Objectives

#### 3.1 Create Shared Language & Mental Models

Roadmap and strategy work:

- should produce:
  - consistent terminology for:
    - themes,
    - initiatives,
    - epics,
    - metrics.

Outcome:

- easier cross‑team collaboration,
- fewer misunderstandings in planning.

---

#### 3.2 Incorporate Non-Feature Work

The roadmap should:

- include:
  - enabling platform work,
  - technical debt paydown,
  - risk and compliance work,
  - operational and analytics improvements.

Outcome:

- a **balanced portfolio** instead of a pure feature factory.

---

### 4. What “Good” vs “Bad” Strategy & Roadmap Look Like

#### 4.1 “Good”

- Vision and themes:
  - are clear, memorable, and linked to reality.
- Roadmap:
  - shows outcomes, not just feature lists,
  - reflects capacity and dependencies,
  - updated regularly.
- Stakeholders:
  - can explain trade‑offs,
  - understand why some things are in/out,
  - use roadmap to coordinate work.

#### 4.2 “Bad”

- Roadmap:
  - is a long list of features with dates,
  - not tied to strategy or metrics.
- Overcommitment:
  - everything is “now” or “top priority”.
- Roadmap never changes publicly:
  - despite new information or setbacks.

---

### 5. Checklist: Have We Met the Objectives?

Before you call this phase “done” for a cycle:

- [ ] Do we have a **written vision and strategy**?
- [ ] Are **strategic themes and outcome goals** defined?
- [ ] Is there a **prioritized list of initiatives/epics** with rationale?
- [ ] Does the **roadmap** show realistic time horizons and key milestones?
- [ ] Are **dependencies and enabling work** visible?
- [ ] Have key stakeholders reviewed and acknowledged:
  - [ ] trade‑offs,
  - [ ] what’s not included?
- [ ] Is there a plan for **regular review and adjustment**?

If many boxes are unchecked, continue iterating on strategy and roadmap.
