## 14 – Salary Benchmarks (Conceptual View for Research Roles)

This file gives a **high‑level, non‑numerical** view of how research responsibilities relate to seniority and compensation.  
Exact salaries vary by region, company, and time; always check local data.

We cover:

- A. Why research skills are valued
- B. Relative positioning by role level
- C. Factors affecting compensation

---

### A. Why Research Skills Are Valued

1. **Risk reduction**  
   - Good research reduces the risk of:
     - building the wrong thing,
     - misreading markets,
     - harming user trust.

2. **Better decisions**  
   - Leaders rely on **credible insights** to allocate budgets and prioritize.

3. **Strategic impact**  
   - Research often reveals:
     - new opportunity spaces,
     - critical constraints,
     - overlooked user segments.

---

### B. Relative Positioning by Role Level

Conceptual ladder:

1. **Junior/Associate Researcher / Junior PM**
   - Supports studies,
   - helps with:
     - note‑taking,
     - basic analysis,
     - documentation.

2. **Mid‑Level Researcher / PM / Designer**
   - Leads focused research:
     - from planning to synthesis,
     - for specific features or journeys.

3. **Senior/Lead Researcher / Senior PM / Senior Designer**
   - Owns:
     - major problem spaces,
     - research programs across multiple projects.

4. **Principal Researcher / Head of Research / Group PM / Design Lead**
   - Shapes:
     - research strategy,
     - standards and practices across teams.

5. **Head of Product / VP Product / Head of UX / Chief Experience Officer**
   - Owns:
     - how research informs overall product and customer strategy.

In many orgs:

- stronger research competency **supports progression** into higher‑impact roles.

---

### C. Factors Affecting Compensation

1. **Industry**
   - In **banking and regulated sectors**:
     - research that helps avoid compliance or reputational issues may be particularly valued.

2. **Scope**
   - Researchers and PMs responsible for:
     - multiple products,
     - major segments,
     - or large markets,
   typically sit in higher bands.

3. **Company Stage**
   - Startups:
     - may offer lower salary but meaningful equity and broad responsibilities.
   - Enterprises:
     - more structured bands and benefits, sometimes narrower scope.

4. **Location & Market**
   - Roles in major tech and financial hubs often pay more than those in smaller markets.

---

### D. Using This Information

If you’re building your career:

- emphasize:
  - how your research **changed decisions**,
  - not just what methods you used.
- demonstrate:
  - ownership of significant problems,
  - ability to influence product and strategy.

These are strong arguments for:

- higher levels,
- broader scope,
- better compensation tiers.
