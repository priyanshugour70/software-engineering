## 19 – Real-World Style Examples (Micro-Scenarios)

This file provides **short, focused scenarios** that illustrate specific parts of Market Opportunity Discovery.  
They are simplified but realistic, and are written in a **beginner-friendly way**.

We cover:

- A. Example: Using support tickets to spot an opportunity
- B. Example: Using analytics to size a problem
- C. Example: Using regulation as an opportunity trigger

---

### A. Using Support Tickets to Spot an Opportunity

**Context**

You are a new PM in a bank’s digital team. The head of customer service mentions that **support volumes are high** for the SME online portal.

**What you do**

1. Pull a **3‑month export** of ticket categories for the SME portal.
2. Filter for **“high volume + high frustration”** topics.
3. You notice:
   - Many tickets tagged with “failed payment to supplier”,
   - Common phrases like “payment status unknown”, “not sure if sent”.

**Interpretation**

- You speak to a few agents:
  - They explain that SMEs often send large payments and then **don’t see clear confirmation or status**.
- You run a few **quick customer calls**:
  - SMEs say they are anxious: “Did my supplier receive it? Should I resend? What if it bounced?”

**Opportunity hypothesis**

> “If we provide **clear, real‑time payment status and notifications** for SME payments (especially large ones), SMEs will feel more confident, reduce duplicate payments, and trust our portal more.”

What you did here:

- Took a **support symptom** → turned it into an **opportunity hypothesis**.
- This can now be:
  - written as an opportunity brief,
  - evaluated alongside others.

### B. Using Analytics to Size a Problem

**Context**

Your team suspects that **onboarding is a pain point** for SMEs, but you don’t know how big the issue is.

**What you do**

1. Work with analytics to build a **funnel**:
   - Started onboarding → Uploaded documents → Submitted → Approved → First transaction.
2. Segment by **channel**:
   - branch‑initiated vs online‑initiated.
3. Results:
   - Online onboarding:
     - 1000 start per month,
     - only 350 complete,
     - big drop between “upload documents” and “submit”.
   - Branch onboarding:
     - 800 start,
     - 720 complete (much higher completion rate).

**Interpretation**

- There is a **clear gap**: online is underperforming vs branch.
- This is not just a “feeling”; it is visible in data.

**Opportunity hypothesis**

> “If we simplify and better guide the document upload and submission steps in online onboarding, we can **increase completion** and shift more SMEs from branch to digital.”

You use this funnel as **evidence** in the opportunity brief and evaluation (see `07-opportunity-evaluation.md` for scoring).

### C. Using Regulation as an Opportunity Trigger

**Context**

The regulator in your country introduces new rules that:

- require **more detailed transaction reporting** for SMEs,
- allow the use of **digital channels** for delivering statements and reports.

Many banks treat this as just **compliance work**.

**What you do**

1. Read the summary from your legal/compliance team.
2. Note:
   - SMEs must have access to **more granular transaction information**.
   - Paper delivery is **no longer required**; digital is allowed.
3. You talk to:
   - a few relationship managers,
   - SMEs in your network.

Findings:

- SMEs often **struggle to produce transaction records** for:
  - audits,
  - tax filings,
  - financing applications.

**Opportunity hypothesis**

> “If we build a **self‑service digital reporting center** for SMEs, aligned with the new regulatory requirements, we can both comply efficiently and deliver a highly valuable feature that simplifies audits and financing.”

Here, regulation provided:

- a **minimum requirement** (compliance),
- but you used discovery skills to see an **added‑value opportunity**.

---

### What Beginners Should Notice in These Examples

Across all micro‑scenarios:

- You start from **real signals**:
  - tickets,
  - metrics,
  - regulations.
- You **talk to people**:
  - agents, customers, internal experts.
- You then **frame the opportunity clearly**:
  - who it helps,
  - what problem it solves,
  - why now.

You are not yet:

- designing UI,
- writing technical specs,
- building features.

You are **shaping the opportunity space** so that later phases can design and build **the right things**.
