## 15 – Contexts and Domains for Problem Definition

Problem Definition applies across many industries and domains, but **context changes what you pay attention to**.

This file gives a beginner‑friendly overview of how Problem Definition plays out in:

- A. Banking and financial services (focus domain)
- B. SaaS and software products
- C. Operations‑heavy businesses (e‑commerce, logistics)
- D. Highly regulated / infrastructure domains

---

### A. Banking and Financial Services

Characteristics:

- Strong **regulatory and risk constraints**.
- Complex **legacy systems and processes**.
- High stakes for **trust, security, and reliability**.

Problem Definition focus:

- **Regulatory correctness**:
  - Is the problem framed in a way that respects:
    - KYC/AML rules,
    - capital requirements,
    - data privacy?
- **Risk exposure**:
  - How do current problems lead to:
    - operational risk incidents,
    - compliance breaches,
    - reputational damage?
- **Multi‑channel journeys**:
  - Branch, call center, digital channels, partner channels.

Example:

- Vague:
  - “Make SME lending more digital.”
- Better:
  - “Reduce time to decision for existing SME customers in Country X from 5 days to <2 hours, while maintaining current risk standards and KYC completeness.”

---

### B. SaaS and Software Products

Characteristics:

- Often easier access to **product analytics**.
- Faster iteration cycles.
- Frequently serve **global or multi‑segment** user bases.

Problem Definition focus:

- **Usage and adoption**:
  - Where do users fail to get value?
  - What core jobs are not well supported?
- **Onboarding and activation**:
  - Are users reaching key “aha” moments?
- **Integration and workflow fit**:
  - How does the product fit into existing tools and processes?

Example:

- Problem:
  - “New users of our analytics product do not reach their first successful dashboard within 24 hours, leading to low activation and trial conversions.”

Here, metrics and event data are often central to problem framing.

---

### C. Operations-Heavy Businesses (E‑Commerce, Logistics, Telco)

Characteristics:

- Complex **physical and digital processes** combined.
- Tight **cost and margin pressures**.
- Many **operational KPIs and SLAs**.

Problem Definition focus:

- **Throughput and bottlenecks**:
  - Where do orders/requests get stuck?
- **Error and defect rates**:
  - Returns, failed deliveries, wrong items.
- **Customer communication and visibility**:
  - Tracking, notifications, self‑service.

Example:

- Problem:
  - “Customers placing large orders experience frequent delivery delays in the last‑mile leg due to route planning issues, causing a 25% increase in support calls and 10% higher return rates in Region Y.”

Here, process mapping and operational data are critical.

---

### D. Highly Regulated / Infrastructure Domains

Domains:

- Telecoms,
- Energy and utilities,
- Healthcare,
- Public sector systems.

Characteristics:

- Multiple **layers of regulation and oversight**.
- Long‑lived, intertwined systems.
- High impact of disruptions.

Problem Definition focus:

- **Safety and continuity**:
  - How do problems threaten availability or safety?
- **Compliance and reporting**:
  - Are failures tied to misreporting or non‑compliance?
- **Upgrade and migration constraints**:
  - What technical and operational windows exist for change?

Example:

- Problem:
  - “Network faults in rural regions are detected but not escalated correctly, leading to prolonged outages beyond regulatory SLAs and increased penalties.”

In such contexts, Problem Definition must involve:

- technical,
- operational,
- legal/regulatory expertise.

---

### What Stays the Same Across Domains

Regardless of industry:

- A good problem statement:
  - names **who**, **what**, **why**, **where/when**, and **how we’ll know it’s solved**.
- You still use:
  - frameworks (`04-frameworks.md`),
  - research methods (`05-research-methods.md`),
  - metrics (`09-metrics.md`),
just filled with **domain‑specific content and constraints**.
