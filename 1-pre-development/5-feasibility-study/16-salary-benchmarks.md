## 16 – Salary Benchmarks (Conceptual View for Feasibility/Architecture Roles)

This file provides a **conceptual**, non‑numeric view of how owning feasibility and architecture decisions typically maps to seniority and compensation.

We cover:

- A. Why feasibility/architecture ownership matters
- B. Relative levels
- C. Factors affecting pay

---

### A. Why Feasibility/Architecture Ownership Matters

- Decisions about:
  - system design,
  - technical options,
  - and risk acceptance
have:
  - long‑term impact on:
    - cost,
    - reliability,
    - security,
    - and ability to deliver future initiatives.

Roles trusted with these decisions:

- significantly influence:
  - risk profile,
  - time‑to‑market,
  - and total cost of ownership,
and are therefore:

- typically placed in **senior bands** with:
  - higher base pay,
  - sometimes specific incentives.

---

### B. Relative Levels (Conceptual)

From lower to higher:

1. **Senior Engineer / Developer**
   - contributes to feasibility discussions,
   - estimates complexity and risk.

2. **Tech Lead / Senior PM**
   - owns feasibility framing for:
     - one or more services/products.

3. **Solution Architect / Lead Engineer / Principal Engineer**
   - responsible for:
     - cross‑system solution directions,
     - major feasibility assessments.

4. **Enterprise Architect / Head of Architecture / Chief Architect**
   - shapes:
     - organization‑wide patterns and standards,
     - large program feasibility.

5. **CTO / VP Engineering / CIO**
   - ultimate accountability for:
     - technology strategy and feasibility at portfolio level.

As you move up:

- day‑to‑day modeling may decrease,
- but:
  - ability to interpret and challenge feasibility assessments becomes critical.

---

### C. Factors Affecting Compensation

1. **Criticality of Systems**
   - roles responsible for:
     - core banking,
     - payment rails,
     - risk platforms,
   often sit in higher bands.

2. **Scope & Influence**
   - number of systems/teams/domains,
   - cross‑country or global responsibilities.

3. **Industry**
   - heavily regulated or safety‑critical sectors:
     - place a premium on strong feasibility and architecture governance.

4. **Company Stage**
   - startups may:
     - combine architecture with hands‑on engineering,
     - offer more equity.
   - enterprises:
     - more formal bands and benefits.
