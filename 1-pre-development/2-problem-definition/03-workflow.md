## 03 – Workflow: Step‑by‑Step Problem Definition

This file gives a **practical, repeatable workflow** to go from an opportunity brief to a strong problem definition.

We’ll walk through:

- A. High‑level stages
- B. Detailed steps
- C. Example timelines

---

### A. High‑Level Stages

1. **Intake & Context Review** – understand the opportunity and existing knowledge.
2. **Stakeholder Discovery** – collect perspectives from key internal partners.
3. **Current State & Data Review** – map how things work today and how bad it is.
4. **Problem Framing & Drafting** – write initial problem statements and scopes.
5. **Validation & Refinement** – check with data, users, and stakeholders.
6. **Finalization & Handover** – freeze a version and link it to next phases.

You can loop between 3–5 as you learn more.

---

### B. Detailed Steps

#### Stage 1 – Intake & Context Review

**Goal:** Make sure you understand *why* you are defining this problem now.

Steps:

1. Read the **opportunity brief** from `1-market-opportunity-discovery`.
2. Identify:
   - strategic objectives and OKRs this opportunity connects to,
   - any existing work or prior attempts in this area.
3. Clarify:
   - what decision this problem definition should enable (e.g., “decide whether to invest next quarter”).

Output:

- A short **“problem definition scope note”** (you can adapt from `23-templates.md`).

---

#### Stage 2 – Stakeholder Discovery

**Goal:** Collect **internal perspectives** on the current situation and pain.

Who to talk to (see `11-roles-and-positions.md`):

- Business owner / sponsor,
- Operations / process owners,
- Customer support / frontline,
- Engineering lead / architect,
- Risk / compliance (where relevant),
- Data / analytics.

Activities:

1. Run **1:1 conversations** or a short **kick‑off workshop**:
   - “What do you see as the main problems here?”
   - “What frustrates customers and staff today?”
   - “What constraints should we keep in mind?”
2. Capture:
   - quotes,
   - recurring themes,
   - disagreements.

Output:

- A list of **candidate problem statements, themes, and constraints**.

---

#### Stage 3 – Current State & Data Review

**Goal:** Understand how things work today and quantify pain.

Activities:

1. **Process mapping**
   - Map the current journey or process (e.g., onboarding, loan application).
   - Identify:
     - steps,
     - hand‑offs,
     - tools/systems used.
2. **Data review**
   - Pull relevant metrics (see `06-data-sources.md` and `09-metrics.md`):
     - volumes,
     - times,
     - error rates,
     - abandonment rates,
     - call volumes, etc.
3. **Document review**
   - Look at:
     - SLAs,
     - existing KPIs,
     - audit reports,
     - policy documents.

Output:

- A **“current state” summary**:
  - key steps,
  - baseline metrics,
  - visible pain points and bottlenecks.

---

#### Stage 4 – Problem Framing & Drafting

**Goal:** Turn insights into **1–2 draft problem statements** with scope and outcomes.

Activities:

1. Use frameworks from `04-frameworks.md`:
   - 5 Whys,
   - current vs desired state,
   - problem statement templates.
2. Draft **problem statements** that:
   - name the target segment,
   - describe current pain and context,
   - state impact and why now.
3. Draft **scope**:
   - what parts of the journey, systems, and geographies you will focus on.
4. Propose **initial outcome metrics**:
   - baseline and target ranges.

Output:

- Draft **Problem Statement Document(s)** (see templates in `23-templates.md`).

---

#### Stage 5 – Validation & Refinement

**Goal:** Make sure problem statements are **accurate, important, and shared**.

Activities:

1. **Check against data**
   - Do the statements match what metrics show?
   - Are there any contradictions or missing pieces?
2. **Quick user validation (if possible)**
   - A few customer interviews or calls focused on:
     - “Does this describe your experience?”
     - “What part of this is most painful?”
3. **Cross‑functional review workshop**
   - Present:
     - current state,
     - draft problem statements,
     - proposed outcomes and scope.
   - Ask:
     - “What feels wrong or incomplete?”
     - “What’s missing from your perspective?”

Then:

- Update statements and scope based on feedback.
- Resolve disagreements or document them explicitly.

Output:

- **Refined problem statements** with broad agreement.

---

#### Stage 6 – Finalization & Handover

**Goal:** Freeze a version that downstream teams can work with.

Activities:

1. Finalize the **Problem Statement Document**:
   - version, date, owner.
2. Ensure it includes:
   - problem statement(s),
   - scope,
   - current state and baselines,
   - outcomes and target ranges,
   - constraints and assumptions,
   - stakeholder sign‑offs (if required).
3. Link it to:
   - **User & Market Research** plans,
   - **Business Case & ROI** analysis,
   - **delivery backlogs** (epics, features).

Output:

- A **versioned problem definition package** that is:
  - discoverable,
  - understandable,
  - linked into the wider lifecycle.

---

### C. Example Timelines

#### 1. Lightweight 1–2 Week Problem Definition (Startup / Small Change)

- Days 1–2:
  - Intake & context review,
  - quick stakeholder chats,
  - pull key metrics.
- Days 3–4:
  - current state mapping,
  - draft problem statement & scope.
- Days 5–7:
  - short review session,
  - refine and finalize.

Used when:

- scope is limited,
- fewer stakeholders,
- data is easy to access.

---

#### 2. Deeper 3–4 Week Problem Definition (Enterprise / Bank)

- Week 1:
  - intake,
  - stakeholder interviews,
  - initial data pull.
- Week 2:
  - detailed process mapping,
  - current state baseline metrics compiled.
- Week 3:
  - draft statements and scopes,
  - initial cross‑functional review.
- Week 4:
  - refinement,
  - final approval,
  - handover to research and business case work.

Used when:

- multiple systems and departments involved,
- regulatory or risk constraints are significant.
