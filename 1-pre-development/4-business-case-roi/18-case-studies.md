## 18 – Case Studies: End‑to‑End Business Case & ROI

These constructed case studies show how Business Case & ROI analysis is done from **inputs to decision**.

We cover:

- Case Study 1: Digital SME Lending
- Case Study 2: SME Cash‑Flow Analytics Add‑On

---

### Case Study 1 – Digital SME Lending

#### Context

- Problems:
  - slow, opaque SME loan process,
  - high abandonment, loss to competitors.
- Research:
  - SMEs want:
    - transparency,
    - faster decisions,
    - better digital experience.

#### Step 1 – Intake & Framing

- Strategic objective:
  - “Grow SME lending profitably via digital channels.”
- Initiative frame:
  - build a digital lending journey for existing SME customers in Country X.
- Stakeholders:
  - Head of SME,
  - PM for SME lending,
  - finance partner,
  - risk & compliance.

#### Step 2 – Identify Drivers

Value drivers:

- loan application volume,
- approval & draw‑down conversion,
- average profit per loan,
- processing cost per loan,
- default/fraud loss rate.

Cost drivers:

- engineering and design effort,
- integrations (bureaus, KYC, fraud tools),
- infrastructure and vendor fees,
- training and change management.

#### Step 3 – Estimate Benefits & Costs

Using baselines:

- 10k SME applications/year,
- 40% convert to loans,
- profit per loan = 2k,
- processing cost per loan = 80,
- fraud/credit loss = 1% of exposure.

Assumed changes (base case):

- applications +15%,
- conversion +5 percentage points (40% → 45%),
- processing cost −30%,
- no change in loss rates initially (risk‑neutral).

Base‑case outcomes:

- incremental loans ≈ +1,175/year,
- incremental profit ≈ 2.35M/year,
- cost savings ≈ 0.1–0.2M/year.

Costs:

- build ≈ 2.7M over 12–18 months,
- run ≈ 0.55M/year.

#### Step 4 – Build Financial Model

Horizon:

- 5 years.

Model:

- Year 0–1:
  - mostly costs (build).
- Year 2–5:
  - ramping benefits and steady run costs.

Metrics:

- base‑case payback:
  - ~2.5–3 years after launch,
- NPV (10% discount):
  - positive (e.g., +8–10M),
- IRR:
  - above the bank’s hurdle rate.

#### Step 5 – Risks, Uncertainty & Options

Scenarios:

- conservative:
  - lower adoption and conversion uplift,
  - payback extends to ~4 years,
  - NPV still positive but smaller.
- aggressive:
  - stronger adoption and cross‑sell,
  - NPV significantly higher.

Sensitivities:

- adoption and conversion → largest impact on NPV,
- cost overruns of ±20%:
  - moderate impact but not decisive.

Option comparison:

- Option A: MVP in one country, existing customers only.
- Option B: full multi‑country rollout.

Decision leaning:

- fund Option A now,
- consider Option B post‑MVP performance.

#### Step 6 – Business Case Document & Committee

The team prepares:

- full business case narrative,
- financial model with scenarios,
- recommendation:
  - “Proceed with MVP in Country X with stage‑gated rollout.”

Investment Committee:

- approves with conditions:
  - measurable MVP targets for adoption and completion,
  - follow‑up review before extending to new segments/countries.

### Case Study 2 – SME Cash‑Flow Analytics Add‑On

#### Context

- Opportunity:
  - help SMEs manage cash‑flow, drawing on bank transaction data.
- Problem definition:
  - SMEs struggle to see future cash positions, leading to stress and sub‑optimal decisions.
- Research:
  - SMEs and accountants rely on spreadsheets and accounting tools,
  - want simple scenario views and alerts.

#### Step 1 – Intake & Framing

Strategic link:

- deepen SME engagement,
- increase stickiness,
- enable cross‑sell (e.g., credit lines).

Initiative options:

- Option 1:
  - basic cash‑flow view inside existing digital banking.
- Option 2:
  - deeper integration with accounting platforms + advanced analytics.

#### Step 2 – Identify Drivers

Value drivers:

- increased retention and reduced churn,
- incremental credit utilization (better cash‑flow understanding → more credit use),
- potential fee‑based revenue for premium analytics.

Cost drivers:

- analytics development,
- integration with accounting tools,
- increased compute and storage,
- support and customer education.

#### Step 3 – Estimate Benefits & Costs

Using:

- internal data linking:
  - product usage,
  - retention,
  - credit utilization.

Assumptions:

- feature usage by:
  - 30% of target SME base in 3 years,
  - 1–2% churn reduction among users,
  - modest uplift in credit line utilization.

Translate into:

- incremental retained revenue,
- incremental interest income,
- potential subscription/fee revenue.

Costs:

- Option 1:
  - moderate build cost, limited integration,
  - moderate run cost.
- Option 2:
  - significantly higher build (cross‑platform integration),
  - higher run and vendor fees.

#### Step 4 – Build & Compare Models

For both options, model:

- 5‑year cash flows,
- ROI, NPV, payback.

Findings:

- Option 1:
  - lower NPV but:
    - faster payback,
    - lower risk.
- Option 2:
  - higher potential NPV, but:
    - more sensitive to usage and churn assumptions,
    - higher delivery risk.

Strategy:

- propose phased approach:
  - Option 1 as core,
  - Option 2 integration only if strong usage and retention impact are proven.

#### Step 5 – Decision

Portfolio view:

- other initiatives:
  - regulatory programs,
  - digital onboarding upgrades,
  - platform modernization.

Committee decision:

- approve Option 1 as part of a **SME financial health** theme,
- revisit deeper Option 2 later.
