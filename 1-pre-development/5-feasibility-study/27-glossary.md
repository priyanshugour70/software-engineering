## 27 – Glossary of Terms (Feasibility Study)

This file defines key terms used in the Feasibility Study handbook.

---

### A

**Architecture (High-Level)**  
The overall structure of systems and their interactions (services, components, data flows), without all implementation details.

**Availability**  
The proportion of time a system or service is operational and accessible (e.g., 99.9% uptime).

---

### C

**Constraint**  
A limitation, rule, or boundary condition that the solution must respect (e.g., regulatory requirement, system limit).

**Capacity**  
The amount of work a system or team can handle in a given time period.

---

### D

**Dependency**  
Something an initiative relies on to succeed (e.g., another project, vendor delivery, a platform capability).

**Disaster Recovery (DR)**  
Processes and technologies used to restore systems and data after a major failure or incident.

---

### F

**Feasibility**  
The practicality of implementing and operating a solution successfully within constraints and risk appetite.

**Failover**  
Automatic or manual switching to a backup system or component when the primary fails.

---

### I

**Integration**  
The way systems connect and exchange data or functionality (e.g., APIs, messaging, batch jobs).

**Incident**  
An unplanned interruption or degradation of a service.

---

### L

**Latency**  
The time delay between a request and its response.

**Legacy System**  
An older system or technology that is still in use but may be hard to modify or integrate with.

---

### N

**Non-Functional Requirements (NFRs)**  
Requirements that describe how a system should behave (performance, scalability, availability, security, etc.) rather than what it should do functionally.

---

### O

**Operational Feasibility**  
The practicality of running and supporting the solution in day‑to‑day operations.

**Owner (Risk/Constraint)**  
Person or team responsible for monitoring and addressing a specific risk or constraint.

---

### P

**Performance**  
How quickly and efficiently a system responds and processes work under specific loads.

**Pilot**  
A limited early rollout of a solution (e.g., to one segment or region) used to test feasibility and performance in production‑like conditions.

---

### R

**Resilience**  
The ability of a system to continue operating (perhaps in degraded mode) in the face of failures or adverse events.

**Risk Register**  
Structured list of identified risks with information about likelihood, impact, owners, and mitigation plans.

---

### S

**Scalability**  
The ability of a system or process to handle increased load by adding resources (vertically or horizontally) without unacceptable degradation.

**Service Level Agreement (SLA)**  
Formal or informal agreement about performance/availability targets (e.g., response time, uptime).

---

### T

**Threat Model**  
A structured representation of potential security threats to a system.

**Throughput**  
The amount of work or number of transactions a system can process in a given time (e.g., requests per second).
